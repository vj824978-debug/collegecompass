A → B (first reaction) and C → D (second reaction). Consider the above two first-order reactions. The rate constant for first reaction at 500 K is double of the same at 300 K. At 500 K, 50% of the reaction becomes complete in 2 hour. The activation energy of the second reaction is half of that of first reaction. If the rate constant at 500 K of the second reaction becomes double of the rate constant of first reaction at the same temperature; then rate constant for the second reaction at 300 K is ____ ×10^(-1) hour^(-1) (nearest integer).

Answer

Correct answer: 5

Solution

For A →(k1) B ln(2) = (E_a1/R)[1/300 - 1/500] E_a1 = (ln(2) × R × 1500)/2 E_a2 = E_a1/2 = (ln(2) × R × 1500)/4 (K1)_at500K = ln(2)/2 (K2)_at500K = ln(2) Now for C →(K2) D ln[(K2)_at500K / (K2)_at300K] = (ln(2) × R × 1500)/4 × 1/R × [1/300 - 1/500] (K2)_at300K = ln(2)/sqrt(2) = 0.49 (K2)_at300K = 4.9 × 10^(-1) Ans is 5.
