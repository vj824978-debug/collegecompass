An organic compound 'A' with empirical formula C6H4O gives sooty flame on burning. Its reaction with bromine solution in low polarity solvent results in high yield of B. B is:

  1. (1) A five-membered ring with oxygen (furan) having a CH2CH2Br substituent
  2. (2) A five-membered ring with oxygen (furan) with two bromine atoms and an ethyl group (CH2CH3)
  3. (3) A benzene ring with bromine at one position, OH at another position (ortho to Br)
  4. (4) A phenol (benzene ring with OH) with bromine atom at para position

Answer

Correct answer: Option 4

Solution

The compound A has empirical formula C6H4O and gives a sooty flame on burning, indicating it contains a benzene ring (aromatic compound with unsaturation). The sooty flame suggests the presence of carbon-rich aromatic structure. When A reacts with bromine (Br2) in a low polarity solvent, it gives a high yield of product B. This is a bromination reaction. Since A has formula C6H4O with one oxygen, it is phenol (C6H5OH). Phenol undergoes electrophilic aromatic substitution with bromine. The OH group is an activating group and is ortho/para directing. In low polarity solvents, the reaction favors formation of the more stable product. The para-isomer (4-bromophenol) is formed as the major product due to steric reasons and thermodynamic stability. Therefore, B is 4-bromophenol, which matches option (4): a benzene ring with OH at one position and Br at the para position.
