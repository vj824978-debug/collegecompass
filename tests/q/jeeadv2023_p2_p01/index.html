An electric dipole is formed by two charges +q and −q located in xy-plane at (0, 2) mm and (0, −2) mm, respectively, as shown in the figure. The electric potential at point P (100, 100) mm due to the dipole is V₀. The charges +q and −q are then moved to the points (−1, 2) mm and (1, −2) mm, respectively. What is the value of electric potential at P due to the new dipole? [Figure shows xy-coordinate system with point P at (100,100) mm, initial dipole charges at (0,2) mm and (0,-2) mm, new dipole charges at (-1,2) mm and (1,-2) mm]

Single correct MCQ · Marks: +3 / -1.0

  1. (A) V₀/4
  2. (B) V₀/2
  3. (C) V₀/√2
  4. (D) 3V₀/4

Answer

Correct answer: B

Solution

Sol. B Case (i) V₀ = kP₀ cos 45° / r² [Figure showing coordinate system with origin O, point P at distance r making 45° angle with x-axis, with P₀ vector along the direction] Case (ii) Component of P₀' along x-axis = -P₀/2 î Component of P₀' along y-axis = P₀ ĵ Potential due to P₀ ĵ ⟹ kP₀ cos 45° / r² = V₀ Potential due to -P₀/2 î ⟹ k(P₀/2) cos 135° / r² = -V₀/2 So, net potential due to new dipole (V₀') = V₀ - V₀/2 - V₀/2 [Figure showing coordinate system with origin O, point P at distance r making 45° angle with x-axis, with P₀' vector decomposed into components P₀/2 along negative x-axis and P₀ ĵ along y-axis]
