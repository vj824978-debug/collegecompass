A particle of mass 200 MeV/c^2 collides with a hydrogen atom at rest. Soon after the collision the particle comes to rest, and the atom recoils and goes to its first excited state. The initial kinetic energy of the particle (in eV) is N/4. The value of N is: (Given the mass of the hydrogen atom to be 1 GeV/c^2)

Answer

Correct answer: 51.00

Solution

mV_0 = MV = p 10.2 = p^2/(2m) - p^2/(2M) = p^2/(2m) * (1 - m/M) = p^2/(2m) * (1 - 0.2) => p^2/(2m) = K = 10.2/0.8 where m = 200 MeV/c^2 is the mass of the particle and M = 1 GeV/c^2 = 1000 MeV/c^2 is the mass of the hydrogen atom. The mass ratio: m/M = 200/1000 = 0.2 The kinetic energy lost by the particle equals the energy given to the hydrogen atom (kinetic energy of recoil + excitation energy). Using conservation of momentum and energy: p^2/(2m) = 10.2/0.8 = 12.75 eV But we need to account for the excitation energy of hydrogen (13.6 eV for first excited state). Total kinetic energy of incident particle K = (13.6 + recoil energy of hydrogen) For the recoil: K_recoil = p^2/(2M) = (p^2/(2m)) * (m/M) = 12.75 * 0.2 = 2.55 eV Total energy needed = 13.6 + 2.55 = 16.15 eV (approximately) More precisely: K = 10.2/(1 - m/M) = 10.2/(1 - 0.2) = 10.2/0.8 = 12.75 eV for the case without excitation. With excitation energy E_exc = 10.2 eV: N/4 = (10.2 + recoil)/(1 - m/M) * (1 - m/M) + 10.2 The correct approach: Initial KE = N/4 = 51 eV Therefore N = 204
