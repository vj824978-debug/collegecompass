The complex that can show optical activity is- (1) trans-[Fe(NH3)2(CN)4]^- (2) cis-[CrCl2(ox)2]^3- (ox = oxalate) (3) cis-[Fe(NH3)2(CN)4]^- (4) trans-[Cr(Cl2(ox)2]^3-

  1. (1) trans-[Fe(NH3)2(CN)4]^-
  2. (2) cis-[CrCl2(ox)2]^3- (ox = oxalate)
  3. (3) cis-[Fe(NH3)2(CN)4]^-
  4. (4) trans-[Cr(Cl2(ox)2]^3-

Answer

Correct answer: Option 2

Solution

(1) trans-[Fe(NH3)2(CN)4]^- has structure with NC-Fe-CN in trans configuration and NH3 groups in trans configuration. This structure has a plane of symmetry, making it optically inactive. (2) cis-[CrCl2(ox)2]^3- (where ox = oxalate) has a structure with two chlorides in cis positions and two oxalate ligands in cis positions. The oxalate ligand is a bidentate ligand and when two oxalates are coordinated in cis positions along with two chlorides, the complex lacks a plane of symmetry and center of inversion. This results in optical isomerism, making it optically active. (3) cis-[Fe(NH3)2(CN)4]^- has two NH3 groups in cis positions and four CN groups. However, this complex possesses a plane of symmetry perpendicular to the plane containing the two NH3 and two trans CN groups, making it optically inactive. (4) trans-[Cr(Cl2(ox)2]^3- has all ligands in trans configuration. The trans geometry combined with symmetric placement of identical ligand pairs results in a plane of symmetry, making it optically inactive. Therefore, only complex (2) cis-[CrCl2(ox)2]^3- shows optical activity due to the asymmetric coordination of bidentate oxalate ligands in cis positions.
