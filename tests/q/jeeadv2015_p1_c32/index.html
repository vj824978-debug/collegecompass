In the following reaction, the major product is [structural formula showing: H2C double bond C(CH3) with CH2 attached, then arrow labeled "1 equivalent HBr"]

Multiple correct (MSQ) · Marks: +4 / -2.0

  1. (A) [structural formula showing: H2C double bond C(CH3) with CH(Br)CH3 attached]
  2. (B) [structural formula showing: H3C-C(Br)(CH3) with CH2 double bond CH attached]
  3. (C) [structural formula showing: H2C double bond C(CH3) with CH2CH2Br attached]
  4. (D) [structural formula showing: H3C-C(CH3) double bond CH-CH2Br]

Answer

Correct answer: D

Solution

The mechanism proceeds as follows: 1. Initial attack: The conjugated diene (H₂C=C(CH₃)-CH=CH₂) reacts with H⁺ and Br⁻ 2. Carbocation formation: Protonation occurs at one of the double bonds, generating a secondary allylic carbocation intermediate (H₃C-C⁺(CH₃)-CH=CH₂), stabilized by resonance with the adjacent double bond 3. Resonance stabilization: The positive charge is distributed between two carbons of the allylic system, creating a resonance-stabilized carbocation 4. Nucleophilic attack: Bromide ion (Br⁻) attacks the carbocation at the terminal carbon (1,2-addition) 5. Final product: The reaction yields H₃C-C(CH₃)=CH-CH₂-Br The 1,2-addition product is favored because the nucleophile attacks the more substituted carbon of the resonance-stabilized carbocation intermediate, following Markovnikov's rule and the stability of the carbocation intermediate.
