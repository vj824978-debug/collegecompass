Consider the following reaction: N(CH3)2 + Na+ SO3- (attached to benzene ring) + Na+ Cl- (attached to benzene ring with N+) + OH- → 'X'

  1. (1) as food grade colourant
  2. (2) in laboratory test for phenols
  3. (3) in protein estimation as an alternative to ninhydrin
  4. (4) in acid base titration as an indicator

Answer

Correct answer: Option 4

Solution

The reaction shows the coupling of N,N-dimethylaniline with a diazonium salt (4-diazonium benzenesulfonate sodium salt) in basic conditions (OH-). This is a classic azo dye synthesis reaction. The N,N-dimethylaniline acts as an electron-rich aromatic nucleophile and attacks the electrophilic diazonium compound, forming an azo linkage (-N=N-) between the two benzene rings. The product is: N,N-dimethylaniline coupled with sulfanilic acid diazonium salt, which forms Methyl orange (as shown in the solution diagram). Methyl orange structure: 4-(N,N-dimethylamino)azobenzene-4'-sulfonic acid sodium salt Methyl orange is a well-known indicator used in: (1) Acid-base titration - it changes color from red (pH < 3.1) to yellow (pH > 4.4) (2) Laboratory test for phenols - it reacts with phenols (3) Protein estimation - alternative to ninhydrin (Lowry method uses Methyl orange) (4) Food grade colorant However, the PRIMARY and most characteristic use of Methyl orange is as an indicator in acid-base titration (especially for strong acid-strong base titrations). The answer is [4] - in acid base titration as an indicator
