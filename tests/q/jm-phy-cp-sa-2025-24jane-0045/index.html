In a Young's double slit experiment, three polarizers are kept as shown in the figure. The transmission axes of P1 and P2 are orthogonal to each other. The polarizer P3 covers both the slits with its transmission axis at 45° to those of P1 and P2. An unpolarized light of wavelength λ and intensity I0 is incident on P1 and P2. The intensity at a point after P3 where the path difference between the light waves from s1 and s2 is λ/3, is :

  1. (1) I0/2
  2. (2) I0/4
  3. (3) I0
  4. (4) I0/3

Answer

Correct answer: Option 2

Solution

After passing through P1 and P2 (which have orthogonal transmission axes), unpolarized light of intensity I0 splits equally: - Light from upper slit s1: I0/2 - Light from lower slit s2: I0/2 Both pass through P3 which is at 45° to P1 and P2. After P3: - From s1: (I0/2) × cos^2(45°) = (I0/2) × (1/2) = I0/4 - From s2: (I0/2) × cos^2(45°) = (I0/2) × (1/2) = I0/4 After passing through third polarizer, intensity of both waves must be I0/4 Now, at a point where path difference = λ/3, phase difference: Δφ = 2π(Δx/λ) = 2π(λ/3)/λ = 2π/3 Resultant intensity: I_res = sqrt((I0/4)^2 + (I0/4)^2 + 2(I0/4)(I0/4)cos(2π/3)) = sqrt((I0^2/16) + (I0^2/16) + 2(I0^2/16)(-1/2)) = sqrt(I0^2/16 + I0^2/16 - I0^2/16) = sqrt(I0^2/16) = I0/4
