In the above diagram, the standard electrode potentials are given in volts (over the arrow). The value of E^0_{FeO4^2-/Fe^2+} is

  1. (1) 1.7 V
  2. (2) 1.2 V
  3. (3) 2.1 V
  4. (4) 1.4 V

Answer

Correct answer: Option 1

Solution

We have the following electrode potentials: FeO4^2- --E1^0=2V--> Fe^3+ --E2^0=0.8V--> Fe^2+ --E3^0=-0.5V--> Fe^0 We need to find E4^0 = E^0_{FeO4^2-/Fe^2+} Using the thermodynamic relation: deltaG^0_4 = deltaG^0_1 + deltaG^0_2 where deltaG^0 = -nFE^0 Therefore: -n_4 FE_4^0 = -n_1 FE_1^0 - n_2 FE_2^0 From the reaction sequence: - FeO4^2- to Fe^3+: n_1 = 3 electrons, E_1^0 = 2V - Fe^3+ to Fe^2+: n_2 = 1 electron, E_2^0 = 0.8V - FeO4^2- to Fe^2+ directly: n_4 = 4 electrons, E_4^0 = ? Applying the relation: 4 × E_4^0 = 3 × 2 + 1 × 0.8 4 × E_4^0 = 6 + 0.8 4 × E_4^0 = 6.8 E_4^0 = 6.8/4 E_4^0 = 1.7 V
