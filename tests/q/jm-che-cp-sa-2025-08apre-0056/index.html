Which one of the following reactions will not lead to the desired ether formation in major proportion? (iso-Bu => isobutyl, sec-Bu => sec-butyl, nPr => n-propyl, 'Bu => tert-butyl, Et => ethyl)

  1. (1) iso-BuO'Na + sec-BuBr → sec-Bu-O-iso-Bu
  2. (2) tBuO'Na + EtBr → 'Bu-O-Et
  3. (3) Cyclohexyl-O'Na + CH3Br → Cyclohexyl-O-CH3
  4. (4) Na-O-phenyl + n-PrBr → n-Pr-O-phenyl

Answer

Correct answer: Option 1

Solution

In reaction (1), iso-BuO'Na is a secondary alkoxide. When it reacts with sec-BuBr (secondary alkyl halide), elimination (E2) becomes more favorable than substitution (SN2) because both the nucleophile and substrate are secondary. This leads to alkene formation rather than the desired ether. Reactions (2), (3), and (4) proceed via SN2 mechanism because they involve primary alkyl halides (EtBr, CH3Br, n-PrBr) or primary/stable nucleophiles, giving ethers as major products.
