An alpha-particle of mass m suffers 1-dimensional elastic collision with a nucleus at rest of unknown mass. It is scattered directly backwards losing 64% of its initial kinetic energy. The mass of the nucleus is:

  1. (1) 2 m
  2. (2) 4 m
  3. (3) 1.5 m
  4. (4) 3.5 m

Answer

Correct answer: Option 2

Solution

Let the mass of nucleus be M and initial velocity of alpha particle be v_i = v_0. Initial kinetic energy: k_0 = (1/2)mv_0^2 After collision, alpha particle is scattered backwards and loses 64% of its initial kinetic energy. Final kinetic energy of alpha particle: k_f = 0.36 k_0 Let final velocity of alpha particle be v_f (backwards, so negative direction) and final velocity of nucleus be v. From kinetic energy relation: k_f = 0.36 k_0 (1/2)m(v_f)^2 = 0.36 × (1/2)mv_0^2 (v_f)^2 = 0.36 v_0^2 v_f = -0.6 v_0 (negative as it moves backwards) Conservation of momentum: mv_0 = m(-0.6v_0) + Mv mv_0 + 0.6mv_0 = Mv 1.6mv_0 = Mv For elastic collision, kinetic energy is conserved: (1/2)mv_0^2 = (1/2)m(-0.6v_0)^2 + (1/2)Mv^2 mv_0^2 = 0.36mv_0^2 + Mv^2 0.64mv_0^2 = Mv^2 From momentum: v = (1.6mv_0)/M Substituting in energy equation: 0.64mv_0^2 = M × [(1.6mv_0)/M]^2 0.64mv_0^2 = M × (2.56m^2v_0^2)/M^2 0.64mv_0^2 = (2.56m^2v_0^2)/M 0.64M = 2.56m M = (2.56/0.64)m M = 4m Alternatively, using the given working: sqrt(2k_0 m) = sqrt(2(0.64k_0)M) - sqrt(2(0.36k_0)m) sqrt(m(1 + 6/10)) = (8/10) × sqrt(m) 16 = 8√m M = 4m
