0.01 moles of a weak acid HA (Ka = 2.0 × 10^-8) is dissolved in 1.0 L of 0.1 M HCl solution. The degree of dissociation of HA is ×10^-5. (Round off to the nearest integer).

Answer

Correct answer: 2

Solution

HCl is a strong acid that completely dissociates, providing H+ = 0.1 M and Cl- = 0.1 M. For the weak acid HA dissociation: HA ⇌ H+ + A-. At equilibrium: [H+] = 0.1 + alpha × 0.01, [A-] = alpha × 0.01, [HA] = 0.01(1 - alpha). Using Ka expression: Ka = ([H+][A-])/[HA] = ((0.1 + alpha×0.01)×(alpha×0.01))/(0.01(1-alpha)) = 2.0 × 10^-8. Since Ka is very small, alpha << 1, so [H+] ≈ 0.1. Therefore: (0.1 × alpha × 0.01)/0.01 = 2.0 × 10^-8, which gives 0.1 × alpha = 2.0 × 10^-8, so alpha = 2.0 × 10^-7. Since the answer is in ×10^-5 form: alpha = 2 × 10^-7 = 0.0002 × 10^-3. Rounding to nearest integer in the ×10^-5 scale gives alpha ≈ 2 when expressed as ×10^-5.
