The structure of the major product formed in the following reaction is: A benzene ring with iodine at one position, bromine at another position, and a CH2Cl group at a third position, treated with AgCN to form a major product.

  1. (1) A benzene ring with iodine, bromine at their original positions, and a CN group attached to the carbon of what was the CH2Cl group
  2. (2) A benzene ring with iodine at original position, bromine replaced, and NC group on the side chain
  3. (3) A benzene ring with iodine at original position, bromine at original position, and CN group on the side chain
  4. (4) A benzene ring with CN on the ring, bromine on the ring, and CH2Cl group

Answer

Correct answer: Option 2

Solution

The reaction involves treatment of an aryl chloromethyl compound with AgCN (silver cyanide). AgCN is a source of cyanide ions (CN-) and acts as a nucleophile in organic synthesis. The primary benzyl chloride (CH2Cl attached to benzene ring) undergoes nucleophilic substitution with CN- ions. The reaction mechanism: 1. AgCN dissociates to provide CN- ions 2. The CN- acts as a nucleophile and attacks the electrophilic carbon of the CH2Cl group 3. Cl- is displaced as a leaving group 4. The product formed is benzyl cyanide (CH2CN or equivalently -CH2-NC) The aromatic iodine (I) and bromine (Br) substituents remain unchanged during this reaction as they are on the aromatic ring and not directly involved in the nucleophilic substitution of the benzylic chloride. Therefore, the major product has: - Iodine at its original position on the ring - Bromine at its original position on the ring - A -CH2NC (isocyanide) or -CH2CN (nitrile) group where the CH2Cl was The correct answer is option (2), which shows the benzyl cyanide product with I and Br retained on the aromatic ring.
