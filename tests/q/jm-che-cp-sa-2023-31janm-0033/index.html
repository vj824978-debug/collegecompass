Adding surfactants in non polar solvent, the micelles structure will look like

  1. (1) Non Polar Solvent with polar heads pointing inward and non-polar tails pointing outward
  2. (2) Non Polar Solvent with mixed arrangement of surfactant molecules
  3. (3) Non Polar Solvent with non-polar tails pointing inward and polar heads pointing outward
  4. (4) Non Polar Solvent with densely packed surfactant molecules in organized arrangement

Answer

Correct answer: Option 2

Solution

In a non-polar solvent, the behavior of surfactants is opposite to that in polar solvents (like water). Surfactant structure: Polar head - non-polar tail In non-polar solvents: - The non-polar part (tail) will interact favorably with the non-polar solvent - The polar part (head) will try to minimize contact with the non-polar solvent - Therefore, the polar heads will point INWARD (toward the center) and the non-polar tails will point OUTWARD (toward the solvent) This creates a REVERSE MICELLE structure, where: - Polar heads cluster together in the center - Non-polar tails extend outward into the non-polar solvent Looking at the options: (a) Shows polar heads pointing inward with non-polar tails pointing outward - this is the reverse micelle structure (b) Shows a more dispersed arrangement (c) Shows non-polar tails pointing inward (incorrect - opposite of what happens) (d) Shows a dense organized packing Option (a) correctly represents the reverse micelle formation in non-polar solvents. Matching with the numbered options: (1) c - incorrect structure (2) a - CORRECT (reverse micelle with heads inward) (3) d - incorrect structure (4) b - incorrect structure Therefore, the answer is (2) which corresponds to option (a).
