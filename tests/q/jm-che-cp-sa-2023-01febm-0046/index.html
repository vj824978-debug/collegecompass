In the following reaction, 'A' is a major product from the reaction of an amino alcohol with an ethyl ester in the presence of ethoxy and ethyl groups

  1. (1) NHCOOEt with CH2OH group and a methyl substituent
  2. (2) A cyclic structure with NH and C=O groups forming a 5-membered ring with a methyl substituent
  3. (3) NH2 group with CH2COOEt and a methyl substituent
  4. (4) A cyclic oxazoline structure with N=C-OEt group and a methyl substituent

Answer

Correct answer: Option 2

Solution

The starting material is an amino alcohol with structure: a compound containing NH2 group, CH2OH group, and an isopropyl/dimethyl substituent. When this reacts with a diethyl carbonate (EtO-C(=O)-OEt) under the conditions shown, the reaction proceeds as follows: 1. The amino group (NH2) of the amino alcohol attacks the carbonyl carbon of the diethyl carbonate 2. This forms a carbamate intermediate: NH-COOEt 3. The hydroxyl group (CH2OH) then performs an intramolecular nucleophilic attack on the carbamate carbon 4. This intramolecular cyclization creates a 5-membered oxazolidinone ring (also called an oxazolidine with a carbonyl group at the 2-position) 5. Ethanol is eliminated as a byproduct The major product 'A' is therefore a 5-membered cyclic structure with: - An NH group integrated into the ring - A C=O group (carbonyl) in the ring - A CH2 group connected to the ring - The original methyl substituent preserved on the adjacent carbon This corresponds to structure (2), which shows the oxazolidinone ring system with the methyl substituent.
