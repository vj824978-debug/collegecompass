Consider the following reactions giving major product. Identify the correct reaction. (1) Phenyl-NH-CO-phenyl with HNO3, H2SO4 (1-2) gives Phenyl-NHCO-phenyl with NO2 group (2) Phthalic anhydride with N-K and phenyl gives X, then with Heat and Hydrolysis gives Phenyl-NH2 (3) Phenyl-CH2NH2 with CHCl3/KOH and heat gives Phenyl-CH2-N=C, then with (i) SnHCl (ii) pH adjusted gives Phenyl-CH2NH2 (4) H3C-CH2-C(=O)-NH2 + Br2 + 4KOH(alc) gives CH3-CH2-NH2 + 2KBr + K2CO3 + 2H2O

  1. (1) Reaction (1)
  2. (2) Reaction (2)
  3. (3) Reaction (3)
  4. (4) Reaction (4)

Answer

Correct answer: Option 4

Solution

(1) Reaction (1) is incorrect. The product shown is an N-substituted amide. When HNO3/H2SO4 acts on this compound, nitration occurs on the aromatic ring attached to nitrogen (which is electron-donating due to the amide linkage), not on the other ring as shown. The product would have NO2 on the wrong aromatic ring. (2) Reaction (2) is incorrect. Phthalic anhydride does not undergo Gabriel phthalimide reaction in this manner because aromatic halides do not give Gabriel phthalimide reaction. Aromatic halides are not reactive enough for this nucleophilic substitution. (3) Reaction (3) is incorrect. The intermediate carbene formation (N=C) and subsequent reduction with SnHCl does not regenerate the original CH2NH2 product. The reaction shown is not a valid synthetic route. (4) Reaction (4) is correct. This is the Hoffmann bromamide degradation reaction: - Primary amide (H3C-CH2-C(=O)-NH2) reacts with Br2 in 4KOH(alc) - The N-H of the amide is deprotonated by KOH - Bromine attacks the nitrogen to form N-Br intermediate - Rearrangement occurs with loss of Br- and formation of isocyanate intermediate - Water attacks the isocyanate - CO2 is lost as it forms K2CO3 - Final product is the primary amine (CH3-CH2-NH2) - Byproducts are 2KBr, K2CO3, and 2H2O Therefore, the correct answer is [4].
