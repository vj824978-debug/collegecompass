The reactivity of compound Z with different halogens under appropriate conditions is given below: [FIGURE: Benzene ring with OH group at top and C(CH3)3 group at bottom, with X2 leading to three different products] monohalo substituted derivative when X2 = I2, dihalo substituted derivative when X2 = Br2, tri halo substituted derivative when X2 = Cl2. The observed pattern of electrophilic substitution can be explained by

Multiple correct (MSQ) · Marks: +4 / -1.0

  1. (A) the steric effect of the halogen
  2. (B) the steric effect of the tert-butyl group
  3. (C) the electronic effect of the phenolic group
  4. (D) the electronic effect of the tert-butyl group

Answer

Correct answer: ABC

Solution

Starting material: [benzene ring with OH group at position 1 and CMe₃ group at position 3] Reaction I - I₂, KMn(I): Produces [benzene ring with OH at position 1, I at position 2, and CMe₃ at position 3] - iodination occurs ortho to OH Reaction II - Br₂, KMn(II): Produces [benzene ring with OH at position 1, Br at position 2, CMe₃ at position 3, and Br at position 5] - dibromination occurs at positions ortho and para to OH Reaction III - Cl₂, KMn(III): Produces [benzene ring with OH at position 1, Cl at position 2, CMe₃ at position 3, Cl at position 4, and Cl at position 6] - trichlorination occurs at positions ortho and para to OH The OH group is a strong activating group and ortho/para director in electrophilic aromatic substitution. The extent of substitution increases with reactivity of the halogen (Cl₂ > Br₂ > I₂), resulting in mono-, di-, and tri-halogenation products respectively.
