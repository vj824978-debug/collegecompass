Heating of 2-chloro-1-phenylbutane with EtOK/EtOH gives X as the major product. Reaction of X with Hg(OAc)2/H2O followed by NaBH4 gives Y as the major product. Y is:

  1. (1) Ph-CH(OH)-CH2-CH2-CH3
  2. (2) Ph-CH2-CH2-CH2-CH3
  3. (3) Ph-CH2-CH(OH)-CH2-CH3
  4. (4) Ph-CH2-CH2-CH(OH)-CH3

Answer

Correct answer: Option 1

Solution

Starting material: 2-chloro-1-phenylbutane has structure Ph-CHCl-CH2-CH2-CH3 Step 1: Heating with EtOK/EtOH (base-catalyzed elimination) The chlorine is on a secondary carbon. Elimination occurs to form an alkene (X). Using Hofmann's rule, the base preferentially removes a proton from the less substituted carbon. The major product X is: Ph-CH=CH-CH2-CH3 (1-phenyl-1-butene, formed by removing HCl with the double bond between the benzylic carbon and the adjacent carbon) Step 2: Reaction of X with Hg(OAc)2/H2O followed by NaBH4 This is oxymercuration-demercuration reaction: - Hg(OAc)2/H2O performs anti-Markovnikov's rule addition of water - The OH group adds to the less substituted carbon of the alkene - NaBH4 reduces the C-Hg bond to C-H For the alkene Ph-CH=CH-CH2-CH3: - The OH adds to the less substituted carbon (the internal carbon) - The H adds to the more substituted carbon (benzylic carbon) - This gives: Ph-CH(OH)-CH2-CH2-CH3 Therefore, Y is Ph-CH(OH)-CH2-CH2-CH3, which corresponds to option (1).
