For the Balmer series in the spectrum of H atom, nu_bar = R_H{(1/n_1^2) - (1/n_2^2)}, the correct statements among (I) to (IV) are: (I) As wavelength decreases, the lines in the series converge (II) The integer n_1 is equal to 2 (III) The lines of longest wavelength corresponds to n_2 = 3 (IV) The ionization energy of hydrogen can be calculated from wave number of these lines

  1. (1) (I) (II), (III), (IV)
  2. (2) (I), (III), (IV)
  3. (3) (I), (II), (III)
  4. (4) (I), (II), (IV)

Answer

Correct answer: Option 3

Solution

nu_bar = (1/lambda) = Rz^2((1/n_1^2) - (1/n_2^2)) For H atom z = 1 nu_bar = R((1/n_1^2) - (1/n_2^2)) For Balmer series: n_1 = 2 When n_2 = 3: (1/lambda) = R(1/4 - 1/9) (1/lambda) = R(5/36) lambda_max = 36/(5R) Statement (I): As wavelength decreases, the lines in the series converge - TRUE. As n_2 increases from 3 to infinity, wavelength decreases and lines converge to a limit. Statement (II): The integer n_1 is equal to 2 - TRUE. For Balmer series, n_1 = 2 by definition. Statement (III): The lines of longest wavelength corresponds to n_2 = 3 - TRUE. The longest wavelength (minimum energy transition) occurs for the smallest value of n_2, which is n_2 = 3 (since n_2 must be greater than n_1 = 2). Statement (IV): The ionization energy of hydrogen can be calculated from wave number of these lines - FALSE. The series limit (when n_2 approaches infinity) gives the ionization energy from n = 2 level, not the total ionization energy from ground state. To get total ionization energy, we would need the Lyman series limit. Therefore, statements (I), (II), (III) are correct.
