Benzene diazonium chloride on reaction with aniline in the presence of dilute hydrochloric acid gives:

  1. (1) Biphenyl-4-amine (two benzene rings connected with amino group on one ring)
  2. (2) N-phenyl-N'-phenylhydrazine (two phenyl groups connected via N=N-NH linkage)
  3. (3) Azobenzene with amino group (N=N linkage between two benzene rings with amino group on one ring)
  4. (4) 4-Aminoazobenzene (N=N linkage between two benzene rings with amino group on para position)

Answer

Correct answer: Option 4

Solution

When benzene diazonium chloride reacts with aniline in the presence of dilute hydrochloric acid, the reaction proceeds as follows: Benzene diazonium chloride (C6H5-N≡N+ Cl-) acts as an electrophile. Aniline (C6H5-NH2) in dilute HCl exists as anilinium ion (C6H5-NH3+), but the free base aniline is present in equilibrium. The amino group (-NH2) of aniline is a strong electron-donating group, which activates the benzene ring towards electrophilic aromatic substitution. The diazonium group (-N≡N+) is an excellent leaving group and acts as an electrophile. The reaction occurs through a coupling mechanism where the diazonium cation couples with aniline to form an azo compound. The coupling takes place at the para position (4-position) relative to the amino group of aniline due to the activating effect of the amino group. The product formed is 4-aminoazobenzene (also known as p-aminoazobenzene or 4-phenylazoaniline): C6H5-N=N-C6H4-NH2 (with NH2 in para position) This is a classic example of azo coupling reaction where an activated aromatic compound couples with a diazonium salt. Therefore, the answer is option [4].
