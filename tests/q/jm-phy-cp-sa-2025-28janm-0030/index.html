A hemispherical vessel is completely filled with a liquid of refractive index μ. A small coin is kept at the lowest point (O) of the vessel as shown in figure. The minimum value of the refractive index of the liquid so that a person can see the coin from point E (at the level of the vessel) is ____.

  1. (1) sqrt(3)
  2. (2) 3/2
  3. (3) sqrt(2)
  4. (4) sqrt(3)/2

Answer

Correct answer: Option 3

Solution

For a person at point E (at the level of the vessel) to see the coin at O, light from O must reach E. The critical case occurs when the light ray from O grazes the edge of the hemisphere at point R and reaches E. For total internal reflection at the liquid-air interface: sin(c) = 1/μ where c is the critical angle. From the geometry of the hemispherical vessel, when light travels from O to R (at the rim) and then to E, the angle of incidence at R with respect to the normal is θ. For the limiting case where the person can just see the coin: θ = c = 45° This gives: sin(45°) = 1/μ 1/sqrt(2) = 1/μ μ = sqrt(2) However, for μ to be minimum such that the person at E can see the coin, we need to consider the geometry more carefully. The critical ray from O must hit the curved surface at an angle such that it can reach E. From the geometry of the hemisphere with radius R: - Point O is at the bottom - Point E is at the rim level - The critical ray makes an angle with the vertical For the ray to just graze and reach E: sin(c) = 1/μ From geometric analysis of the hemispherical vessel: The angle comes out to be such that: sin(θ_critical) = sqrt(2)/2 requires μ = sqrt(2) But the actual minimum refractive index considering the full geometry is: μ = sqrt(2) is not the answer given. Recalculating with proper geometry: At the critical angle for the rim point: μ_min = sqrt(2) ≈ 1.414 But examining the options and the solution hint θ = c = 45°: μ = 1/sin(45°) = 1/(1/sqrt(2)) = sqrt(2) Wait, the answer is [3] which corresponds to sqrt(2). This matches our calculation.
