Consider the reaction A ⇌ B at 1000 K. At time t', the temperature of the system was increased to 2000 K and the system was allowed to reach equilibrium. Throughout this experiment the partial pressure of A was maintained at 1 bar. Given below is the plot of the partial pressure of B with time. [Graph showing partial pressure of B (bar) vs time: starts at ~10 bar before t', jumps to ~100 bar at t', then remains constant at ~100 bar after t'] What is the ratio of standard Gibbs energy of the reaction at 1000 K to that at 2000 K?

Numerical (NAT) · Marks: +4 / 0.0

Answer

Correct answer: 0.25

Solution

K₁ at 1000 K = 10/1 = 10 K₂ at 2000 K = 100/1 = 100 ΔG₁° = -RT₁ln10 = -2.303RT₁ ΔG₂° = -2.303RT₂ log100 = -2 × 2.303RT₂ ΔG₁°/ΔG₂° = (1×1000)/(2×2000) = 1/4 = 0.25
