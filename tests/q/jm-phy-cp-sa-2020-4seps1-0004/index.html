A Tennis ball is released from a height h and after freely falling on a wooden floor it rebounds and reaches height h/2. The velocity versus height of the ball during its motion may be represented graphically by: (graph are drawn schematically and on not to scale)

  1. (1) Graph (1): Shows v vs h(v) with curve starting near h/2 on h-axis, curving down through origin, and extending to h
  2. (2) Graph (2): Shows v vs h(v) with curve starting at h on h-axis, curving down to h/2
  3. (3) Graph (3): Shows v vs h(v) with loop-like curve between 0 and h, with h/2 marked
  4. (4) Graph (4): Shows v vs h(v) with straight line from h/2 to h on h-axis

Answer

Correct answer: Option 2

Solution

Velocity at ground (means zero height) is non-zero therefore one is incorrect and velocity versus height is non-linear therefore two is also incorrect. Using energy conservation: v^2 = 2gh Taking derivative: v(dv/dh) = 2g = constant Therefore: dv/dh = constant/v Here we can see slope is very high when velocity is low therefore at Maximum height the slope should be very large which is in option 2 and as velocity increase slope must decrease therefore option 2 is correct. Physical interpretation: - During downward motion: ball falls from height h to ground (h = 0), velocity increases from 0 to sqrt(2gh) - During upward motion after rebound: ball rises from ground (h = 0) to height h/2, velocity decreases from sqrt(2gh) to 0 - The curve shows dv/dh = constant/v, which means steep slope at low velocities (near maximum height) and gentler slope at high velocities (near ground) - Option (2) correctly shows this behavior: steep curve near h (where v is small during upward motion) and flattening as h approaches 0 (where v is large)
