A thin ring of 10 cm radius carries a uniformly distributed charge. The ring rotates at a constant angular speed of 40 π rad s^(-1) about its axis, perpendicular to its plane. If the magnetic field at its centre is 3.8 × 10^(-9) T, then the charge carried by the ring is close to (μ_0 = 4π × 10^(-7) NA^(-2)).

  1. (1) 7 × 10^(-6) C
  2. (2) 4 × 10^(-5) C
  3. (3) 2 × 10^(-6) C
  4. (4) 3 × 10^(-5) C

Answer

Correct answer: Option 4

Solution

Given: R = 10 cm = 10^(-1) m ω = 40 π rad/s B_0 = 3.8 × 10^(-9) T μ_0 = 4π × 10^(-7) NA^(-2) When a charged ring rotates, it constitutes a circular current. The current is given by: I = ΔQ/Δt = Q/(2π/ω) = Qω/(2π) The magnetic field at the centre of a current-carrying ring is: B_0 = μ_0 I/(2R) Substituting the expression for I: B_0 = μ_0/(2R) × [Q × ω/(2π)] B_0 = μ_0 Q ω/(4πR) Solving for Q: Q = (B_0 × 4πR)/(μ_0 × ω) Substituting values: Q = (3.8 × 10^(-9) × 4π × 10^(-1))/(4π × 10^(-7) × 40π) Q = (3.8 × 10^(-9) × 4π × 10^(-1))/(4π × 10^(-7) × 40π) Q = (3.8 × 10^(-9) × 4 × 10^(-1))/(4 × 10^(-7) × 40π) Q = (380 × 10^(-2) × 10^(-9) × 10^(-1))/(40 × 3.14 × 10^(-7)) Q = (380 × 10^(-12) × 10^(-1))/(125.6 × 10^(-7)) Q = (380 × 10^(-13))/(125.6 × 10^(-7)) Q = (380/125.6) × 10^(-13+7) Q = 3.02 × 10^(-6) C Q ≈ 3 × 10^(-5) C
