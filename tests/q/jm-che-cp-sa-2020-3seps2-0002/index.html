100 mL of 0.1 M HCl is taken in a beaker and to it 100 mL of 0.1 M NaOH is added in steps 2 mL and the pH is continuously measured. Which of the following graphs correctly depicts the change in pH?

  1. (1) Graph showing pH starting at 7, then decreasing with vol. of NaOH
  2. (2) Graph showing pH starting low, then increasing steeply around equivalence point with vol. of NaOH
  3. (3) Graph showing pH increasing linearly with vol. of NaOH, with horizontal line at pH 7
  4. (4) Graph showing pH starting at 7, then increasing steeply with vol. of NaOH

Answer

Correct answer: Option 2

Solution

For titration of strong acid (HCl) with strong base (NaOH): Initial conditions: - 100 mL of 0.1 M HCl contains 0.1 × 100/1000 = 0.01 moles of HCl - pH initially = -log(0.1) = 1 At equivalence point: - Volume of NaOH needed = 100 mL (since molarities and volumes are equal) - At equivalence point, pH = 7 Characteristics of strong acid-strong base titration: 1. Initial pH is very low (around 1) due to strong acid 2. pH rises slowly in the beginning as NaOH neutralizes HCl 3. Near the equivalence point (around 100 mL NaOH added), there is a steep, vertical rise in pH 4. After equivalence point, pH levels off at high values (excess NaOH) The correct graph should show: - Starting at low pH (around 1) - Gradual increase initially - A steep vertical rise around 100 mL of NaOH (equivalence point) - Leveling off at high pH afterward Graph (2) shows exactly this behavior with the characteristic steep rise at the equivalence point.
