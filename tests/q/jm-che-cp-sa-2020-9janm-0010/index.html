The major product (Y) in the following reactions is: CH3-CH(CH3)-C≡CH with (i) C2H5MgBr, H2O (ii) Conc. H2SO4/Δ

  1. (1) CH3-C(CH3)=C(CH3)-C2H5
  2. (2) H3C-C(CH2)=CH-CH(C2H5)
  3. (3) CH3-CH(CH3)-C=CH2 with C2H5 substituent
  4. (4) CH3-CH(CH3)-C=CH-CH3 with CH3 substituent

Answer

Correct answer: Option 1

Solution

Step 1: The starting material is CH3-CH(CH3)-C≡CH (an internal alkyne with a methyl branch) Step 2: First reaction with C2H5MgBr (Grignard reagent) and H2O - The Grignard reagent attacks the terminal alkyne hydrogen - This forms a C-C bond between the alkyne and the ethyl group - After hydrolysis with H2O, we get X: CH3-CH(CH3)-C(O)=CH-CH2-CH3 (actually this gives a ketone intermediate) - Actually, the Grignard adds to form: CH3-CH(CH3)-C(=OMgBr)-CH2CH3, and upon hydrolysis gives the ketone X Step 3: Second reaction of X with Conc. H2SO4/Δ (dehydration) - The ketone undergoes dehydration (loss of H2O) - This leads to formation of an alkene (carbocation rearrangement may occur) - The OH group from intermediate and H are removed to form C=C - Following Zaitsev's rule and carbocation stability, the major product forms Step 4: The mechanism shows formation of: CH3-CH(CH3)-C(OH)(CH2CH3)-CH3 intermediate (from Grignard addition to alkyne followed by carbonyl formation) Step 5: Upon dehydration with H2SO4 and heat: - Loss of water occurs - Double bond forms between the carbon and the adjacent carbon - Major product is CH3-C(CH3)=C(CH3)-C2H5 (option 1) This follows the rule that the most substituted alkene (Zaitsev's rule) is the major product.
