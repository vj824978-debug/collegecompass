What will be the major product when m-cresol is reacted with propargyl bromide (HC≡C-CH2Br) in presence of K2CO3 in acetone?

  1. (1) A compound with OH group on benzene ring, CH3 group, and a terminal alkene (HC=C-)
  2. (2) A compound with OH group on benzene ring, CH3 group on the ring, and CH2 connected to an alkyne
  3. (3) A compound with OH group on benzene ring, CH3 group, and an internal alkyne with CH2 linker
  4. (4) A compound with an ether linkage to a benzene ring with CH3 group, and a terminal alkyne

Answer

Correct answer: Option 4

Solution

When m-cresol is reacted with propargyl bromide (HC≡C-CH2Br) in the presence of K2CO3 in acetone: 1. K2CO3 acts as a base and deprotonates the hydroxyl group (-OH) of m-cresol to form the phenoxide ion (C6H4-O^(-) with CH3 substituent) 2. The phenoxide ion is a strong nucleophile and attacks the primary alkyl halide (propargyl bromide) at the CH2 carbon (SN2 mechanism) 3. This results in the formation of an ether linkage between the oxygen and the CH2 group, leaving the terminal alkyne intact 4. The product is m-cresyl propargyl ether with structure: benzene ring with CH3 and O-CH2-C≡CH substituents 5. The major product is the allyl ether derivative with the alkyne group still present as a terminal alkyne Answer: Option [4] - The compound with an ether linkage connecting the m-cresol oxygen to the CH2 of propargyl, with the terminal alkyne (HC≡C) remaining intact
