The correct order of reactivity of CH3Br in methanol with the following nucleophiles is F^-, I^-, C2H5O^- and C6H5O^-

  1. (1) I^- > C6H5O^- > F^- > C2H5O^-
  2. (2) I^- > C2H5O^- > C6H5O^- > F^-
  3. (3) I^- > C2H5O^- > F^- > C6H5O^-
  4. (4) I^- > F^- > C6H5O^- > C2H5O^-

Answer

Correct answer: Option 2

Solution

Order of nucleophilicity: I^- > C2H5O^- > PhO^- > F^- Explanation: 1. The nucleophilicity order depends on multiple factors including basicity, size, polarizability, and solvation effects in protic solvents like methanol. 2. I^- is the most nucleophilic because: - Although it is the weakest base (least basic) - It is large and highly polarizable - In protic solvents like methanol, polarizability dominates over basicity - The large size allows better orbital overlap 3. C2H5O^- (ethoxide) is more nucleophilic than F^- because: - Both are strong bases - However, C2H5O^- is better solvated in methanol (alcohol solvent) - The negative charge is more diffuse on oxygen in C2H5O^- - F^- is small and highly solvated by methanol through hydrogen bonding, making it less available for nucleophilic attack 4. C6H5O^- (phenoxide) is less nucleophilic than C2H5O^- because: - The negative charge is delocalized into the aromatic ring through resonance - This reduces the electron density at the oxygen atom - Less available for nucleophilic attack 5. F^- is the least nucleophilic in methanol because: - Although it is a strong base - It is very small and heavily solvated by methanol through hydrogen bonding - Strong solvation decreases its nucleophilicity - The solvation shell makes it unavailable for attack Therefore, the correct order is: I^- > C2H5O^- > C6H5O^- > F^-
