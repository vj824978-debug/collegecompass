Dihydrogen of high purity (> 99.95%) is obtained through: (1) the electrolysis of warm Ba(OH)2 solution using Ni electrodes. (2) the reaction of Zn with dilute HCl (3) the electrolysis of brine solution. (4) the electrolysis of acidified water using Pt electrodes.

  1. (1) the electrolysis of warm Ba(OH)2 solution using Ni electrodes.
  2. (2) the reaction of Zn with dilute HCl
  3. (3) the electrolysis of brine solution.
  4. (4) the electrolysis of acidified water using Pt electrodes.

Answer

Correct answer: Option 1

Solution

High purity (>99.95%) dihydrogen is obtained by electrolysing warm aqueous barium hydroxide solution between nickel electrodes. Analysis of each option: (1) Electrolysis of warm Ba(OH)2 solution using Ni electrodes: This is the correct method. The Ni electrodes are inert and do not react. Ba(OH)2 provides the necessary ions for conduction. At the cathode, water is reduced to produce H2 gas of high purity. At the anode, OH- ions are oxidized to produce O2. Since no other species can be easily oxidized or reduced, the products are very pure. (2) Reaction of Zn with dilute HCl: This produces H2 gas but it is contaminated with water vapor and traces of HCl vapors. The purity is not >99.95%. (3) Electrolysis of brine solution: This produces H2 at the cathode, but Cl2 is also produced at the anode. The H2 gas gets contaminated with Cl2 and water vapor, reducing purity. (4) Electrolysis of acidified water using Pt electrodes: While Pt electrodes are inert, acidified water electrolysis produces both H2 and O2. The H2 collected can be contaminated with O2 and water vapor, especially if not carefully separated. Therefore, the answer is (1).
