Let f : [0, 1] → R be a twice differentiable function in (0, 1) such that f(0) = 3 and f(1) = 5. If the line y = 2x + 3 intersects the graph of f at only two distinct points in (0, 1) then the least number of points x ∈ (0, 1) at which f''(x) = 0, is _____.

Answer

Correct answer: 2

Solution

Let g(x) = f(x) - (2x + 3). Since the line y = 2x + 3 intersects the graph of f at only two distinct points in (0, 1), g(x) = 0 at exactly two points in (0, 1). Also g(0) = f(0) - 3 = 0 and g(1) = f(1) - 5 = 0. So g(x) has at least 3 zeros in [0, 1]: at x = 0, two interior points, and x = 1. By Rolle's theorem, g'(x) = 0 at least at 2 points in (0, 1). Since g'(x) = f'(x) - 2, we have f'(x) = 2 at least 2 points. By Rolle's theorem applied to g'(x), g''(x) = 0 at least once in (0, 1). Since g''(x) = f''(x), we have f''(x) = 0 at least once. To have the minimum, we need exactly 2 zeros of f''(x).
