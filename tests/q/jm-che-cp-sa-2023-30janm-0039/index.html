The major products 'A' and 'B', respectively, are H3C-C(=CH2)-CH3 with Cold H2SO4 giving product A, and H3C-C(=CH2)-CH3 with H2SO4 at 80°C giving product B

  1. (1) H3C-C(CH3)2 & CH3-CH(CH2CH2)-HC(CH3) with OSO3H
  2. (2) CH3-CH(CH2CH2)-CH(CH3) & H3C-C(CH3)2 with OSO3H
  3. (3) CH3-C(CH3)(CH2CH3) & CH3-C(CH3)2 with OSO3H
  4. (4) H3C-C(CH3)2 & CH3-C(CH3)(CH(CH3)2)-CH3 with CH3

Answer

Correct answer: Option 4

Solution

The reaction involves the addition of H2SO4 to the alkene H3C-C(=CH2)-CH3 (2-methylpropene) under different conditions: At cold temperature with H2SO4: - The reaction proceeds via carbocation intermediate formation - H+ adds to the double bond following Markovnikov's rule - The tertiary carbocation (H3C-C(+)(CH3)2) forms preferentially - Water attack or HSO4- attack is slow at low temperature - The major product A is the tertiary alkyl bisulfate: H3C-C(CH3)2 with OSO3H At 80°C with H2SO4: - The tertiary carbocation (H3C-C(+)(CH3)2) forms via Markovnikov's addition - At elevated temperature, the carbocation can undergo rearrangement or further reaction - The carbocation can react with another molecule of 2-methylpropene - This leads to dimerization forming CH3-C(CH3)(CH(CH3)2)-CH3 - The major product B is: CH3-C(CH3)(CH(CH3)2)-CH3 with additional methyl groups Therefore, product A = H3C-C(CH3)2 with OSO3H and product B = CH3-C(CH3)(CH(CH3)2)-CH3 with CH3 groups The answer is option (4).
