Which one of the following about an electron occupying the 1s orbital in a hydrogen atom is incorrect ? (The Bohr radius is represented by a_0).

  1. (1) The magnitude of the potential energy is double that of its kinetic energy on an average.
  2. (2) The probability density of finding the electron is maximum at the nucleus.
  3. (3) The total energy of the electron is maximum when it is at a distance a_0 from the nucleus.
  4. (4) The electron can be found at a distance 2a_0 from the nucleus.

Answer

Correct answer: Option 3

Solution

(1) T.E. = -K.E. = PE/2 This is correct according to the virial theorem for hydrogen atom. The magnitude of potential energy is indeed double that of kinetic energy. (2) The probability density is given by |psi|^2. For 1s orbital, |psi|^2 is maximum at the nucleus (r = 0). This is correct. (4) The electron wavefunction extends to infinity, so the electron can be found at any distance from the nucleus, including 2a_0. The probability decreases with distance but is non-zero. This is correct. (3) The total energy of the electron in the 1s orbital is a constant value = -13.6 eV. It does NOT depend on the distance from the nucleus. The total energy is NOT maximum at any particular distance - it remains constant throughout the orbital. This statement is incorrect because: - Total energy is constant for a given orbital - It is not maximum at distance a_0 - Energy only changes when the electron transitions between different orbitals
