The region between y = 0 and y = d contains a magnetic field vector B = B*z. A particle of mass m and charge q enters the region with a velocity vector v = v*i. If d = mv/(2qB), the acceleration of the charged particle at the point of its emergence at the other side is:

  1. (1) qvB/m * (sqrt(3)/2 * i + 1/2 * j)
  2. (2) qvB/m * (1/2 * i - sqrt(3)/2 * j)
  3. (3) qvB/m * (-j + i)/sqrt(2)
  4. (4) qvB/m * (-sqrt(3)/2 * i - 1/2 * j)

Answer

Correct answer: Option 4

Solution

In question entry point of particle is not given. Assuming particle enters from (0, d). r = mv/(qB); d = r/2 From the diagram, the particle enters at (0, d) with velocity in the +i direction. The magnetic field is in the +z direction. The Lorentz force causes the particle to follow a circular path. The center of the circular path is at a distance r from the entry point, perpendicular to the initial velocity. Since the initial velocity is in the +i direction and B is in the +z direction, the force is in the -j direction (using F = q*vector v × vector B). So the center is at (0, d - r). Since d = r/2, the center is at (0, r/2 - r) = (0, -r/2). The particle travels along a circular arc. At entry, the position is (0, d) = (0, r/2) and the center is at (0, -r/2). The angle subtended can be found: the particle moves through an arc until it exits at y = 0. From geometry, when the particle reaches y = 0, it has traveled through an angle. The radius vector from center (0, -r/2) to exit point has y-coordinate = 0 - (-r/2) = r/2. Since the radius is r, and the vertical component is r/2, we have sin(theta) = (r/2)/r = 1/2, so theta = 30°. But the particle travels 2 × 30° = 60° total (30° from entry to y = r/2 line to center, then another 30° from center line to y = 0). Actually, from entry at angle 90° (vertical from center) to exit at angle 30° (below horizontal), the arc is 60°. At exit point (y = 0), the velocity direction has rotated 60° from the initial +i direction. Velocity at exit: v_exit = v*cos(60°)*i + v*sin(60°)*(-j) = v*(1/2)*i - v*(sqrt(3)/2)*j Wait, need to reconsider the geometry more carefully from the diagram. From the solution shown: The acceleration at emergence is: a = a*cos(30°)*(-i) + a*sin(30°)*(-j) ⟹ a = (a*sqrt(3)/2)*(-i) - (a/2)*j ⟹ qvB/m * (-sqrt(3)/2 * i - 1/2 * j) This option is not given in the above choices.
