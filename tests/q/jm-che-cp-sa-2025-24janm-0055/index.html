Which of the following linear combination of atomic orbitals will lead to formation of molecular orbitals in homonuclear diatomic molecules [internuclear axis in z-direction]?

  1. (1) 2p_z and 2p_x
  2. (2) 2s and 2p_y
  3. (3) 3d_xy and 3d_{x^2-y^2}
  4. (4) 2s and 2p_z

Answer

Correct answer: Option 3

Solution

To form molecular orbitals from linear combination of atomic orbitals (LCAO), the atomic orbitals must have proper symmetry and overlap along the internuclear axis (z-direction). A: 2p_z and 2p_x The p_z orbital is aligned along the internuclear axis (z-direction) with proper overlap. The p_x orbital is perpendicular to the internuclear axis. These have different symmetries and nodal planes. The combination does not lead to molecular orbital formation. B: 2s and 2p_y The 2s orbital has spherical symmetry but the 2p_y orbital is perpendicular to the internuclear axis. They do not have compatible geometry for overlap along the z-direction. No molecular orbital forms. C: 3d_xy and 3d_{x^2-y^2} The d_xy orbital has lobes in the xy-plane (perpendicular to internuclear axis). The d_{x^2-y^2} orbital also has lobes in the xy-plane. Neither orbital overlaps along the z-direction. No molecular orbital forms. D: 2s and 2p_z The 2s orbital has spherical symmetry (symmetric about z-axis). The 2p_z orbital is aligned along the z-axis. When combined, they can produce sigma (σ) molecular orbital with proper overlap along the internuclear axis. Molecular orbital forms. E: 2p_z and 3d_{x^2-y^2} The 2p_z orbital overlaps along z-direction. The 3d_{x^2-y^2} orbital has lobes in the xy-plane (perpendicular to z-direction). These have incompatible symmetries. No molecular orbital forms. Therefore, only option D (2s and 2p_z) leads to formation of a molecular orbital (sigma molecular orbital).
