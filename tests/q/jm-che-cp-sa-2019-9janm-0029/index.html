The major product of the following reaction is: Starting material is a benzene ring with a side chain CH2CH3 and a carbonyl group with NH2 attached (carboxamide). The reaction conditions are (i) Br2/hv (ii) KOH (dil)

  1. (1) A benzene ring fused with a 6-membered ring containing O and NH, with CH3 as substituent
  2. (2) A benzene ring fused with a 5-membered ring containing NH, with CH2 group
  3. (3) A benzene ring fused with a 4-membered ring containing NH, with CH3 as substituent
  4. (4) A benzene ring fused with a 6-membered ring containing NH and C=O, with CH2 group

Answer

Correct answer: Option 1

Solution

The reaction involves two steps: Step 1: Br2/hv (Benzylic halogenation) The ethyl group (CH2CH3) undergoes free radical bromination at the benzylic position (the carbon adjacent to the benzene ring). The bromine substitutes for one hydrogen on the CH2 carbon, giving: benzene ring with CONH2 group and CH(Br)CH3 side chain. Step 2: KOH (dil) - Acid-Base reaction followed by intramolecular nucleophilic substitution The amide NH2 is deprotonated by KOH to form the nucleophilic NH- group. This negatively charged nitrogen then performs an intramolecular nucleophilic substitution on the benzylic carbon bearing the bromide. The bromide leaves as Br-, and a new C-N bond forms, creating a 6-membered ring (isoquinoline ring system) fused to the benzene ring. The resulting structure is a benzene ring fused with a 6-membered ring containing NH and C=O groups, with a CH3 methyl group as substituent on the newly formed ring. This is an example of the Hofmann-type rearrangement applied to an ortho-substituted benzamide with a benzylic bromide intermediate.
