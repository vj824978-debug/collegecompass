Let P(x₁, y₁) and Q(x₂, y₂) be two distinct points on the ellipse x²/9 + y²/4 = 1 such that y₁ > 0 and y₂ > 0. Let C denote the circle x² + y² = 9, and M be the point (3, 0). Suppose the line x = x₁ intersects C at R, and the line x = x₂ intersects C at S, such that the y-coordinates of R and S are positive. Let ∠ROM = π/6 and ∠SOM = π/3, where O denotes the origin (0, 0). Let |XY| denote the length of the line segment XY. Then which of the following statements is (are) TRUE?

Multiple correct (MSQ) · Marks: +4 / -2.0

  1. (A) The equation of the line joining P and Q is 2x + 3y = 3(1 + sqrt(3))
  2. (B) The equation of the line joining P and Q is 2x + y = 3(1 + sqrt(3))
  3. (C) If N₂ = (x₂, 0), then 3|N₂Q| = 2|N₂S|
  4. (D) If N₁ = (x₁, 0), then 9|N₁P| = 4|N₁R|

Answer

Correct answer: AC

Solution

Coordinate of P: (3√3/2, 1), R: (3√3/2, 3/2) Q: (3/2, √3), S: (3/2, 3√3/2) Equation of line PQ: y - √3 = (1-√3)/(3√3/2 - 3/2) (x - 3/2) ⟹ 2x + 3y = 3(1+√3) N₂Q/N₂S = √3/(3√3/2) = 2/3 Also N₁P/N₁R = 1/(3/2) = 2/3 [Circle diagram with center O, points labeled S, R, Q, P, M on the circle.
