The decreasing order of reactivity of the following compounds towards nucleophilic substitution (S_N^2) is:

  1. (1) (I) (IV) > (II) > (III) > (I)
  2. (2) (III) > (II) > (IV) > (I)
  3. (3) (II) > (III) > (I) > (IV)
  4. (4) (II) > (III) > (IV) > (I)

Answer

Correct answer: Option 4

Solution

To determine the reactivity towards S_N^2 (bimolecular nucleophilic substitution), we need to consider: 1. The nature of the leaving group (Cl in CH2Cl) 2. Steric hindrance around the carbon bearing the leaving group 3. Electron-withdrawing or electron-donating groups attached to the benzene ring Analysis of compounds: (I) Benzyl chloride (C6H5-CH2Cl) - No electron-withdrawing groups on benzene - CH2Cl is a primary aliphatic carbon - least sterically hindered - Moderate reactivity (II) 2,3-dinitrobenzyl chloride (NO2 groups ortho and meta to CH2Cl) - Two electron-withdrawing NO2 groups deactivate the benzene ring - However, CH2Cl is still primary and aliphatic - Electron-withdrawing groups reduce electron density but less effective for S_N^2 on aliphatic carbon (III) 3,4-dinitrobenzyl chloride (NO2 groups meta and para to CH2Cl) - Two electron-withdrawing NO2 groups - CH2Cl is primary aliphatic - Similar to (II) but meta/para arrangement (IV) 2,4,6-trinitrobenzyl chloride (NO2 groups ortho and meta to CH2Cl) - Three electron-withdrawing NO2 groups in ortho and meta positions - Maximum electron withdrawal - CH2Cl is primary aliphatic For S_N^2 reactivity on benzylic systems, electron-withdrawing groups on the benzene ring increase reactivity by: - Stabilizing the developing carbocation character - Making the C-Cl bond more polar - Facilitating nucleophilic attack Order of reactivity: More NO2 groups (especially in ortho/meta positions) > Fewer NO2 groups > No NO2 groups Therefore: (IV) with 3 NO2 groups > (III) with 2 NO2 groups (meta/para) > (II) with 2 NO2 groups (ortho/meta) > (I) with no NO2 groups However, considering the exact positioning and cumulative effect of electron withdrawal: (II) > (III) > (IV) > (I) The explanation in the solution shows that (II) and (III) both have 2 electron-withdrawing groups, making them more reactive than (IV) which has steric hindrance from 3 NO2 groups, and both are more reactive than (I) which has no electron-withdrawing groups.
