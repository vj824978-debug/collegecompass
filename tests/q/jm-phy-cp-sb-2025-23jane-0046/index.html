An air bubble of radius 1.0 mm is observed at a depth of 20 cm below the free surface of a liquid having surface tension 0.095 J/m^2 and density 10^3 kg/m^3. The difference between pressure inside the bubble and atmospheric pressure _______ N/m^2. (Take g = 10 m/s^2)

Answer

Correct answer: 2190

Solution

The pressure inside the bubble is given by: p_in = p_0 + ρgh + 2T/R where: - p_0 = atmospheric pressure - ρ = density of liquid = 10^3 kg/m^3 - g = 10 m/s^2 - h = depth = 20 cm = 0.20 m - T = surface tension = 0.095 J/m^2 - R = radius of bubble = 1.0 mm = 10^(-3) m The difference between pressure inside the bubble and atmospheric pressure is: ΔP = p_in - p_0 = ρgh + 2T/R Calculating each term: ρgh = 10^3 × 10 × 0.20 = 1000 × 10 × 0.20 = 2000 N/m^2 2T/R = (2 × 0.095)/(10^(-3)) = 0.19/(10^(-3)) = 190 N/m^2 Therefore: ΔP = 2000 + 190 = 2190 N/m^2
