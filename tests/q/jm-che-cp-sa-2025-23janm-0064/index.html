Propane molecule on chlorination under photochemical condition gives two di-chloro products, "x" and "y". Amongst "x" and "y", "x" is an optically active molecule. How many tri-chloro products (consider only structural isomers) will be obtained from "x" when it is further treated with chlorine under the photochemical condition?

  1. (1) 4
  2. (2) 2
  3. (3) 5
  4. (4) 3

Answer

Correct answer: Option 4

Solution

'X' is CH3-*CH-CH2 with Cl and Cl attached to the middle carbon (2,3-dichloropropane with asterisk indicating chiral center). This is the optically active di-chloro isomer. When X is further treated with Cl2 under photochemical conditions, one more chlorine is added to form tri-chloro products. The chlorine can substitute at three different positions: 1. On the CH3 group: CH2Cl-*CH-CH2 with two Cl atoms already present - gives 1,2,3-trichloropropane 2. On the *CH group: CH3-*CCl-CH2 with already two Cl atoms - but this position already has chlorines, so another Cl adds to this carbon 3. On the terminal CH2 group: CH3-*CH-CHCl2 with already two Cl atoms - gives 1,2,3-trichloropropane (different isomer) 4. Different regioisomers can be formed depending on which hydrogen is abstracted. Considering only structural isomers and the different positions where the third chlorine can be introduced on the three-carbon chain while maintaining the structure, there are 4 distinct tri-chloro structural isomers obtained: - 1,1,2-trichloropropane - 1,2,2-trichloropropane - 1,2,3-trichloropropane (can form from different substitution patterns) - 2,2,3-trichloropropane Therefore, the answer is 4 tri-chloro products.
