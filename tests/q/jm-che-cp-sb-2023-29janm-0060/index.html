17 mg of a hydrocarbon (M.F. C₁₀H₁₆) takes up 8.40 mL of the H₂ gas measured at 0°C and 760 mm of Hg. Ozonolysis of the same hydrocarbon yields CH₃ - C - CH₃, H - C - H, H - C - CH₂ - CH₂ - C - C - H with double bonds (indicated by || in the original). The number of double bonds/s present in the hydrocarbon is ________

Answer

Correct answer: 3

Solution

C₁₀H₁₆ + H₂ → reaction Calculate moles of C₁₀H₁₆: Molar mass of C₁₀H₁₆ = 10(12) + 16(1) = 136 g/mol Moles of C₁₀H₁₆ = (17 × 10^(-3))/136 = 0.125 milli moles Calculate moles of H₂: Using ideal gas law at STP (0°C, 760 mm Hg): Volume of H₂ = 8.40 mL = 8.4 × 10^(-3) L At STP, 1 mole = 22.4 L = 22400 mL Moles of H₂ = (8.4 × 10^(-3))/(0.082 × 273) = (1 × 8.4 × 10^(-3))/(0.082 × 273) = 0.375 milli moles Ratio of H₂ to C₁₀H₁₆: 0.375 milli moles H₂ / 0.125 milli moles C₁₀H₁₆ = 3 Therefore, 1 mole of C₁₀H₁₆ requires 3 moles of H₂, indicating there are 3 π-bonds (double bonds) in the hydrocarbon. Degree of unsaturation (DOU) = 3
