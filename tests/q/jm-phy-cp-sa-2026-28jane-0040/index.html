A small block of mass m slides down from the top of a frictionless inclined surface, while the inclined plane is moving towards left with constant acceleration a_0. The angle between the inclined plane and ground is theta and its base length is L. Assuming that initially the small block is at the top of the inclined plane, the time it takes to reach the lowest point of the inclined plane is ____.

  1. (1) sqrt(2L/(gsin2theta - a_0(1 + cos2theta)))
  2. (2) sqrt(4L/(gsin2theta - a_0(1 + cos2theta)))
  3. (3) sqrt(4L/(gcos^2theta - a_0sin(theta)costheta))
  4. (4) sqrt(2L/(gsintheta - a_0costheta))

Answer

Correct answer: Option 2

Solution

In the reference frame of the inclined plane (non-inertial frame), the block experiences: 1) Weight component along the plane: mgsin(theta) 2) Pseudo force due to acceleration of plane: ma_0 (towards right) 3) Component of pseudo force along the plane: ma_0cos(theta) 4) Normal reaction: N Force equation perpendicular to plane: N = mgcos(theta) + ma_0sin(theta) Force equation along the plane (taking down the plane as positive): mgsin(theta) - ma_0cos(theta) = ma Therefore: a = gsin(theta) - a_0cos(theta) Distance to travel along the plane: S = L/cos(theta) Using kinematic equation S = ut + (1/2)at^2, with u = 0: L/cos(theta) = (1/2)(gsin(theta) - a_0cos(theta))t^2 Solving for t: t^2 = 2L/(cos(theta)(gsin(theta) - a_0cos(theta))) Simplifying using trigonometric identities: sin(theta)cos(theta) = (1/2)sin(2theta) cos^2(theta) = (1 + cos(2theta))/2 t^2 = 4L/(gsin(2theta) - a_0(1 + cos(2theta))) Therefore: t = sqrt(4L/(gsin2theta - a_0(1 + cos2theta)))
