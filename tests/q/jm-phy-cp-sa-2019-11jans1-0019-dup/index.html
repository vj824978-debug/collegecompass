A compound 'X' on treatment with Br2/NaOH, provided C3H9N, which gives positive carbylamine test. Compound 'X' is:

  1. (1) CH3CH2CH2CONH2
  2. (2) CH3CON(CH3)2
  3. (3) CH3CH2COCH2NH2
  4. (4) CH3COCH2NHCH3

Answer

Correct answer: Option 1

Solution

X undergoes Br2/NaOH (Hoffmann Bromamide) reaction to give C3H9N (1° amine) that shows positive carbylamine test. The Hoffmann Bromamide reaction converts primary amides (R-CONH2) to primary amines (R-NH2) with one less carbon atom. For the product to be C3H9N (a primary amine with formula C3H9N): - The original amide must be C4H9NO - The primary amine product: C3H9N (propylamine, n-C3H7-NH2) Therefore, the original compound X must be: CH3CH2CH2-CONH2 (butanamide, 4 carbons) When treated with Br2/NaOH: CH3CH2CH2CONH2 → CH3CH2CH2NH2 + CO2 This gives n-propylamine (C3H9N), which is a primary amine and gives positive carbylamine (isocyanide) test. Answer: Option (1) CH3CH2CH2CONH2
