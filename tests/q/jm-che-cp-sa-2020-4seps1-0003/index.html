When neopentyl alcohol is heated with an acid, it slowly converted into an 85:15 mixture of alkenes A and B, respectively. What are these alkenes?

  1. (1) (H3C)C=CH-CH3 and H3C-CH=CH2
  2. (2) (H3C)C=CH-CH3 and H3C-CH=CH-CH3
  3. (3) (H3C)C=CH-CH3 and (CH3)CH=CH2
  4. (4) CH3-CH=CH-CH3 and H3C-CH=CH-CH3

Answer

Correct answer: Option 3

Solution

Neopentyl alcohol has the structure: (CH3)3C-CH2OH When heated with acid, it undergoes dehydration via carbocation rearrangement: Step 1: Protonation of OH group (CH3)3C-CH2-OH + H+ → (CH3)3C-CH2-OH2+ Step 2: Loss of water to form carbocation (CH3)3C-CH2+ (primary carbocation - unstable) Step 3: Methyl shift rearrangement The primary carbocation rearranges to a more stable tertiary carbocation: (CH3)3C-CH2+ → (CH3)2C(CH3)-CH2+ → (CH3)2(CH3)C+ - CH2 After rearrangement, we get a tertiary carbocation: (CH3)3C+ with a CH2 group Step 4: Deprotonation gives two possible alkenes (Zaitsev's rule - major product is more substituted alkene): (A) Major product (85%) - more substituted alkene: (H3C)C=CH-CH3 (2-methylbut-2-ene, trisubstituted) (B) Minor product (15%) - less substituted alkene: (CH3)CH=CH2 (2-methylprop-1-ene, disubstituted) The major product follows Zaitsev's rule which states that the more substituted alkene is the major product in elimination reactions.
