The major product of the following reaction is: A benzene ring with CH3 at the top position and NO2 at the bottom position is treated with 2HBr

  1. (1) A cyclohexadiene structure with H3C and Br at adjacent positions on top, and Br and NO2 at adjacent positions on bottom
  2. (2) A cyclohexadiene structure with Br and CH3 at adjacent positions on top, and Br and NO2 at adjacent positions on bottom
  3. (3) A cyclohexadiene structure with CH3 and Br at adjacent positions on top, and Br and NO2 at adjacent positions on bottom
  4. (4) A cyclohexadiene structure with CH3 and Br at adjacent positions on top, and Br and NO2 at adjacent positions on bottom (geminal to each other)

Answer

Correct answer: Option 2

Solution

The reaction involves addition of 2 equivalents of HBr to the benzene ring containing electron-donating (CH3) and electron-withdrawing (NO2) groups. Benzene ring activation/deactivation: - CH3 is an ortho/para-directing electron-donating group (EDG) - NO2 is a meta-directing electron-withdrawing group (EWG) These groups are in para positions (opposite ends of the benzene ring). When HBr adds to this aromatic system: 1. The CH3 group activates the benzene ring and directs incoming electrophiles to ortho and para positions 2. The NO2 group deactivates the ring but directs to meta positions 3. Since these groups are para to each other, the ortho positions to CH3 are meta to NO2, making them the most activated sites 4. HBr adds across the double bonds formed in the resonance structures The major product results from addition at the positions ortho to the methyl group (which are meta to the nitro group). This places both bromines on adjacent carbons, with one Br on the carbon adjacent to the carbon bearing CH3, and the other Br also adjacent to the NO2-bearing carbon. Product (2) shows this regiochemistry correctly with Br and CH3 at adjacent positions and Br and NO2 at adjacent positions.
