A galvanometer having a coil resistance 100 Ω gives a full scale deflection when a current of 1 mA is passed through it. What is the value of the resistance which can convert this galvanometer into a voltmeter giving full scale deflection for a potential difference of 10 V?

  1. (1) 8.9 kΩ
  2. (2) 7.9 kΩ
  3. (3) 9.9 kΩ
  4. (4) 10 kΩ

Answer

Correct answer: Option 3

Solution

To convert a galvanometer into a voltmeter, we need to connect a high resistance R_s (called multiplier) in series with the galvanometer. Given: - Coil resistance of galvanometer, R_g = 100 Ω - Full scale deflection current, I_g = 1 mA = 1 × 10^(-3) A - Required potential difference for full scale deflection, V = 10 V For full scale deflection of the voltmeter, the current through the galvanometer should be I_g and the total potential difference across the combination should be V. Using Ohm's law: V = I_g × (R_g + R_s) 10 = (1 × 10^(-3)) × (100 + R_s) 10 = 0.001 × (100 + R_s) 10/0.001 = 100 + R_s 10,000 = 100 + R_s R_s = 10,000 - 100 R_s = 9,900 Ω = 9.9 kΩ
