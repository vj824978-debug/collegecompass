What will be the major product in the following mononitrition reaction?

  1. (1) NO2 group at para position of the benzene ring attached to N-H, with O=C-Ph group attached to N
  2. (2) NO2 group at para position of the benzene ring attached to C=O, with Ph-NH attached to that benzene
  3. (3) NO2 group at meta position of the benzene ring attached to N-H, with O=C-Ph group attached to N
  4. (4) NO2 group at ortho position of the benzene ring attached to C=O, with Ph-NH attached to that benzene

Answer

Correct answer: Option 1

Solution

The starting material is N-phenylbenzamide (C6H5-CO-NH-C6H5). This is a mononitrition reaction using HNO3 and concentrated H2SO4. To determine the major product, we need to analyze the directing effects: 1. The amide group (CO-NH) is an electron-withdrawing group (EWG) due to the carbonyl, making the benzene ring it is attached to deactivated and meta-directing. 2. The N-H of the amide provides electron donation through resonance to the phenyl group it is attached to, making that benzene ring activated and ortho/para-directing. 3. Between the two benzene rings, the one directly attached to nitrogen (N-phenyl) is more electron-rich and thus more activated. 4. The solution explanation states: "More e- rich so E+ will attack here" - referring to the phenyl ring attached to nitrogen. 5. On the activated phenyl ring (C6H5-N-), the nitration will occur at the ortho and para positions. The para position is typically preferred due to steric reasons. 6. Therefore, the major product is 4-nitro-N-phenylbenzamide, with the NO2 group at the para position of the phenyl ring attached to nitrogen. The answer is option (1): NO2 at para position on the N-attached benzene ring.
