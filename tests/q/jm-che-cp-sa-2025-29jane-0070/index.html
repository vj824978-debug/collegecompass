Which one of the following, with HBr will give a phenol?

  1. (1) Benzene with CH2 group attached to a carbon with two OCH3 groups and one H
  2. (2) Phenyl methyl ether (C6H5-O-CH3)
  3. (3) Benzyl methyl ether (C6H5-CH2-O-CH3)
  4. (4) Benzyl ether with a branched structure containing CH and CH3 groups

Answer

Correct answer: Option 2

Solution

Anisole (C6H5-O-CH3) reacts with HBr through the following mechanism: 1. Protonation: HBr protonates the oxygen of the methoxy group to form C6H5-O(+)H-CH3 2. SN2 Cleavage: Bromide ion attacks the methyl group, breaking the C-O bond: C6H5-O(+)H-CH3 + Br(-) → C6H5-OH + CH3Br 3. Product: Phenol (C6H5-OH) is formed along with methyl bromide (CH3Br) This is a classic ether cleavage reaction where HBr cleaves the C-O bond of an aryl methyl ether to produce a phenol. The mechanism proceeds via SN2 attack on the activated methyl group after protonation of the oxygen. The other options do not produce phenol: - Option (1): Produces benzyl alcohol derivative, not phenol - Option (3): Produces benzyl alcohol, not phenol - Option (4): Produces a different product, not phenol
