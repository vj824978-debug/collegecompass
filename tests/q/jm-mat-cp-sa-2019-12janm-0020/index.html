Let C1 and C2 be the centres of the circles x^2 + y^2 - 2x - 2y - 2 = 0 and x^2 + y^2 - 6x - 6y + 14 = 0 respectively. If P and Q are the points of intersection of these circles, then the area (in sq. units) of the quadrilateral PC1QC2 is:

  1. (1) 4
  2. (2) 6
  3. (3) 9
  4. (4) 8

Answer

Correct answer: Option 1

Solution

For circle 1: x^2 + y^2 - 2x - 2y - 2 = 0 Rewriting: (x-1)^2 + (y-1)^2 = 4 Centre C1 = (1,1), radius r1 = 2 For circle 2: x^2 + y^2 - 6x - 6y + 14 = 0 Rewriting: (x-3)^2 + (y-3)^2 = 4 Centre C2 = (3,3), radius r2 = 2 Equation of PQ (line of intersection): Subtracting circle 1 from circle 2: (x^2 + y^2 - 6x - 6y + 14) - (x^2 + y^2 - 2x - 2y - 2) = 0 -4x - 4y + 16 = 0 x + y - 4 = 0 Distance from C1(1,1) to line x + y - 4 = 0: PM = |1 + 1 - 4|/sqrt(2) = |-2|/sqrt(2) = 2/sqrt(2) = sqrt(2) Distance from C2(3,3) to line x + y - 4 = 0: C2M = |3 + 3 - 4|/sqrt(2) = |2|/sqrt(2) = 2/sqrt(2) = sqrt(2) In triangle C1PM: C1P = 2 (radius) PM = sqrt(2) By Pythagoras: PP^2 = C1P^2 - PM^2 = 4 - 2 = 2 So PM = sqrt(2) Length PQ = 2 * sqrt(C1P^2 - PM^2) = 2 * sqrt(4 - 2) = 2 * sqrt(2) = 2sqrt(2) Distance C1C2 = sqrt((3-1)^2 + (3-1)^2) = sqrt(4 + 4) = 2sqrt(2) The quadrilateral PC1QC2 is a rhombus with diagonals PQ and C1C2. Area = (1/2) * d1 * d2 = (1/2) * 2sqrt(2) * 2sqrt(2) = (1/2) * 8 = 4
