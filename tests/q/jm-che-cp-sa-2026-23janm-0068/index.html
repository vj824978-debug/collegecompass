Compound 'P' undergoes the following sequence of reactions: P --(i) NH3, (ii) Delta--> Q --(i) KOH, Br2, (ii) CHCl3, KOH (alc), Delta--> Cyclohexyl-NC. 'P' is:

  1. (1) Cyclohexanecarbaldehyde (cyclohexyl-CHO)
  2. (2) Cyclohexanecarboxylic acid (cyclohexyl-COOH)
  3. (3) Cyclohexyl ethyl ketone (cyclohexyl-CO-CH2-CH3)
  4. (4) Cyclohexanecarboxamide (cyclohexyl-CONH2)

Answer

Correct answer: Option 2

Solution

Let us trace backward from the final product to identify compound P. Final Product: Cyclohexyl-NC (isocyanide/isonitrile) The conversion to isocyanide occurs via the sequence: (i) KOH, Br2 (ii) CHCl3, KOH (alc), Delta This is the Hofmann degradation/rearrangement sequence: - Step (i): KOH, Br2 converts primary amide (R-CONH2) to isocyanide through formation of N-bromoamide - Step (ii): CHCl3 and KOH complete the rearrangement to form R-NC Therefore, compound Q must be: Cyclohexyl-CONH2 (cyclohexanecarboxamide/primary amide) Now, P converts to Q (cyclohexyl-CONH2) via: (i) NH3 (ii) Delta (heat) This is the conversion of a carboxylic acid derivative to primary amide: - R-COOH + NH3 --(heat)--> R-CONH2 This reaction occurs most readily from carboxylic acids. Direct heating with ammonia converts carboxylic acids to amides. Therefore, compound P must be: Cyclohexanecarboxylic acid (cyclohexyl-COOH) Verification of reaction sequence: Cyclohexyl-COOH --(NH3, heat)--> Cyclohexyl-CONH2 --(KOH, Br2, then CHCl3, KOH, heat)--> Cyclohexyl-NC ✓ The answer is option (2): Cyclohexanecarboxylic acid
