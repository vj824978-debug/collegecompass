A and B are two substances undergoing radioactive decay in a container. The half life of A is 15 min and that of B is 5 min. If the initial concentration of B is 4 times that of A and they both start decaying at the same time, how much time will it take for the concentration of both of them to be same?

Answer

Correct answer: 15

Solution

Let initial concentration of A = Co Initial concentration of B = 4Co For substance A with half-life = 15 min: After time t, concentration [A] = Co × (1/2)^(t/15) For substance B with half-life = 5 min: After time t, concentration [B] = 4Co × (1/2)^(t/5) We need to find t when [A] = [B]: Co × (1/2)^(t/15) = 4Co × (1/2)^(t/5) Dividing both sides by Co: (1/2)^(t/15) = 4 × (1/2)^(t/5) (1/2)^(t/15) = 2^2 × (1/2)^(t/5) (1/2)^(t/15) = 2^2 × 2^(-t/5) 2^(-t/15) = 2^(2-t/5) Comparing exponents: -t/15 = 2 - t/5 -t/15 + t/5 = 2 t(-1/15 + 1/5) = 2 t(-1/15 + 3/15) = 2 t(2/15) = 2 t = 2 × 15/2 = 15 min Verification: After 15 min: [A] = Co × (1/2)^(15/15) = Co × (1/2) = Co/2 [B] = 4Co × (1/2)^(15/5) = 4Co × (1/2)^3 = 4Co × 1/8 = Co/2 Both concentrations are equal at t = 15 min
