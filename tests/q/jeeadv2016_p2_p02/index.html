The ends Q and R of two thin wires, PQ and RS, are soldered (joined) together. Initially each of the wires has a length of 1 m at 10 °C. Now the end P is maintained at 10 °C, while the end S is heated and maintained at 400 °C. The system is thermally insulated from its surroundings. If the thermal conductivity of wire PQ is twice that of the wire RS and the coefficient of linear thermal expansion of PQ is 1.2 × 10⁻⁵ K⁻¹, the change in length of the wire PQ is

Single correct MCQ · Marks: +3 / -1.0

  1. (A) (A) 0.78 mm
  2. (B) (B) 0.90 mm
  3. (C) (C) 1.56 mm
  4. (D) (D) 2.34 mm

Answer

Correct answer: A

Solution

From given data: R_PQ/R_RS = 1/2 so, T - 10 = (400 - T)/2 ⟹ T = 140 °C As a function of x, T(x) = 10 + 130x ⟹ ΔT(x) = T(x) - 10 = 130x Extension in a small element of length dx is dℓ = αΔT(x)dx = 130α xdx ⟹ Net extension Δℓ = 130α ∫₀¹ xdx = (130/2) × 1.2 × 10⁻⁵ × 1 or, Δℓ = 0.78 mm [Diagram shows a horizontal line with points P, Q, R, S marked from left to right, with distance x indicated by an arrow above, and origin at P]
