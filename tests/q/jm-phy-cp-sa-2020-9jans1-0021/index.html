In a Young's double slit experiment 15 fringes are observed on a small portion of the screen when light of wavelength 500 nm is used. Ten fringes are observed on the same section of the screen when another light source of wavelength lambda is used. Then the value of lambda is (in nm).

Answer

Correct answer: 750

Solution

For Young's double slit experiment, the fringe width is given by: beta = lambda * D / d where D is the distance from slits to screen and d is the separation between slits. For a fixed portion of the screen, the number of fringes observed is inversely proportional to the fringe width. For the first wavelength (500 nm): 15 fringes are observed For the second wavelength (lambda): 10 fringes are observed Since the same section of the screen is considered: N_1 * beta_1 = N_2 * beta_2 15 * 500 * (D/d) = 10 * lambda * (D/d) The D/d terms cancel out: 15 * 500 = 10 * lambda lambda = (15 * 500) / 10 = 7500 / 10 = 750 nm
