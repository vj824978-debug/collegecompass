If the magnetic moment of a dioxygen species is 1.73 B.M, it may be:

  1. (1) O2, O2- or O2+
  2. (2) O2 or O2-
  3. (3) O2 + O2+
  4. (4) O2- or O2+

Answer

Correct answer: Option 4

Solution

Magnetic moment = 1.73 B.M Unpaired electron = 1 Electron configuration of O2: O2 = σ1s^2, σ*1s^2, σ2s^2, σ*2s^2, σ2p^2, π2p_x^2, π2p_y^2, π*2p_x^1, π*2p_y^1 For O2: has 2 unpaired electrons (π*2p_x^1, π*2p_y^1) Magnetic moment of O2 = sqrt(2(2+1)) = sqrt(6) ≈ 2.45 B.M For O2- (adding 1 electron): σ1s^2, σ*1s^2, σ2s^2, σ*2s^2, σ2p^2, π2p_x^2, π2p_y^2, π*2p_x^2, π*2p_y^1 O2- has 1 unpaired electron Magnetic moment of O2- = sqrt(1(1+1)) = sqrt(2) ≈ 1.41 B.M For O2+ (removing 1 electron): σ1s^2, σ*1s^2, σ2s^2, σ*2s^2, σ2p^2, π2p_x^2, π2p_y^2, π*2p_x^1 O2+ has 1 unpaired electron Magnetic moment of O2+ = sqrt(1(1+1)) = sqrt(2) ≈ 1.41 B.M ≈ 1.73 B.M (approximately) Hence O2- & O2+ have one unpaired electron each and both can have magnetic moment of 1.73 B.M.
