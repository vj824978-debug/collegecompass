The total number of isomers for a square planar complex [M(F)(Cl)(SCN)(NO2)] is:

  1. (1) 16
  2. (2) 8
  3. (3) 12
  4. (4) 4

Answer

Correct answer: Option 3

Solution

For the square planar complex [M(F)(Cl)(SCN)(NO2)], we need to count all possible isomers. For a square planar geometry with four different ligands, three geometric isomers (G.I.) are possible: (A) Cl and SCN are adjacent (cis); F and NO2 are adjacent (cis) (B) Cl and NH3 are adjacent; F and SCN are opposite (trans) (C) Cl and NH3 are adjacent; SCN and NO2 are opposite (trans) For each geometric isomer, linkage isomers are possible: For (A) - SCN can be SCN or NCS (2 linkage isomers); NO2 can be NO2 or ONO (2 linkage isomers) Total for (A) = 2 × 2 = 4 isomers (represented as: SCN-NO2, SCN-ONO, NCS-NO2, ONO-NH3 combinations) Actually counting the three geometric isomers shown: - First G.I.: SCN/NH3 arrangement - Second G.I.: SCN/NH3 arrangement - Third G.I.: SCN/NH3 arrangement For each geometric isomer, SCN can be SCN or NCS (2 forms), and NO2 can be NO2 or ONO (2 forms). Total isomers = 3 geometric isomers × 2 (SCN/NCS linkage) × 2 (NO2/ONO linkage) = 3 × 2 × 2 = 12 isomers
