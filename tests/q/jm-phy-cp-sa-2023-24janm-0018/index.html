From the photoelectric effect experiment, following observations are made. Identify which of these are correct. A. The stopping potential depends only on the work function of the metal. B. The saturation current increases as the intensity of incident light increases. C. The maximum kinetic energy of a photo electron depends on the intensity of the incident light. D. Photoelectric effect can be explained using wave theory of light.

  1. (1) A, B, D only
  2. (2) A, C, D only
  3. (3) B only
  4. (4) B, C only

Answer

Correct answer: Option 3

Solution

(A) From Einstein's equation K_max = eV_s = hv - phi Form the stopping potential (V_s) depends on phi & v. This is INCORRECT because stopping potential depends on both the work function phi and the frequency v of incident light, not just the work function. (B) Saturation current is proportional to intensity, i.e., number of incident photons. This is CORRECT. The saturation current depends on the number of photons hitting the surface per unit time, which is proportional to the intensity of incident light. (C) K_max only depends on nature of photon and phi. This is INCORRECT because K_max depends on the frequency of the incident photon (nature of photon) and the work function phi. It does not depend on intensity. The intensity only affects the saturation current (number of electrons ejected), not the maximum kinetic energy of individual electrons. (D) Einstein used particle behaviour of photon to explain photoelectric effect. This is INCORRECT because the wave theory of light cannot explain the photoelectric effect. Einstein's explanation requires treating light as particles (photons) with energy E = hv. The wave theory predicts that the kinetic energy of ejected electrons should depend on intensity, which contradicts experimental observations. Only B is correct.
