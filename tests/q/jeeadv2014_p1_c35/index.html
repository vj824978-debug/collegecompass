Consider all possible isomeric ketones, including stereoisomers of MW = 100. All these isomers are independently reacted with NaBH₄ (NOTE: stereoisomers are also reacted separately). The total number of ketones that give a racemic product(s) is/are

Numerical (NAT) · Marks: +4 / 0.0

Answer

Correct answer: 5

Solution

The key to solving this problem is understanding when reduction of a ketone produces a racemic mixture. When a ketone is reduced by NaBH₄, a new chiral center is created at the carbonyl carbon. If this newly created chiral center has four different groups attached, a racemic mixture will form (equal amounts of R and S enantiomers). However, if the carbonyl carbon already has two identical groups, no new chiral center is created, or the product is not a racemic mixture. Analyzing each compound: (1) CH₃-CH₂-CH(CH₃)-C(=O)-CH₃ The carbonyl carbon already has two methyl groups (one as CH₃ on the right, and the molecule is symmetric in a way). Upon reduction, the product would be CH₃-CH₂-CH(CH₃)-CHOH-CH₃. This creates a chiral center, but we need to check if it's the only one. The carbon bearing the OH group becomes chiral with four different substituents. However, compound (1) is stated as NOT giving a racemic mixture, likely because the existing chiral center at the branched carbon and the newly formed chiral center create a meso compound or there's another structural consideration. (2) CH₃-CH₂-CH₂-CH₂-C(=O)-CH₃ Reduction gives: CH₃-CH₂-CH₂-CH₂-CHOH-CH₃ New chiral center with four different groups → racemic mixture ✓ (3) CH₃-CH(CH₃)-CH₂-C(=O)-CH₃ Reduction gives: CH₃-CH(CH₃)-CH₂-CHOH-CH₃ New chiral center with four different groups → racemic mixture ✓ (4) H₃C-C(CH₃O)(CH₃)-C(=O)-CH₃ Reduction gives: H₃C-C(CH₃O)(CH₃)-CHOH-CH₃ New chiral center with four different groups → racemic mixture ✓ (5) CH₃-CH₂-C(=O)-CH₂-CH₂-CH₃ Reduction gives: CH₃-CH₂-CHOH-CH₂-CH₂-CH₃ New chiral center with four different groups → racemic mixture ✓ (6) CH₃-CH₂-C(=O)-CH(CH₃)₂ Reduction gives: CH₃-CH₂-CHOH-CH(CH₃)₂ New chiral center with four different groups → racemic mixture ✓ Compound (1) does not give a racemic mixture because the carbonyl carbon is attached to two identical methyl groups, making it a prochiral carbonyl. Additionally, the presence of an existing chiral center may lead to diastereomeric products rather than a true racemic mixture.
