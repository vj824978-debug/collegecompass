When a particle of mass m is attached to a vertical spring of spring constant k and released, its motion is described by y(t) = y0*sin^2(ωt), where 'y' is measured from the lower end of unstretched spring. Then ω is:

  1. (1) sqrt(g/y0)
  2. (2) sqrt(g/(2*y0))
  3. (3) (1/2)*sqrt(g/y0)
  4. (4) sqrt(2*g/y0)

Answer

Correct answer: Option 2

Solution

y = y0*sin^2(ωt) Using the identity sin^2(ωt) = (1 - cos(2ωt))/2: y = (y0/2)*(1 - cos(2ωt)) y = y0/2 - (y0/2)*cos(2ωt) This represents simple harmonic motion about the equilibrium position y0/2 with amplitude y0/2. From the diagram and analysis: y0/2 = mg/k (equilibrium position from natural length) For a mass on a spring, the angular frequency is: ω_natural = sqrt(k/m) From y0/2 = mg/k, we get: k = 2mg/y0 Therefore: 2ω = sqrt(k/m) = sqrt(2mg/(m*y0)) = sqrt(2g/y0) Solving for ω: ω = sqrt(2g/y0)/(2) Wait, let me recalculate. The equation y = y0/2 - (y0/2)*cos(2ωt) shows the angular frequency of oscillation is 2ω. For oscillation about equilibrium with k/m: ω_oscillation^2 = k/m But 2ω represents the frequency in our equation, so: (2ω)^2 = k/m From equilibrium: k = 2mg/y0 Therefore: 4ω^2 = (2mg/y0)/m = 2g/y0 ω^2 = g/(2y0) ω = sqrt(g/(2*y0))
