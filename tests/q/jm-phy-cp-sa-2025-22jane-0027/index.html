A small rigid spherical ball of mass M is dropped in a long vertical tube containing glycerine. The velocity of the ball becomes constant after some time. If the density of glycerine is half the density of the ball, then the viscous force acting on the ball will be (consider g as acceleration due to gravity)

  1. (1) (3/2) Mg
  2. (2) Mg/2
  3. (3) Mg
  4. (4) 2 Mg

Answer

Correct answer: Option 2

Solution

When the ball reaches terminal velocity, the acceleration becomes zero (a = 0). At this point, the net force on the ball is zero. Forces acting on the ball: 1. Weight: mg (downward) 2. Buoyant force: F_B (upward) 3. Viscous force: f (upward) Applying equilibrium condition: mg - F_B - f = 0 Buoyant force F_B = weight of glycerine displaced F_B = (density of glycerine) × (volume of ball) × g Given: density of glycerine = (1/2) × density of ball Let density of ball = ρ_ball Then density of glycerine = ρ_glycerine = ρ_ball/2 Volume of ball V = M/ρ_ball F_B = ρ_glycerine × V × g = (ρ_ball/2) × (M/ρ_ball) × g = Mg/2 From equilibrium: mg - Mg/2 - f = 0 f = Mg - Mg/2 = Mg/2
