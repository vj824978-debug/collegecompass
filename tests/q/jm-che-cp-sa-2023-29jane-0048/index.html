Reaction of propanamide with Br₂/KOH (aq) produces:

  1. (1) Propanenitrile
  2. (2) Ethylnitrile
  3. (3) Ethylamine
  4. (4) Propylamine

Answer

Correct answer: Option 3

Solution

Propanamide reacts with Br₂/KOH (aq) to undergo Hofmann's degradation (Hofmann rearrangement). In this reaction, a primary amide loses one carbon atom from the carbonyl group and gets converted to a primary amine with one less carbon. Propanamide: CH₃CH₂CONH₂ The reaction mechanism involves: 1. Formation of N-bromo intermediate: CH₃CH₂CONBr (from reaction with Br₂) 2. Base-catalyzed rearrangement where the alkyl group migrates from carbon to nitrogen 3. Loss of CO₂ to form isocyanate intermediate 4. Hydrolysis to form primary amine Product: CH₃CH₂NH₂ (Ethylamine) The carbon chain is shortened by one carbon (from 3 carbons in propanamide to 2 carbons in ethylamine), and the amide group is converted to a primary amine (-NH₂). Therefore, the answer is Ethylamine.
