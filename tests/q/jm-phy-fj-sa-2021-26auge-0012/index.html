The two thin coaxial rings, each of radius 'a' and having charges +Q and -Q respectively are separated by a distance 's'. The potential difference between the centres of the two rings is:

  1. (1) (Q/2*pi*epsilon_0) * (1/a - 1/sqrt(s^2 + a^2))
  2. (2) (Q/4*pi*epsilon_0) * (1/a - 1/sqrt(s^2 + a^2))
  3. (3) (Q/2*pi*epsilon_0) * (1/a + 1/sqrt(s^2 + a^2))
  4. (4) (Q/4*pi*epsilon_0) * (1/a + 1/sqrt(s^2 + a^2))

Answer

Correct answer: Option 1

Solution

For a charged ring of radius 'a' with charge Q, the potential at a point on the axis at distance x from the centre is V = (1/4*pi*epsilon_0) * Q/sqrt(x^2 + a^2). At the centre of the ring (x=0), V = (1/4*pi*epsilon_0) * Q/a. For the +Q ring at point A (its centre): V_A = (1/4*pi*epsilon_0) * Q/a. For the -Q ring at point B (its centre, distance s away from A): V_B = -(1/4*pi*epsilon_0) * Q/sqrt(s^2 + a^2). The potential difference V_A - V_B = (1/4*pi*epsilon_0) * Q * (1/a + 1/sqrt(s^2 + a^2)) = (Q/4*pi*epsilon_0) * (1/a - (-1/sqrt(s^2 + a^2))) = (Q/4*pi*epsilon_0) * (1/a - 1/sqrt(s^2 + a^2))
