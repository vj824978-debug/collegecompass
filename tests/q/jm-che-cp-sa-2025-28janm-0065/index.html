What is the freezing point depression constant of a solvent, 50 g of which contain 1 g non volatile solute (molar mass 256 g mol^(-1)) and the decrease in freezing point is 0.40 K?

  1. (1) 5.12 K kg mol^(-1)
  2. (2) 4.43 K kg mol^(-1)
  3. (3) 1.86 K kg mol^(-1)
  4. (4) 3.72 K kg mol^(-1)

Answer

Correct answer: Option 1

Solution

ΔT_f = K_f × m where ΔT_f is the freezing point depression, K_f is the freezing point depression constant, and m is the molality. Given: - Mass of solvent = 50 g = 50 × 10^(-3) kg - Mass of solute = 1 g - Molar mass of solute = 256 g mol^(-1) - ΔT_f = 0.40 K Molality (m) = (mass of solute in g / molar mass of solute) / (mass of solvent in kg) m = (1 / 256) / (50 × 10^(-3)) m = (1/256) / (50 × 10^(-3)) Now applying the formula: 0.40 = K_f × (1/256)/(50 × 10^(-3)) 0.40 = K_f × (1)/(256 × 50 × 10^(-3)) K_f = 0.40 × 256 × 50 × 10^(-3) / 1 K_f = 0.40 × 256 × 0.05 K_f = 0.40 × 12.8 K_f = 5.12 K kg mol^(-1)
