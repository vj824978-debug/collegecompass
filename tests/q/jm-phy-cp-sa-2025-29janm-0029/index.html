The pair of physical quantities not having same dimensions is:

  1. (1) Torque and energy
  2. (2) Surface tension and impulse
  3. (3) Angular momentum and Planck's constant
  4. (4) Pressure and Young's modulus

Answer

Correct answer: Option 2

Solution

[τ] = [E] [σ] ≠ [I] [L] = [h] [P] = [Y] Let us check each pair: (1) Torque and Energy: [Torque] = [Force × Distance] = ML^2T^(-2) [Energy] = ML^2T^(-2) Both have same dimensions. (2) Surface tension and Impulse: [Surface tension] = [Force/Length] = MLT^(-2)/L = MT^(-2) [Impulse] = [Force × Time] = MLT^(-2) × T = MLT^(-1) These have different dimensions. (3) Angular momentum and Planck's constant: [Angular momentum] = [Mass × Velocity × Distance] = M × LT^(-1) × L = ML^2T^(-1) [Planck's constant] = [Energy × Time] = ML^2T^(-2) × T = ML^2T^(-1) Both have same dimensions. (4) Pressure and Young's modulus: [Pressure] = [Force/Area] = MLT^(-2)/L^2 = ML^(-1)T^(-2) [Young's modulus] = [Stress/Strain] = [Force/Area]/[dimensionless] = ML^(-1)T^(-2) Both have same dimensions. Therefore, Surface tension and Impulse do not have same dimensions.
