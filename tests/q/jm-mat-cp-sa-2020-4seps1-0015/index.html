Two vertical poles AB = 15 m and CD = 10 m are standing apart on a horizontal ground with points A and C on the ground. If P is the point of intersection of BC and AD, then the height of P (in m) above the line AC is:

  1. (1) 10/3
  2. (2) 20/3
  3. (3) 5
  4. (4) 6

Answer

Correct answer: Option 4

Solution

Let us set up a coordinate system with A at the origin and C on the positive x-axis at distance x. Point B is at (0, 15) and point D is at (x, 10). The line AD goes from A(0, 0) to D(x, 10), so its equation is: y = (10/x) * x_coord The line BC goes from B(0, 15) to C(x, 0), so its equation is: y = 15 - (15/x) * x_coord Let P be the intersection point at horizontal distance from A equal to some value, and let h be its height. From the geometry, using similar triangles: tan(theta) = 10/x₂ = h/x₂ => x₂ = hx/10 tan(phi) = 15/x₁ = h/x₁ => x₁ = hx/15 Now, x₁ + x₂ = x (since the horizontal distances must add up): hx/15 + hx/10 = x Dividing by x: h/15 + h/10 = 1 Finding common denominator (30): 2h/30 + 3h/30 = 1 5h/30 = 1 h/6 = 1 h = 6
