Let k be a positive real number and let A = [2*sqrt(k), 1, -2k; -2*sqrt(k), 2k, -1] and B = [1-2k, 0, 2*sqrt(k); -sqrt(k), -2*sqrt(k), 0]. If det(adj A) + det(adj B) = 10^6, then [k] is equal to [Note: adj M denotes the adjoint of a square matrix M and [k] denotes the largest integer less than or equal to k].

Numerical (NAT) · Marks: +4 / 0.0

Answer

Correct answer: 5

Solution

|A| = (2k + 1)³, |B| = 0 (Since B is a skew-symmetric matrix of order 3) ⟹ det (adj A) = |A|^(n-1) = ((2k + 1)³)² = 106 ⟹ 2k + 1 = 10 ⟹ 2k = 9 [k] = 4
