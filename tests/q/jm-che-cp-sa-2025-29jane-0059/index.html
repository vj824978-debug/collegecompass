0.1 M solution of KI reacts with excess of H2SO4 and KIO3 solution. According to equation 5I^- + IO3^- + 6H^+ → 3I2 + 3H2O. Identify the correct statements: (A) 200 mL of KI solution reacts with 0.004 mol of KIO3. (B) 200 mL of KI solution reacts with 0.006 mol of H2SO4. (C) 0.5 L of KI solution produced 0.005 mol of I2. (D) Equivalent weight of KIO3 is equal to (Molecular weight / 5)

  1. (1) (A) and (D) only
  2. (2) (B) and (C) only
  3. (3) (A) and (B) only
  4. (4) (C) and (D) only

Answer

Correct answer: Option 1

Solution

Given: 0.1 M KI solution, reaction equation: 5I^- + IO3^- + 6H^+ → 3I2 + 3H2O First, calculate moles of I^- in different volumes: - In 200 mL = 0.2 L: moles of I^- = 0.1 × 0.2 = 0.02 mol - In 500 mL = 0.5 L: moles of I^- = 0.1 × 0.5 = 0.05 mol From stoichiometry: 5I^- + IO3^- + 6H^+ → 3I2 + 3H2O Ratio I^- : IO3^- = 5 : 1 Ratio I^- : I2 = 5 : 3 Ratio IO3^- : H^+ = 1 : 6 Checking statement (A): For 0.02 mol of I^-, moles of IO3^- needed = 0.02/5 = 0.004 mol. TRUE Checking statement (B): For 0.02 mol of I^-, moles of H^+ needed = (0.02/5) × 6 = 0.024 mol. H2SO4 provides 2H^+ per molecule, so moles of H2SO4 = 0.024/2 = 0.012 mol, not 0.006 mol. FALSE Checking statement (C): For 0.05 mol of I^-, moles of I2 produced = (0.05/5) × 3 = 0.03 mol, not 0.005 mol. FALSE Checking statement (D): In the reaction, IO3^- gains 5 electrons (from +5 to 0 in I2) while I^- loses 1 electron each (from -1 to 0 in I2). For IO3^-, the n-factor (number of electrons transferred per molecule) = 5. Equivalent weight = Molecular weight / n-factor = Molecular weight / 5. TRUE Therefore, statements (A) and (D) are correct.
