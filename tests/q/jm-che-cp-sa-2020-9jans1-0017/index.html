The decreasing order of basicity of the following amines is:

  1. (1) (I) (III) > (I) > (II) > (IV)
  2. (2) (II) > (III) > (IV) > (I)
  3. (3) (I) > (III) > (IV) > (II)
  4. (4) (III) > (II) > (I) > (IV)

Answer

Correct answer: Option 4

Solution

To determine the basicity order of amines, we need to consider factors affecting the availability of the lone pair on nitrogen. Compound (III) - Cyclohexylamine (aliphatic amine): Nitrogen l.p. not participate in resonance. The lone pair is fully available for donation, making it highly basic. Compound (I) - Aniline (aromatic primary amine): Nitrogen l.p. participate in resonance. The lone pair on nitrogen is partially delocalized into the benzene ring through resonance, reducing its availability for protonation. This decreases basicity compared to aliphatic amines. Compound (II) - Pyridine (aromatic heterocycle): Nitrogen l.p. participate in resonance and increase the stability of the compound due to aromaticity. The lone pair participates in maintaining the aromatic character, making it less available for protonation. However, pyridine is more basic than aniline because the nitrogen's lone pair is in an sp2 orbital (more electronegative orientation). Compound (IV) - Pyrrole (aromatic heterocycle): Nitrogen l.p. participate in resonance due to increase the stability of the compound. The lone pair of nitrogen is essential for the aromaticity of pyrrole (contributes to the 4n+2 pi electrons). The lone pair is heavily involved in resonance, making it least available for protonation. This makes pyrrole the weakest base. Decreasing order of basicity: (III) > (II) > (I) > (IV) Note: Aliphatic amines > aromatic amines with conjugated lone pairs > heterocycles with lone pairs in resonance.
