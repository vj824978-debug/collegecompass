A single current carrying loop of wire carrying current I flowing in anticlockwise direction seen from +ve z-direction and lying in xy plane is shown in figure. The plot of y component of magnetic field (B_y) at a distance 'a' (less than radius of the coil) and on yz plane vs z coordinate look like

  1. (1) B_y(0, a, z) with a wave pattern showing a peak near z=0, then oscillating and crossing zero
  2. (2) B_y(0, a, z) with a symmetric peak centered at z=0
  3. (3) B_y(0, a, z) with two peaks equidistant from z=0 with a dip in the middle
  4. (4) B_y(0, a, z) with a flat-topped symmetric distribution centered at z=0

Answer

Correct answer: Option 1

Solution

To determine the y-component of the magnetic field B_y at a point P_1 = (0, a, z) on the yz plane at distance 'a' from the z-axis: The current loop lies in the xy plane with current I flowing anticlockwise when viewed from +ve z-direction. Using Biot-Savart law or symmetry arguments: 1. At z = 0 (center of the loop): The magnetic field lines are parallel to the xy plane and perpendicular to the radial direction from the loop axis. At point (0, a, 0), which is inside the loop but offset from the axis, B_y will have a significant value. 2. For the y-component specifically at (0, a, z): - Due to the anticlockwise current (when viewed from +z), the field contributions from different parts of the loop will have y-components. - At z = 0, B_y reaches a peak. - As z increases positively or negatively, the contributions change. - For large |z|, the field approaches that of a magnetic dipole, and B_y → 0. 3. The key observation from the solution diagram: The magnetic field line shown indicates that at point (0, a, 0), the field has a positive y-component. The field lines curve around the loop. 4. As we move along the z-axis from negative to positive z: - B_y starts near zero for large negative z - B_y increases as z approaches 0 - B_y reaches maximum near z = 0 - B_y decreases as z increases beyond 0 - B_y approaches zero for large positive z - The shape shows an asymmetric pattern with oscillations 5. Option [1] shows this behavior: a peak with oscillatory behavior and sign changes as z varies, which matches the physical situation of observing the y-component of field from an anticlockwise current loop. Therefore, B_y at P_1 = 0 [options 2 and 4 are incorrect because they show symmetric or zero field at origin] B_y has opposite directions for +z and -z axes [confirming option 1]
