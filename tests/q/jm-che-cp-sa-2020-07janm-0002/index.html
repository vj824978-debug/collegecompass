If the following reaction sequence, starting material is aniline with para-methyl group (4-methylaniline). First step: Ac2O/AcOH converts it to compound A (N-acetyl-4-methylaniline). Second step: Br2/AcOH converts A to compound B. What is the major product B?

  1. (1) N-acetyl compound with Br at ortho position relative to acetyl group and CH3 at para position
  2. (2) N-acetyl compound with both COCH3 and Br substituents on the benzene ring
  3. (3) N-acetyl compound with Br at ortho position relative to CH3 group (which becomes CH2Br)
  4. (4) N-acetyl compound with Br at meta position relative to acetyl group and CH3 at para position

Answer

Correct answer: Option 4

Solution

Starting with 4-methylaniline (p-toluidine): Step 1: Ac2O/AcOH → Compound A The amino group (-NH2) undergoes acetylation to form N-acetyl-4-methylaniline (A), with structure: CH3-C6H4-NH-CO-CH3 Step 2: Br2/AcOH → Compound B The acetamide group (-NHCOCH3) is a strong ortho/para-directing group (deactivating but directing) due to the NH being part of the amide. The methyl group (-CH3) is also an ortho/para-directing group (activating). Both groups direct incoming electrophiles to ortho and para positions. However, the acetamide group's directing effect is dominant. The bromine will preferentially enter at the ortho position relative to the acetamide group. Given that there is already a methyl group at the para position relative to the acetamide, bromination occurs at the ortho position(s) to the acetamide group (which are meta to the methyl group). The major product B is: N-acetyl-4-methylaniline with bromine at the position ortho to the acetamide group and meta to the methyl group. This corresponds to the structure shown in option (4): NHCOCH3 group with Br at ortho position and CH3 at para position (meta relationship between Br and CH3).
