N2(g) + 3H2(g) ⇌ 3NH3(g). Starting with 20 g of N2 and 5 g of H2, consider the above reaction. The limiting reagent of the reaction and number of moles of NH3 formed respectively are:

  1. (1) H2, 1.42 moles
  2. (2) H2, 0.71 moles
  3. (3) N2, 1.42 moles
  4. (4) N2, 0.71 moles

Answer

Correct answer: Option 3

Solution

Calculate moles: n(N2) = 20/28 = 5/7 mol, n(H2) = 5/2 = 2.5 mol. Stoichiometric ratio: N2:H2 = 1:3. For N2: 5/7 requires (5/7)×3 = 15/7 H2. For H2: 2.5 requires 2.5/3 = 5/6 N2. Since 5/7 > 5/6, H2 is in excess and N2 is limiting. From stoichiometry: N2 + 3H2 → 2NH3, so n(NH3) = 2 × n(N2) = 2 × (20/28) = 40/28 = 1.42 moles.
