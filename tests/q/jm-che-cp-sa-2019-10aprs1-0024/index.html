The major product of the following reaction is: CH3-C(CH3)(H Br)-CH(CH3) + CH3OH →

  1. (1) CH3-C(CH3)-CH2CH3 with OCH3 group
  2. (2) CH3-C(CH3)=CH-CH3 with H group
  3. (3) CH3-C(CH3)-CH(CH3) with H OCH3 groups
  4. (4) CH3-C(CH3)=CH CH3

Answer

Correct answer: Option 1

Solution

It is SN1-Reaction Starting material: CH3-C(CH3)(H Br)-CH(CH3) Step 1: Loss of Br- and formation of carbocation R.D.S. → -Br- forms carbocation: CH3-C^+(CH3)-CH(CH3) Step 2: The carbocation is stabilized by resonance with the H atom adjacent to it, forming a more stable secondary/tertiary carbocation intermediate. Step 3: Nucleophilic attack by CH3OH (solvent) The methanol attacks the carbocation at the central carbon where the positive charge is localized. Step 4: Deprotonation The resulting oxonium intermediate CH3-C(CH3)-CH(CH3)-O^+H-CH3 loses a proton (H-) to form the final product. Step 5: Final Product CH3-C(CH3)-CH2-CH3 with OCH3 substituent, giving: CH3-C(CH3)(OCH3)-CH2CH3 This SN1 mechanism proceeds through a carbocation intermediate. The methoxide/methanol nucleophile attacks to form an ether product. The methoxy group (-OCH3) replaces the bromine atom at the carbon bearing the positive charge, giving option (1) as the major product.
