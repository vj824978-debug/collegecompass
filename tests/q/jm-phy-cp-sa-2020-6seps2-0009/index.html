Two identical electric point dipoles have dipole moments vector p_1 = pi and vector p_2 = -pi and are held on the x axis at distance 'a' from each other. When released, they move along the x-axis with the direction of their dipole moments remaining unchanged. If the mass of each dipole is 'm', their speed when they are infinitely far apart is:

  1. (1) p/a * sqrt(1/(pi*epsilon_0*ma))
  2. (2) p/a * sqrt(3/(2*pi*epsilon_0*ma))
  3. (3) p/a * sqrt(1/(2*pi*epsilon_0*ma))
  4. (4) p/a * sqrt(2/(pi*epsilon_0*ma))

Answer

Correct answer: Option 3

Solution

Using energy conservation: KE_i + PE_i = KE_f + PE_f vector p_1 = pi (pointing right), vector p_2 = -pi (pointing left) Initial configuration: dipoles separated by distance a O + (2KP/a^3) × P = (1/2)*m*v^2 × 2 + 0 where KP = (1/(4*pi*epsilon_0))*p_1*p_2/r^3 interaction The interaction energy between two dipoles on axis: U = (1/(4*pi*epsilon_0)) * (2*p^2/a^3) This potential energy converts to kinetic energy as they move apart: (1/(4*pi*epsilon_0)) * (2*p^2/a^3) = (1/2)*m*v^2 × 2 (1/(4*pi*epsilon_0)) * (2*p^2/a^3) = m*v^2 v^2 = (2*p^2)/(4*pi*epsilon_0*a^3*m) v = sqrt((2*p^2)/(4*pi*epsilon_0*a^3*m)) v = p/a * sqrt(1/(2*pi*epsilon_0*ma))
