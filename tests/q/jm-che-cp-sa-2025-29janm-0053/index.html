The molar conductivity of a weak electrolyte when plotted against the square root of its concentration, which of the following is expected to be observed?

  1. (1) A small decrease in molar conductivity is observed at infinite dilution.
  2. (2) A small increase in molar conductivity is observed at infinite dilution.
  3. (3) Molar conductivity increases sharply with increase in concentration.
  4. (4) Molar conductivity decreases sharply with increase in concentration.

Answer

Correct answer: Option 4

Solution

For a weak electrolyte, when molar conductivity is plotted against the square root of concentration (sqrt(C)), the following behavior is observed: At infinite dilution (sqrt(C) → 0), the molar conductivity approaches a maximum value (Λ₀). As concentration increases (sqrt(C) increases), the degree of dissociation (α) of the weak electrolyte decreases due to the common ion effect and ionic strength effects. Since molar conductivity is given by: Λ = κ × (1000/C), where κ is the specific conductivity. For weak electrolytes, as concentration increases: - The degree of dissociation decreases - The number of ions available for conduction decreases - Therefore, molar conductivity decreases sharply with increase in concentration The graph shows a sharp decrease in molar conductivity as sqrt(C) increases from 0 onwards, which is characteristic of weak electrolytes. This is because the dissociation equilibrium shifts towards the undissociated form as concentration increases (Le Chatelier's principle). Therefore, option (4) is correct: Molar conductivity decreases sharply with increase in concentration.
