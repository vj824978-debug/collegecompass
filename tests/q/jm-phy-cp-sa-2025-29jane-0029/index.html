In an experiment with photoelectric effect, the stopping potential (1) increases with increase in the wavelength of the incident light (2) increases with increase in the intensity of the incident light (3) is (1/e) times the maximum kinetic energy of the emitted photoelectrons (4) decreases with increase in the intensity of the incident light

  1. (1) increases with increase in the wavelength of the incident light
  2. (2) increases with increase in the intensity of the incident light
  3. (3) is (1/e) times the maximum kinetic energy of the emitted photoelectrons
  4. (4) decreases with increase in the intensity of the incident light

Answer

Correct answer: Option 3

Solution

From Einstein's photoelectric equation: hC/lambda = W + eV_s Also, hC/lambda = W + (K_max) Therefore: eV_s = K_max V_s = K_max/e This shows that the stopping potential is (1/e) times the maximum kinetic energy of the emitted photoelectrons. Let's verify other options: (1) False: Stopping potential depends on frequency (hC/lambda), not wavelength. As wavelength increases, frequency decreases, so stopping potential decreases. (2) False: Stopping potential is independent of intensity. Intensity only affects the number of photoelectrons emitted, not the energy of individual electrons. (4) False: Stopping potential does not depend on intensity. (3) True: V_s = K_max/e, which means V_s is (1/e) times the maximum kinetic energy.
