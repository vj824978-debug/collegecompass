Consider the following reaction: A dibrominated alkane with structure shown reacts with 2NaNH2 to form X. Then X undergoes reaction with (i) NaNH2 and (ii) a brominated compound to form Y. What is the structure of product Y?

  1. (1) 2-methylhex-2-yne
  2. (2) 5-methylhex-2-yne
  3. (3) 2-methylhex-3-yne
  4. (4) Isopropylbut-1-yne

Answer

Correct answer: Option 3

Solution

The starting material is a dibrominated alkane with two bromine atoms on adjacent carbons (geminal dibromide or vicinal dibromide based on the structure shown). Step 1: Reaction with 2NaNH2 When the dibrominated alkane reacts with 2 equiv of NaNH2, this is a double elimination reaction. Each NaNH2 removes a HBr molecule, leading to the formation of a triple bond (alkyne). The product X is an alkyne. From the structure shown, the dibromide has the formula: CH3-CH2-CH2-CH(Br)-CH(Br)-H After double elimination with 2NaNH2: CH3-CH2-CH2-C≡C-H (X is a terminal alkyne) Step 2: Reaction of X with NaNH2 The terminal alkyne X reacts with NaNH2 to form the acetylide anion (alkynyl anion): CH3-CH2-CH2-C≡C-Na+ (an alkynyl carbanion) Step 3: SN2 reaction with alkyl bromide The acetylide anion then undergoes SN2 reaction with an alkyl bromide (shown as a brominated compound in step ii). Based on the answer being 2-methylhex-3-yne, the alkyl bromide must be (CH3)2CH-CH2-Br (isopropylmethyl bromide). The SN2 coupling between the alkynyl carbanion and the alkyl bromide gives: CH3-CH2-CH2-C≡C-CH2-CH(CH3)2 Counting the carbons and numbering from the end nearest the triple bond: Position 1,2: triple bond (hex-3-yne) With a methyl group at position 2 Therefore, the product Y is 2-methylhex-3-yne.
