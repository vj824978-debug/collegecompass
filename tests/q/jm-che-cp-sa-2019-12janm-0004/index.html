What is the work function of the metal if the light of wavelength 4000Å generates photoelectrons of velocity 6 × 10^5 ms^(-1) from it? (Mass of electron = 9 × 10^(-31) kg; Planck's constant = 6.626 × 10^(-34) Js; Velocity of light = 3 × 10^8 ms^(-1); Charge of electron = 1.6 × 10^(-19) eV^(-1))

  1. (1) 4.0 eV
  2. (2) 0.9 eV
  3. (3) 2.1 eV
  4. (4) 3.1 eV

Answer

Correct answer: Option 3

Solution

Using the photoelectric effect equation: phi = hv - (1/2)mv^2 where phi is work function, h is Planck's constant, v is frequency, m is mass of electron, and v is velocity of photoelectrons. First, calculate hv: hv = (6.626 × 10^(-34) × 3 × 10^8)/(4000 × 10^(-10)) hv = 3.35 × 10^(-19) J Next, calculate kinetic energy: KE = (1/2)mv^2 = (1/2) × 9 × 10^(-31) × (6 × 10^5)^2 KE = (1/2) × 9 × 10^(-31) × 36 × 10^10 KE = 1.62 × 10^(-19) J Therefore: phi = 3.35 × 10^(-19) - 1.62 × 10^(-19) phi = 1.73 × 10^(-19) J Converting to eV: phi ≈ 2.1 eV
