One mole of the octahedral complex compound Co(NH3)5Cl3 gives 3 moles of ions on dissolution in water. One mole of the same complex reacts with excess of AgNO3 solution to yield two moles of AgCl(s). The structure of the complex is:

  1. (1) [Co(NH3)5Cl]Cl2
  2. (2) [Co(NH3)4Cl].Cl2.NH3
  3. (3) [Co(NH3)4Cl2]Cl.NH3
  4. (4) Co(NH3)5Cl3.2NH3

Answer

Correct answer: Option 1

Solution

[Co(NH3)5Cl]Cl2 → [Co(NH3)5Cl]^(2+)(aq.) + 2Cl^-(aq.) 3 ions in water [Co(NH3)5Cl]Cl2(aq.) + 2AgNO3(aq.) → [Co(NH3)5Cl](NO3)2(aq.) + 2AgCl(s) Explanation: For option (1) [Co(NH3)5Cl]Cl2: - Upon dissolution: produces 1 complex cation [Co(NH3)5Cl]^(2+) + 2 chloride ions Cl^- = 3 ions total ✓ - With excess AgNO3: only the 2 chloride ions outside the coordination sphere precipitate as AgCl = 2 moles of AgCl ✓ - The Cl inside the coordination sphere is not precipitated as it is bonded to Co Checking other options: Option (2): [Co(NH3)4Cl].Cl2.NH3 would give 4 ions (1 complex + 2Cl^- + NH3 is not an ion) Option (3): [Co(NH3)4Cl2]Cl.NH3 would give 2 ions (1 complex + 1Cl^-) and only 1 mole AgCl Option (4): Co(NH3)5Cl3.2NH3 gives non-ionic complex structure, would not give ions on dissolution Therefore, structure (1) [Co(NH3)5Cl]Cl2 satisfies both conditions.
