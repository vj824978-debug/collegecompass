The major product in the following reaction is ? A purine base (adenine with NH2 group and imidazole ring fused to pyrimidine) reacts with CH3I in base

  1. (1) Purine with NH2 group and CH3 attached to the imidazole nitrogen (N-methylated at imidazole)
  2. (2) Purine with NH2 group and CH3 attached to pyrimidine nitrogen forming a positive charge on the pyrimidine ring (N-methylated at pyrimidine with quaternary N+)
  3. (3) Purine with NHCH3 group (secondary amine methylation) on the exocyclic position and H on imidazole nitrogen
  4. (4) Purine with NH2 group and CH3 attached to pyrimidine nitrogen forming a positive charge (N-methylated at pyrimidine with quaternary N+ adjacent to imidazole)

Answer

Correct answer: Option 1

Solution

The reaction involves methylation of adenine (a purine base) with CH3I in basic conditions. Adenine structure: A bicyclic purine with an exocyclic amino group (NH2), and two nitrogen atoms in the imidazole ring (one being NH, one being a quaternary N) and three nitrogen atoms in the pyrimidine ring. In basic conditions with CH3I (methyl iodide): - The base (OH- or similar) deprotonates the most acidic nitrogen - The imidazole NH is acidic and readily deprotonated - The deprotonated nitrogen (N-) acts as a nucleophile - This N- attacks the electrophilic carbon of CH3I (Sn2 mechanism) - The resulting product is N-methylation at the imidazole ring nitrogen The methylation occurs at the imidazole ring nitrogen (the NH position) rather than: - The exocyclic NH2 (less likely to be deprotonated in basic conditions, and methylation here would give secondary amine) - The pyrimidine nitrogens (less acidic under these conditions) Therefore, the major product is adenine with a methyl group attached to the imidazole nitrogen, giving 9-methyladenine or N9-methyladenine (option 1). The solution diagram shows: Starting material (adenine with NH on imidazole) + CH3I under base conditions → Product with CH3 attached to the imidazole nitrogen (N-methylated adenine).
