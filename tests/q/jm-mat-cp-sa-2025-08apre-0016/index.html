Let a be the length of a side of a square OABC with O being the origin, its side OA makes an acute angle α with the positive x-axis and the equations of its diagonals are (sqrt(3)+1)x + (sqrt(3)-1)y = 0 and (sqrt(3)-1)x - (sqrt(3)+1)y + 8*sqrt(3) = 0. Then a^2 is equal to

  1. (1) 24
  2. (2) 32
  3. (3) 16
  4. (4) 48

Answer

Correct answer: Option 4

Solution

Find intersection point P of diagonals: (sqrt(3)+1)x + (sqrt(3)-1)y = 0 and (sqrt(3)-1)x - (sqrt(3)+1)y + 8*sqrt(3) = 0. Solving gives P(3-sqrt(3), 3+sqrt(3)). Since P is the center of square OABC, and OA = a, we have OA^2 + AB^2 = OP^2 + AP^2. Using 2a^2 = 4[(3-sqrt(3))^2 + (3+sqrt(3))^2] = 4[12-6*sqrt(3)+12+6*sqrt(3)] = 4×24 = 96, we get a^2 = 48.
