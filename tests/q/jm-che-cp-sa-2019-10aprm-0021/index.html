The difference between ΔH and ΔU (ΔH − ΔU), when the combustion of one mole of heptane (l) is carried out at a temperature T, is equal to:

  1. (1) 3 RT
  2. (2) 4 RT
  3. (3) −3 RT
  4. (4) −4 RT

Answer

Correct answer: Option 4

Solution

The combustion reaction of heptane is: C_7H_16(l) + O_2(g) → 7CO_2(g) + 8H_2O(l) The relationship between ΔH and ΔU is: ΔH = ΔU + Δn_g RT where Δn_g is the change in number of moles of gas. From the reaction: Δn_g = (moles of gaseous products) − (moles of gaseous reactants) Δn_g = 7 − 1 = 6 Wait, let me recalculate. Looking at the products and reactants: - Reactants: 1 mole of O_2(g) = 1 mole of gas - Products: 7 moles of CO_2(g) = 7 moles of gas (water is liquid) Δn_g = 7 − 1 = 6 However, the solution shows: ΔH − ΔU = (7 − 11)RT = −4RT This suggests Δn_g = −4 Let me reconsider: The total moles of gas on the product side minus reactant side: Products: 7CO_2(g) = 7 moles of gas Reactants: 1O_2(g) = 1 mole, but we need to check the stoichiometry. Actually for complete combustion of C_7H_16: C_7H_16 + 11O_2 → 7CO_2 + 8H_2O So: Δn_g = 7 − 11 = −4 Therefore: ΔH − ΔU = Δn_g RT = (−4)RT = −4RT
