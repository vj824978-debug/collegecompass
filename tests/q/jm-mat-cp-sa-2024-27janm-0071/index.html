If ∫[0 to 1] 1/(sqrt(3+x) + sqrt(1+x)) dx = a + b√2 + c√3, where a, b, c are rational numbers, then 2a + 3b - 4c is equal to

  1. (1) 10
  2. (2) 8
  3. (3) 4
  4. (4) 7

Answer

Correct answer: Option 2

Solution

Rationalize by multiplying and dividing by sqrt(3+x) - sqrt(1+x). Split into two integrals: (1/2)∫[sqrt(3+x) - sqrt(1+x)]dx. Integrate: (1/2)[2/3(3+x)^(3/2) - 2/3(1+x)^(3/2)] from 0 to 1. Evaluate at limits: (1/2)[2/3(4^(3/2) - 3^(3/2)) - 2/3(2^(3/2) - 1)] = (1/3)[8 - 2√2 - 3√3]. Simplify to get: 3 - (2√2)/3 - √3. Compare with a + b√2 + c√3 to get a = 3, b = -2/3, c = -1. Therefore 2a + 3b - 4c = 6 - 2 + 4 = 8.
