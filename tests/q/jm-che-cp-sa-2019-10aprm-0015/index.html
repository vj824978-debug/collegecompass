The major product 'Y' in the following reactions is:

  1. (1) HO with a structure containing methyl groups
  2. (2) Br with a structure containing a branched chain
  3. (3) Br with a structure containing methyl groups
  4. (4) Br with a structure containing a longer chain

Answer

Correct answer: Option 2

Solution

The starting material is a chloroalkene with the structure: a carbon chain with Cl attached to one carbon and a double bond. The reaction sequence involves: 1. EtONa (Ethoxide) with Heat: This is a base-catalyzed elimination reaction (E2 mechanism). The ethoxide acts as a strong base, removing a hydrogen from a carbon adjacent to the one bearing Cl, while simultaneously the Cl leaves as a leaving group. This produces an alkene (conjugated or substituted alkene depending on structure). 2. HBr addition: The resulting alkene from step 1 undergoes addition of HBr. According to Markovnikov's rule, the H adds to the carbon of the double bond that already has more hydrogen atoms, and Br adds to the carbon with fewer hydrogen atoms. This produces the more stable carbocation intermediate leading to the major product. In this case, following the mechanism shown in the solution section: - The chloroalkene undergoes E2 elimination with EtONa to form an alkene - The alkene then undergoes Markovnikov's addition of HBr - The Br adds to the more substituted carbon of the alkene - This produces a secondary or tertiary alkyl bromide as the major product The major product Y is the brominated compound shown in option (2), which shows the Br atom added to the more substituted carbon of the alkene formed after elimination.
