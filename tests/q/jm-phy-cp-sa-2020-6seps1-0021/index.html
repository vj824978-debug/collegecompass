Two bodies of the same mass are moving with the same speed, but in different directions in a plane. They have a completely inelastic collision and move together thereafter with a final speed which is half of their initial speed. The angle between the initial velocities of the two bodies (in degree) is ______.

Answer

Correct answer: 120

Solution

Let the mass of each body be m and initial speed be v_0. Both bodies move with speed v_0 in different directions making angle theta between them. After completely inelastic collision, they move together with final speed v_0/2. Applying conservation of momentum: Let the two initial velocity vectors be at angle theta from each other. The magnitude of resultant momentum before collision: |p_initial|^2 = (m*v_0)^2 + (m*v_0)^2 + 2(m*v_0)(m*v_0)cos(theta) |p_initial|^2 = m^2*v_0^2(2 + 2cos(theta)) |p_initial| = m*v_0*sqrt(2 + 2cos(theta)) After collision, both bodies move together with mass 2m and velocity v_0/2: |p_final| = 2m * (v_0/2) = m*v_0 By conservation of momentum: m*v_0*sqrt(2 + 2cos(theta)) = m*v_0 sqrt(2 + 2cos(theta)) = 1 2 + 2cos(theta) = 1 2cos(theta) = -1 cos(theta) = -1/2 theta = 120 degrees Alternatively, using component method: Momentum conservation along x-axis: 2m*v_0*cos(theta/2)*cos(theta/2) = 2m*(v_0/2) Simplifying: cos(theta) = 1/2 gives theta = 60 degrees for each direction from x-axis. Angle between them = 2 * 60 = 120 degrees
