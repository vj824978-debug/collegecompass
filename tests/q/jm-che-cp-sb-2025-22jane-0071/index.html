An Acyclic compound with molecular formula C6H6, which gives only one monobromo derivative and takes up four moles of hydrogen per mole for complete hydrogenation has ______ π electrons.

Answer

Correct answer: 8

Solution

Given information: - Molecular formula: C6H6 - Only one monobromo derivative - Takes up 4 moles of H2 for complete hydrogenation - Acyclic compound Step 1: Determine the degree of unsaturation Using formula: DBE = (2C + 2 - H)/2 = (2(6) + 2 - 6)/2 = (12 + 2 - 6)/2 = 8/2 = 4 This means 4 degrees of unsaturation (combinations of double bonds and rings). Step 2: Analyze the hydrogenation data The compound takes up 4 moles of H2, which means it has 4 double bonds (or triple bonds equivalent). Since it's acyclic and takes 4 H2, it must have 4 double bonds. Step 3: Structure determination With C6H6 formula, acyclic, 4 double bonds, and only ONE monobromo derivative: This indicates a highly symmetric structure. The structure is: CH2=CH-CH=CH-CH=CH2 (1,3,5-hexatriene) But this would give 3 monobrominated derivatives. The correct structure is cyclopropene with extended conjugation or more likely: The structure showing only one monobromo derivative with 4 double bonds in C6H6 is Dewar benzene (a valence isomer of benzene) or a symmetric conjugated system. Actually, the solution shown indicates a cyclopropene derivative: cyclopropene with substituents showing N. of π e- = 6 initially. For the C6H6 acyclic compound with 4 H2 uptake and one monobromo derivative: The structure has 2 triple bonds or equivalent unsaturation. Step 4: Count π electrons For complete hydrogenation taking 4 H2: - 4 double bonds = 8 π electrons Therefore, the compound has 8 π electrons.
