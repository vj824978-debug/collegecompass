A hollow, right circular cone of base radius R and height h, with its tip at the origin is rotating about the Z-axis with an angular velocity ω, as shown in the figure. The cone carries a total charge Q uniformly distributed on its curved surface. The magnitude of magnetic field at a point (0, 0, z), where z ≫ R and z ≫ h, is (nμ₀QR²ω)/(4π z³). The value of n is:

Numerical (NAT) · Marks: +4 / 0.0

Answer

Correct answer: 0.5

Solution

Solution: For a point on the axis at a distance z ≫ R and z ≫ h, the rotating cone can be considered as a magnetic dipole. The magnetic field on the axis of a dipole is given by: B = (μ₀/4π) × (2M/z³) where M is the magnetic dipole moment of the cone. To find M, consider an elemental ring on the cone at a vertical distance y from the origin, with vertical thickness dy. The radius of this ring is r = (R/h)y and its slant length is dl = (√(R² + h²)/h)dy. The surface charge density of the cone is σ = Q/(πR√(R² + h²)) The charge on the elemental ring is: dq = σ(2πrdt) = (Q/(πR√(R² + h²))) × (2πR/h × y × √(R² + h²)/h)dy = (2Q/h²)ydy The current due to the rotation of this ring is: dI = dq/T = (dq × ω)/(2π) = (Qω/πh²)ydy The magnetic moment of this elemental ring is: dM = dI(πr²) = (Qω/πh²)ydy × π(R/h)²y² = (QωR²/h⁴)y³dy Integrating from y = 0 to y = h, the total magnetic moment is: M = ∫₀ʰ (QωR²/h⁴)y³dy = (QωR²/h⁴)[y⁴/4]₀ʰ = QωR²/4 Alternatively, using the gyromagnetic ratio for a uniform charge distribution, M/L_ang = Q/(2m). The moment of inertia of a hollow cone is I = (1/2)mR², so the angular momentum is L_ang = (1/2)mR²ω. Thus, M = (Q/(2m)) × ((1/2)mR²ω) = (1/4)QR²ω. Substituting M into the magnetic field formula: B = (μ₀/4π) × (2/z³) × ((1/4)QR²ω) = (μ₀/4π) × (QR²ω/2z³) Comparing this with the given expression B = (nμ₀/4π) × (QR³ω/z³), we get: n = 1/2 = 0.5 Answer: 0.5
