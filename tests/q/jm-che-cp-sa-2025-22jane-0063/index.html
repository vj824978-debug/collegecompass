The most stable carbocation from the following is:

  1. (1) A benzene ring with para-OMe substituent and a +CH2 group attached to the ring
  2. (2) A benzene ring with meta-OMe substituent and a +CH2 group attached to the ring
  3. (3) A benzene ring with para-H3C substituent and a +CH2 group attached to the ring
  4. (4) A benzene ring with no substituent and a +CH2 group attached to the ring

Answer

Correct answer: Option 1

Solution

To determine the most stable carbocation, we need to consider electron-donating and electron-withdrawing effects of substituents on the benzene ring. The stability order of the given carbocations is: Structure (1): para-OMe with +CH2 > Structure (3): para-CH3 with +CH2 > Structure (4): unsubstituted +CH2 > Structure (2): meta-OMe with +CH2 Reasoning: 1. The +CH2 carbocation is stabilized by electron-donating groups attached to the benzene ring through resonance and inductive effects. 2. The -OMe (methoxy) group is a strong electron-donating group due to its +M effect (mesomeric effect), where the lone pair on oxygen can donate electron density to the benzene ring through resonance. 3. In Structure (1), the -OMe group is at the para position relative to the +CH2 group. This allows maximum resonance stabilization of the positive charge through the π-electron system of the benzene ring. 4. In Structure (2), the -OMe group is at the meta position. Meta-directing groups cannot effectively stabilize the carbocation through resonance, making this less stable. 5. In Structure (3), the -CH3 group is electron-donating but only through the inductive effect, which is weaker than the resonance effect of -OMe. 6. Structure (4) has no electron-donating substituent, so it is the least stabilized among options with substituents. Due to the +M effect of -OMe at the para position, Structure (1) is the most stable carbocation among these four options.
