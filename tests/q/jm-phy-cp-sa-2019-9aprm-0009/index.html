A very long solenoid of radius R is carrying current I(t) = kte^(-at) (k > 0), as a function of time (t ≥ 0). Counter clockwise current is taken to be positive. A circular conducting coil of radius 2R is placed in the equatorial plane of the solenoid and concentric with the solenoid. The current induced in the outer coil is correctly depicted, as a function of time, by:

  1. (1) Graph (1): Current starts at maximum at t=0 and exponentially decays to zero
  2. (2) Graph (2): Current starts at zero at t=0, rises to maximum, then decays to zero
  3. (3) Graph (3): Current oscillates, starting negative at t=0, then becomes positive
  4. (4) Graph (4): Current starts at zero at t=0, becomes negative, then returns toward zero

Answer

Correct answer: Option 2

Solution

phi = (mu_0 * n * K * t * e^(-at)) * 4*pi*R^2 e = -d(phi)/dt = -ce^(-at)(1 - at) i_induced = -ce^(-at)(1 - at)/R At t = 0: i_ind => +V_c This means at t = 0, the induced current is positive (counter-clockwise). The induced EMF is: e = -ce^(-at)(1 - at) For small t: (1 - at) is positive and close to 1, so e is negative initially. As t increases, the term (1 - at) decreases and eventually becomes negative. At t = 1/a: the term (1 - at) = 0, so e = 0. For t > 1/a: (1 - at) becomes negative, making e positive. The induced current i_induced = -ce^(-at)(1 - at)/R At t = 0: i_induced = -c(1)/R = positive value (since c is negative from the EMF expression) The current: - Starts at a positive value (maximum) at t = 0 - Crosses zero at t = 1/a - Becomes negative for t > 1/a - Returns toward zero as t → infinity due to exponential decay This matches graph (2) where the current starts positive at t = 0, rises to a maximum, then decays back through zero and becomes negative.
