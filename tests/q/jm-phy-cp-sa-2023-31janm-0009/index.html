Which of the following correctly represents the variation of electric potential(V) of a charged spherical conductor of radius (R) with radial distance (r) from the center?

  1. (1) Graph showing potential increasing to peak at R then decreasing
  2. (2) Graph showing constant potential up to R then decreasing
  3. (3) Graph showing potential starting at R with sharp peak then decreasing
  4. (4) Graph showing potential decreasing from origin

Answer

Correct answer: Option 2

Solution

For a charged spherical conductor: Inside the conductor (r < R): - Electric field E = 0 (property of conductors in electrostatic equilibrium) - Since E = -dV/dr, and E = 0, we have dV/dr = 0 - Therefore, V = constant (inside the conductor) Outside the conductor (r > R): - The conductor behaves like a point charge - Electric potential V = kQ/r, where Q is the total charge - As r increases, V decreases (proportional to 1/r) - V decreases monotonically and approaches zero as r → infinity At the boundary (r = R): - The potential is continuous - V(r < R) = V(r = R) = kQ/R (constant inside) Therefore, the correct graph shows: - Constant potential for 0 ≤ r ≤ R - Decreasing potential (following 1/r curve) for r > R - A smooth transition at r = R with no discontinuity This matches option (2).
