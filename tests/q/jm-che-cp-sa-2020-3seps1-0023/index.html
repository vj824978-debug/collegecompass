The total number of monohalo­genated organic products in the following (including stereoisomers) reaction is ______. (Simplest optically active alkene)

Answer

Correct answer: 8

Solution

Step 1: Identify the starting material - a simplest optically active alkene with the structure showing a double bond and a methyl group. Step 2: First reaction with H2/Ni/A (hydrogenation): The alkene is reduced to an alkane (mc structure): a saturated hydrocarbon with a chiral center (marked as *), creating one stereoisomer. Step 3: Second reaction with Cl2/hv (free radical chlorination): The Cl2 with light (hv) causes free radical chlorination. The saturated alkane undergoes monochlorination (replacing one H with Cl). Step 4: Count monochlorinated products: The molecule has a chiral center. Free radical chlorination can occur at different positions: - Position at the chiral carbon: creates 2 stereoisomers (R and S configurations with Cl at this position) - Position at adjacent methyl groups: creates different regioisomers - Position at the other alkyl chain: creates another regioisomer Step 5: Analyze all possible monochlorination positions: For the saturated hydrocarbon structure obtained after hydrogenation, monochlorination at different positions gives: 1. Cl at the chiral center (alpha position): 2 stereoisomers (due to existing chiral center) 2. Cl at one of the adjacent methyl carbons: 1 product 3. Cl at another position on the chain: 1 product 4. Cl at terminal positions of different alkyl groups: multiple products Considering the structure and all possible monochlorination positions along with stereoisomerism at the chiral center: - 2 isomers from chlorination at chiral center position - 2 isomers from chlorination at one adjacent position - 2 isomers from chlorination at another position - 2 isomers from chlorination at yet another position Total = 8 monohaloganated organic products (including stereoisomers)
