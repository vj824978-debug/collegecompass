Consider the following equation: 2SO₂(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2SO₃(g), ΔH = -190 kJ. The number of factors which will increase the yield of SO₃ at equilibrium from the following is ___

  1. (1) Increasing temperature
  2. (2) Increasing pressure
  3. (3) Adding more SO₂
  4. (4) Adding more O₂

Answer

Correct answer: Option 3

Solution

2SO₂(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2SO₃(g), ΔH = -190 kJ It is an exothermic reaction Analyzing each factor: A. Increasing temperature: Since the reaction is exothermic (ΔH < 0), increasing temperature shifts equilibrium to the left (towards reactants). This DECREASES the yield of SO₃. ✗ B. Increasing pressure: The reaction has 3 moles of gas on the left (2 + 1) and 2 moles on the right. Increasing pressure shifts equilibrium towards the side with fewer moles (right side, towards products). This INCREASES the yield of SO₃. ✓ C. Adding more SO₂: SO₂ is a reactant. Adding more SO₂ shifts equilibrium to the right (towards products) by Le Chatelier's principle. This INCREASES the yield of SO₃. ✓ D. Adding more O₂: O₂ is a reactant. Adding more O₂ shifts equilibrium to the right (towards products) by Le Chatelier's principle. This INCREASES the yield of SO₃. ✓ E. Addition of catalyst: A catalyst speeds up the reaction but does not change the equilibrium position or yield at equilibrium. ✗ Therefore, factors B, C, and D will increase the amount of SO₃. The number of factors = 3
