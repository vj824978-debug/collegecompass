Find out the major product from the following reaction. Starting material: A cyclohexane ring with a methyl group and an OH group attached to adjacent carbons, with the OH carbon also having a methyl substituent. Reagent: H2SO4 (Concentrated) with heat (Delta) Find the major product.

  1. (1) Cyclohexene with a methyl group and a double bond, with an additional methyl group on the ring
  2. (2) Methylcyclohexene with a methyl substituent on the ring
  3. (3) Cyclohexene with a methyl group attached
  4. (4) Cyclohexane with a methyl group and a double bond in a different position

Answer

Correct answer: Option 1

Solution

The reaction involves dehydration of a secondary alcohol under acidic conditions (H2SO4, heat). Starting material: Cyclohexane ring with OH group and adjacent methyl and geminal methyl substituents Step 1: Protonation of the OH group by H2SO4 The oxygen of the hydroxyl group is protonated, forming R-OH2+, which makes it a better leaving group. Step 2: Formation of carbocation The protonated hydroxyl group leaves as water (H2O), generating a secondary carbocation at the carbon that originally bore the OH group. Step 3: Carbocation rearrangement (1,2-methyl shift) The geminal methyl group adjacent to the carbocation undergoes a 1,2-methyl shift (methyl migration) from the neighboring carbon to the carbocation center. This converts the secondary carbocation into a more stable tertiary carbocation. Step 4: Deprotonation (E1 elimination) A water molecule (or H2SO4) abstracts a proton from a carbon adjacent to the tertiary carbocation, forming a C=C double bond. The major product is a trisubstituted alkene (more stable due to Zaitsev's rule - preferential formation of the most substituted alkene) with the methyl groups positioned to give maximum stability. Answer: Option (1) - the product shown represents the trisubstituted alkene formed after rearrangement and elimination.
