The figure shows the variation of specific heat capacity (C) of a solid as a function of temperature (T). The temperature is increased continuously from 0 to 500 K at a constant rate. Ignoring any volume change, the following statement(s) is (are) correct to a reasonable approximation. [Figure shows a curve starting near origin, rising steeply initially, then leveling off to approach a horizontal asymptote around 400 K. X-axis labeled T (K) from 0 to 500, Y-axis labeled C]

Multiple correct (MSQ) · Marks: +4 / -2.0

  1. (A) (A) the rate at which heat is absorbed in the range 0-100 K varies linearly with temperature T.
  2. (B) (B) heat absorbed in increasing the temperature from 0-100 K is less than the heat required for increasing the temperature from 400–500 K.
  3. (C) (C) there is no change in the rate of heat absorption in range 400–500 K.
  4. (D) (D) the rate of heat absorption increases in the range 200–300 K.

Answer

Correct answer: ABCD

Solution

Option (A) is correct because the graph between (0 – 100 K) appears to be a straight line upto a reasonable approximation. Option (B) is correct because area under the curve in the temperature range (0 – 100 K) is less than in range (400 – 500 K) Option (C) is correct because the graph of C versus T is constant in the temperature range (400 – 500 K) Option (D) is correct because in the temperature range (200 – 300 K) specific heat capacity increases with temperature.
