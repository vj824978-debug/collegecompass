Increasing rate of S_N1 reaction in the following compounds is:

  1. (1) (B) < (A) < (D) < (C)
  2. (2) (A) < (B) < (C) < (D)
  3. (3) (A) < (B) < (D) < (C)
  4. (4) (B) < (A) < (C) < (D)

Answer

Correct answer: Option 4

Solution

Rate of S_N1 reaction ∝ stability of C^+ - I.m. The four compounds are: (A) C6H5-CH(I)(CH3) - primary carbocation with phenyl group (B) MeO-C6H4-CH(I)(CH3) - primary carbocation with electron-donating methoxy group (C) H3C-C6H4-CH(I)(CH3) - primary carbocation with electron-donating methyl group (D) H3CO-C6H4-CH(I)(CH3) - primary carbocation with electron-donating methoxy group All compounds form secondary carbocations (the carbon bearing I is attached to CH3 and the aromatic ring). The stability of carbocations increases with electron-donating groups on the aromatic ring. Electron-donating ability: MeO > CH3 > H For S_N1 reaction, we need to consider the stability of the carbocation intermediate formed: - (B) has MeO (strong electron-donating group) - (D) has MeO (strong electron-donating group) - (C) has CH3 (weak electron-donating group) - (A) has H (no electron-donating group) However, looking at the positions and structures: (B) and (D) both have methoxy groups but (D) appears to have methoxy on the aromatic ring directly. Order of carbocation stability: (A) < (B) < (C) < (D) Wait, reconsidering: The electron-donating groups stabilize the carbocation through resonance. MeO is stronger than CH3, and both are stronger than H. Correct order of S_N1 reaction rate: (B) < (A) < (C) < (D)
