As per Hardy-Schulze formulation, the flocculation values of the following for ferric hydroxide sol are in the order:

  1. (1) AlCl3 > K3[Fe(CN)6] > K2CrO4 > KBr = KNO3
  2. (2) K3[Fe(CN)6] < K2CrO4 < KBr = KNO3 = AlCl3
  3. (3) K3[Fe(CN)6] < K2CrO4 < AlCl3 < KBr < KNO3
  4. (4) K3[Fe(CN)6] > AlCl3 > K2CrO4 > KBr > KNO3

Answer

Correct answer: Option 2

Solution

According to Hardy-Schulze rule, coagulation value alpha = 1/(Coagulation Power). The coagulation power depends on the charge of the counter ion and its polarizing effect. For ferric hydroxide sol (positive colloid), anions are the counter ions. The order of coagulation power follows: Higher charge density and smaller size = higher coagulation power = lower coagulation value (flocculation value). K3[Fe(CN)6] has charge -3 (highest charge), K2CrO4 has charge -2, KBr and KNO3 have charge -1, and AlCl3 provides Al^(3+) cation with charge +3. Since ferric hydroxide is positively charged, the divalent and trivalent anions coagulate more effectively than monovalent ions. The order of flocculation values (coagulation values) is: K3[Fe(CN)6] < K2CrO4 < KBr = KNO3 = AlCl3. The trivalent anion K3[Fe(CN)6] has the highest coagulation power (lowest coagulation value), followed by divalent K2CrO4, then monovalent KBr and KNO3 with equal values, and AlCl3 is also among the least effective.
