In a conductometric titration, small volume of titrant of higher concentration is added stepwise to a larger volume of titrate of much lower concentration, and the conductance is measured after each addition. The limiting ionic conductivity (λ_0) values (in mS·m²·mol⁻¹) for different ions in aqueous solutions are given below: [TABLE: Ions | Ag⁺ | K⁺ | Na⁺ | H⁺ | NO₃⁻ | Cl⁻ | SO₄²⁻ | OH⁻ | CH₃COO⁻ λ_0: 6.2 | 7.4 | 5.0 | 35.0 | 7.2 | 7.0 | 16.0 | 19.9 | 4.1] For different combinations of titrates and titrants given in List-I, the graphs of 'conductance' versus 'volume of titrant' are given in List-II. Match each entry List-I with the appropriate entry in List-II and choose the correct option. List-I: (P) Titrate: KCl, Titrant: AgNO₃ (Q) Titrate: AgNO₃, Titrant: KCl (R) Titrate: NaOH, Titrant: HCl (S) Titrate: NaOH, Titrant: CH₃COOH List-II: (1) [Graph showing conductance vs volume of titrant with minimum point, then increases] (2) [Graph showing conductance vs volume of titrant starting high, decreasing to minimum, then increasing] (3) [Graph showing conductance vs volume of titrant starting at value, decreasing linearly to minimum point] (4) [Graph showing conductance vs volume of titrant starting low, increasing gradually then more steeply] (5) [Graph showing conductance vs volume of titrant starting high, decreasing sharply, then leveling off]

Matrix match · Marks: +3 / -1.0

  1. (A) (A) (P) → (4), (Q) → (3), (R) → (2), (S) → (5)
  2. (B) (B) (P) → (2), (Q) → (4), (R) → (3), (S) → (1)
  3. (C) (C) (P) → (3), (Q) → (4), (R) → (2), (S) → (5)
  4. (D) (D) (P) → (4), (Q) → (3), (R) → (2), (S) → (1)

Answer

Correct answer: C

Solution

(P) → (3) [Graph showing conductance vs volume of titrant with initial high value decreasing to a minimum, then increasing] (Q) → (4) [Graph showing conductance vs volume of titrant with initial lower value increasing throughout] (R) → (2) [Graph showing conductance vs volume of titrant starting high, decreasing to a minimum point, then increasing] (S) → (5) [Graph showing conductance vs volume of titrant starting high and remaining relatively constant/flat]
