Consider the following reactions. PbCl2 + K2CrO4 → A + 2KCl (Hot solution). A + NaOH ⇌ B + Na2CrO4. PbSO4 + 4CH3COONH4 → (NH4)2SO4 + X. In the above reactions, A, B and X are respectively.

  1. (1) Na2[Pb(OH)4], PbCrO4 and (NH4)2[Pb(CH3COO)4]
  2. (2) PbCrO4, Na2[Pb(OH)4] and [Pb(NH3)4]SO4
  3. (3) Na2[Pb(OH)4], PbCrO4 and [Pb(NH3)4]SO4
  4. (4) PbCrO4, Na2[Pb(OH)4] and (NH4)2[Pb(CH3COO)4]

Answer

Correct answer: Option 4

Solution

Reaction 1: PbCl2 + K2CrO4 → PbCrO4 (A) + 2KCl. In hot solution, PbCrO4 is formed. Reaction 2: PbCrO4 + 4NaOH (excess) → Na2[Pb(OH)4] (B) + Na2CrO4. Lead chromate dissolves in excess NaOH forming plumbate ion. Reaction 3: PbSO4 + 4CH3COONH4 → (NH4)2[Pb(CH3COO)4] (X) + (NH4)2SO4. Lead sulfate complexes with acetate ions. Therefore A = PbCrO4, B = Na2[Pb(OH)4], X = (NH4)2[Pb(CH3COO)4].
