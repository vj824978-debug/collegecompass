A solid sphere of radius R carries a charge (Q + q) distributed uniformly over its volume. A very small point like piece of it of mass m gets detached from the bottom of the sphere and falls down vertically under gravity. This piece carries charge q. If it acquires a speed v when it has fallen through a vertical height y (see figure), then : (assume the remaining portion to be spherical).

  1. (1) v^2 = 2y[qQ/(4π ε_0 R (R+y)m) + g]
  2. (2) v^2 = y[qQ/(4π ε_0 R^2 ym) + g]
  3. (3) v^2 = 2y[qQR/(4π ε_0 (R + y)^3 m) + g]
  4. (4) v^2 = y[qR/(4π ε_0 R(R + y)m) + g]

Answer

Correct answer: Option 1

Solution

When the small piece of charge q detaches from the bottom of the sphere and falls through height y, we apply energy conservation. Initial state: piece at rest at bottom of sphere Final state: piece has fallen distance y with speed v The remaining sphere has charge (Q - q) and can be treated as spherical (given assumption). Using energy conservation: Work by gravity + Work by electric force = Change in kinetic energy mgy + W_electric = (1/2)mv^2 The electric potential at distance (R + y) from the center due to the remaining sphere of charge (Q - q) is: V = k(Q - q)/(R + y) = (Q - q)/(4π ε_0(R + y)) Initial potential energy of the piece (at surface, distance R): U_i = qV_i = q × (Q - q)/(4π ε_0 R) Final potential energy of the piece (at distance R + y): U_f = qV_f = q × (Q - q)/(4π ε_0(R + y)) Change in electric potential energy: ΔU_e = U_f - U_i = q(Q - q)/(4π ε_0) × [1/(R + y) - 1/R] = q(Q - q)/(4π ε_0) × [R - (R + y)]/[R(R + y)] = -qQy/(4π ε_0 R(R + y)) + q^2y/(4π ε_0 R(R + y)) For small q compared to Q, neglecting q^2 terms: ΔU_e ≈ -qQy/(4π ε_0 R(R + y)) Energy conservation: mgy - qQy/(4π ε_0 R(R + y)) = (1/2)mv^2 Multiplying by 2: 2mgy - 2qQy/(4π ε_0 R(R + y)) = mv^2 v^2 = 2gy - 2qQy/(4π ε_0 mR(R + y)) v^2 = 2y[g - qQ/(4π ε_0 mR(R + y))] v^2 = 2y[qQ/(4π ε_0 R(R + y)m) + g]
