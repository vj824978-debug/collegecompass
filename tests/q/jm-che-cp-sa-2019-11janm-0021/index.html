The major product of the following reaction is: A compound with OH group at top, SO3H group at bottom, and a benzene ring connecting them, treated with Br2(excess)

  1. (1) A compound with OH group at top, Br groups at three positions (left, right, and bottom), and SO3H group remains at bottom
  2. (2) A compound with OH group at top, Br groups at two positions (left and right, ortho to OH), SO3H group at bottom, and one Br at para position to OH
  3. (3) A compound with OH group at top, Br group ortho to OH, and SO3H group at bottom position
  4. (4) A compound with OH group at top, Br groups at two positions (ortho to OH), SO3H group at bottom, and Br at para position to SO3H

Answer

Correct answer: Option 1

Solution

Due to activating nature of phenol it gets tribrominated (even – COOH and-SO3H can be replaced by IPSO attack). The OH group is a strong activating group and ortho/para directing group. When Br2 in excess is added to the compound containing both OH and SO3H groups, the OH group activates the benzene ring towards electrophilic aromatic substitution. Since Br2 is in excess, all available positions ortho and para to the OH group get brominated. The SO3H group is a deactivating group but the OH group's activation dominates the reactivity. All three positions (two ortho and one para to OH) get brominated, resulting in tribromination. The major product is 2,4,6-tribromophenol where the SO3H group remains attached (or may be replaced during the reaction).
