With increasing biasing voltage of a photodiode, the photocurrent magnitude:

  1. (1) increases initially and saturates finally
  2. (2) increases initially and after attaining certain value, it decreases
  3. (3) increases linearly
  4. (4) remains constant

Answer

Correct answer: Option 1

Solution

I-V characteristic of a photodiode is as follows: [Graph shows mA on y-axis and V on x-axis, with a curve in the reverse bias region showing photocurrent (μA) increasing and then saturating] On increasing the potential difference the current first increases and then attains a saturation. At low reverse bias voltages, the photocurrent increases with increasing bias voltage. As the reverse bias voltage increases further, the drift velocity of the charge carriers increases, but eventually reaches a limit when all the photogenerated carriers are collected before they can recombine. At this point, the photocurrent saturates and remains constant with further increase in reverse bias voltage. Therefore, the photocurrent magnitude increases initially and saturates finally.
