The major product of the following reaction is: Starting material is a benzene ring with CH3, OH, and NO2 groups attached, reacted with conc. HNO3 + conc. H2SO4

  1. (1) Benzene ring with H3C, O2N, and NO2 groups (two NO2 groups ortho to each other, one meta to CH3)
  2. (2) Benzene ring with H3C, OH, NO2, and NO2 groups (OH and two NO2 groups)
  3. (3) Benzene ring with H3C, OH, and NO2 groups (single NO2 addition para to OH)
  4. (4) Benzene ring with H3C, OH, NO2, and NO2 groups (OH ortho, two NO2 groups)

Answer

Correct answer: Option 3

Solution

The starting material is 4-methyl-2-nitrophenol (or similar isomer with CH3, OH, and NO2 on benzene ring). When subjected to nitration with conc. HNO3 and conc. H2SO4: 1) Both OH and CH3 are electron-donating groups (activating groups), while NO2 is an electron-withdrawing group (deactivating group). 2) The OH group is a stronger activating group (ortho/para director) compared to CH3 (also ortho/para director). 3) The NO2 group is a deactivating group and meta-director. 4) Directing effects analysis: - OH directs incoming NO2 to ortho and para positions - CH3 directs incoming NO2 to ortho and para positions - NO2 directs incoming NO2 to meta position 5) In the given starting material structure, the most activated position is the one that is: - Ortho or para to OH (strong activator) - Ortho or para to CH3 (moderate activator) - Meta to existing NO2 (avoids deactivator) 6) The major product forms when the new NO2 group enters at the position ortho to OH (the strongest activating group) and para to CH3, which gives option (3). 7) The solution diagram shows that with more donating groups available, nitration occurs at the position activated by OH, giving the major product with a single additional NO2 group para to the existing substituents, while a minor product forms due to steric crowding when trying to add at alternative positions.
