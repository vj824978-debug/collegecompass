Which of the following compounds will produce a precipitate with AgNO₃?

  1. (1) Br attached to a pyridine ring (nitrogen-containing aromatic ring)
  2. (2) Br attached to a cyclooctatetraene ring (8-membered aromatic ring)
  3. (3) Br attached to a cyclohexane ring (6-membered saturated ring)
  4. (4) Br attached to a benzene ring (6-membered aromatic ring)

Answer

Correct answer: Option 2

Solution

Those alkyl halide which forms stable carbocation gives precipitate with AgNO₃ The key to this problem is understanding that AgNO₃ causes the formation of a carbocation through an SN1 mechanism. A stable carbocation is required for a positive test (precipitate formation). Analysis of each compound: (1) Bromopyridine - The Br is attached to the aromatic pyridine ring. Breaking this C-Br bond would create a carbocation on the aromatic ring, which would disrupt the aromatic system (4π electrons for pyridine). This carbocation is unstable. No precipitate. (2) Bromocy clooctatetraene - The Br is attached to a 7-membered ring carbon. When the C-Br bond breaks, it forms a carbocation that is stabilized by the aromatic ring below it. The cyclooctatetraene ring has 6π electrons (Hückel rule: 4n+2 where n=1), making the resulting cation a stable 6π aromatic system. The cation is therefore aromatic and highly stable. Precipitate forms. (3) Bromocyclohexane - The Br is attached to a saturated, non-aromatic cyclohexane ring. The resulting secondary alkyl carbocation is relatively unstable and not aromatic. Minimal precipitate formation. (4) Bromobenzene - The Br is attached directly to the benzene ring. Breaking this C-Br bond would destroy the aromatic benzene ring and create an unstable carbocation. No precipitate. Answer: [2] - The cyclooctatetraene bromide forms a stable 6π aromatic carbocation, enabling AgNO₃ precipitation.
