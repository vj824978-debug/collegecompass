For a reaction, 4M(s) + nO2(g) → 2M2On(s), the free energy change is plotted as a function of temperature. The temperature below which the oxide is stable could be inferred from the plot as the point at which:

  1. (1) the slope changes from positive to zero
  2. (2) the free energy change shows a change from negative to positive value
  3. (3) the slope changes from negative to positive
  4. (4) the slope changes from positive to negative

Answer

Correct answer: Option 2

Solution

For a reaction to be thermodynamically stable, the free energy change (ΔG) must be negative. When ΔG < 0, the reaction is spontaneous and the product (oxide) is stable. When ΔG > 0, the reaction is non-spontaneous and the oxide is unstable. The temperature below which the oxide is stable is the temperature at which ΔG transitions from negative to positive as temperature increases. Using the Gibbs-Helmholtz equation: ΔG = ΔH - TΔS At low temperatures where ΔH is negative and TΔS is small, ΔG is negative (oxide stable). At high temperatures where TΔS becomes large, ΔG becomes positive (oxide unstable). The boundary between these regions is where ΔG changes from negative to positive value, which occurs at the temperature where ΔG = 0 (the transition point). Therefore, the temperature below which the oxide is stable is identified by the point where the free energy change shows a change from negative to positive value.
