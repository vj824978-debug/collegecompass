The temporary hardness of a water sample is due to compound X. Boiling this sample converts X to compound Y. X and Y, respectively, are:

  1. (1) Mg(HCO3)2 and MgCO3
  2. (2) Ca(HO3)2 and CaO
  3. (3) Ca(HCO3)2 and Ca(OH)2
  4. (4) Mg(HCO3)2 and Mg(OH)2

Answer

Correct answer: Option 4

Solution

Mg(HCO3)2 → Mg(OH)2 + CO2 + H2O (with heat/delta) Temporary hardness in water is caused by bicarbonates of calcium and magnesium. When the water is boiled, these bicarbonates decompose: - The bicarbonate ion (HCO3)- decomposes due to heat - CO2 is released as a gas - Water is released - The metal hydroxide precipitates out, removing the hardness For magnesium bicarbonate Mg(HCO3)2: Upon heating, it converts to Mg(OH)2 (magnesium hydroxide), which is the insoluble compound that causes hardness to be removed. Therefore, X = Mg(HCO3)2 and Y = Mg(OH)2
