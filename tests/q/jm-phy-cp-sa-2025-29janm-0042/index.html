Let u and v be the distances of the object and the image from a lens of focal length f. The correct graphical representation of u and v for a convex lens when |u| > f, is :

  1. (1) A curve showing v approaching negative infinity as u approaches f from the left, with v becoming less negative as u increases
  2. (2) A curve showing v starting from negative values and increasing toward positive values, asymptotically approaching f as u increases
  3. (3) A curve showing v as a decreasing function approaching zero from above as u increases, with discontinuity at u = f
  4. (4) A straight line with positive slope passing through the origin

Answer

Correct answer: Option 2

Solution

For a convex lens, the lens formula is: 1/f = 1/u + 1/v Rearranging for v: 1/v = 1/f - 1/u 1/v = (u - f)/(fu) v = fu/(u - f) Alternatively, we can write this as: (u + f)(v - f) = f^2 Analyzing the behavior of v as a function of u when |u| > f: 1. When u → f^+: v → +∞ (vertical asymptote at u = f) 2. When u → ∞: v → f^+ (horizontal asymptote at v = f) 3. When u > f: v > f (always positive for real images) 4. dv/du = f^2/(u - f)^2 > 0, so v is strictly increasing 5. d^2v/du^2 = -2f^2/(u - f)^3 < 0 for u > f, so the curve is concave down This represents a hyperbolic curve that: - Has a vertical asymptote at u = f - Has a horizontal asymptote at v = f - Is decreasing in rate of increase (concave down) - Passes through regions where both u and v are positive and greater than f Graph (2) correctly shows this hyperbolic behavior with the appropriate asymptotes.
