In a random experiment, a fair die is rolled until two fours are obtained in succession. The probability that the experiment will end in the fifth throw of the die is equal to:

  1. (1) 200/6^5
  2. (2) 225/6^5
  3. (3) 150/6^5
  4. (4) 175/6^5

Answer

Correct answer: Option 4

Solution

The experiment ends on the fifth throw means that we get two consecutive fours for the first time on throws 4 and 5. For the experiment to end on exactly the fifth throw: - Throws 4 and 5 must both be 4 - The sequence up to throw 3 must NOT contain two consecutive 4s - Throw 3 must not be 4 (otherwise throws 3-4 would be consecutive 4s) Possible sequences ending on throw 5: - Throw 1 = not 4, Throw 2 = not 4, Throw 3 = not 4, Throw 4 = 4, Throw 5 = 4: (5)(5)(5)(1)(1) - Throw 1 = not 4, Throw 2 = 4, Throw 3 = not 4, Throw 4 = 4, Throw 5 = 4: (5)(1)(5)(1)(1) - Throw 1 = 4, Throw 2 = not 4, Throw 3 = not 4, Throw 4 = 4, Throw 5 = 4: (1)(5)(5)(1)(1) These correspond to the three patterns: (1)(5)(5)(1)(1) = 1 × 5 × 5 × 1 × 1 = 25 (5)(1)(5)(1)(1) = 5 × 1 × 5 × 1 × 1 = 25 (5)(5)(5)(1)(1) = 5 × 5 × 5 × 1 × 1 = 125 Alternatively, using the formula approach: = (1 × 5 × 5 + 5 × 1 × 5 + 5 × 5 × 5)/(6^5) = (125 + 50)/(6^5) = 175/(6^5) OR = (1/6^2)(5^3/6^3 + 2C1·5^2/6^3) = (1/6^2)(125/6^3 + 2·25/6^3) = (1/6^2)(175/6^3) = 175/(6^5)
