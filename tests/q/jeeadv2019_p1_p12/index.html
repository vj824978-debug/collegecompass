Two identical moving coil galvanometers have 10 Ω resistance and full scale deflection at 2 μA current. One of them is converted into a voltmeter of 100 mV full scale reading and the other into an Ammeter of 1mA full scale current using appropriate resistors. These are then used to measure the voltage and current in the Ohm's law experiment with R = 1000 Ω resistor by using an ideal cell. Which of the following statement(s) is/are correct?

Multiple correct (MSQ) · Marks: +4 / -1.0

  1. (A) The measured value of R will be 978 Ω < R < 982 Ω
  2. (B) The resistance of the Voltmeter will be 100 kΩ
  3. (C) If the ideal cell is replaced by a cell having internal resistance of 5Ω then the measured value of R will be more than 1000 Ω
  4. (D) The resistance of the Ammeter will be 0.02Ω (round off to 2^nd decimal place)

Answer

Correct answer: AD

Solution

Sol. A, D V = Ig(G + RV) G + RV = RV = 5×10⁴ Ω IgG = (1 - Ig)S S = 20 m Ω Rammeter = GS/(G + S) = 20×10⁻³ Ω i = ε/[1000RV/(1000 + RV)] = 5Iε/(5×10³) (RA is small) il = ((1000 + RV)/1000) · ε/1000 Rmeasured = il(1000)/i = 980.4Ω [Circuit diagram showing: top branch with G and RV in series; middle branch with current I splitting into Ig and another path through G and S in parallel; bottom branch with voltmeter V, current i through 1000Ω resistor to ammeter A, with capacitor Ic] [Solution shows galvanometer circuit with shunt resistance, where measured resistance is calculated from the ratio of current through 1000Ω resistor to total current]
