What are the functional groups present in the structure of maltose?

  1. (1) One acetal and one hemiacetal
  2. (2) One ketal and one hemiketal
  3. (3) Two acetals
  4. (4) One acetal and one ketal

Answer

Correct answer: Option 1

Solution

Maltose is a disaccharide composed of two glucose units linked together. Looking at the structure: 1. The left glucose unit forms a complete cyclic acetal ring (six-membered pyranose ring with a C-O-C linkage at the anomeric carbon). 2. The right glucose unit is in equilibrium with its open-chain form and exists as a hemiacetal. The hemiacetal functional group is present at the anomeric carbon (the C=O is in equilibrium with C-OH in the ring form), as shown by the dashed circle in the structure. 3. An acetal is formed when an aldehyde (or ketone) reacts with one alcohol group, resulting in a C bonded to two oxygen atoms (C-O-C-O-C arrangement). 4. A hemiacetal is an intermediate stage where the carbonyl carbon is bonded to one OH group and one OR group (from a ring). In maltose: - The glycosidic bond between the two glucose units creates an acetal at the anomeric carbon of the left glucose unit - The anomeric carbon of the right glucose unit exists as a hemiacetal (it can still exist in equilibrium with the open-chain aldehyde form) Therefore, maltose contains one acetal and one hemiacetal functional group.
