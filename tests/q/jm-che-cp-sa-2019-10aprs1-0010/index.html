The major product of the following reaction is: CH3CHCH2CH2NH2 with OH group on the second carbon, reacted with ethyl formate (1 equiv.) and triethylamine

  1. (1) CH3-CH(OH)-CH=CH2
  2. (2) CH3CH=CH-CH2NH2
  3. (3) CH3CHCH2CH2NH2 with O double bonded to C and H (formyl group)
  4. (4) CH3CH(OH)CH2CH2NHCHO

Answer

Correct answer: Option 4

Solution

It is eg. of S_N2 Th-reaction CH3-CH(OH)-CH2-CH2-NH2 + H-C(=O)-OCH2CH3 undergoes nucleophilic substitution by triethylamine. The reaction mechanism: - The primary amine (NH2) acts as the nucleophile - It attacks the carbonyl carbon of ethyl formate - The C=O bond is activated by the electron-withdrawing ester group - Triethylamine acts as a base to deprotonate the amine, making it a better nucleophile - The reaction proceeds through nucleophilic acyl substitution - The ethoxy group (-OCH2CH3) leaves as a good leaving group - Water is eliminated as a byproduct: -C2H5O^o(C2H5)3N The final product is: CH3-CH(OH)-CH2-CH2-NH-C(=O)H This is a formamide derivative where the primary amine has been converted to a secondary amide (specifically a formamide) by reaction with ethyl formate. The hydroxyl group on the side chain remains intact as it does not react under these conditions.
