Iodoform test can differentiate between A. Methanol and Ethanol B. CH3COOH and CH3CH2COOH C. Cyclohexene and cyclohexanone D. Diethyl ether and Pentan-3-one E. Anisole and acetone Choose the correct answer from the options given below: (1) A & E only (2) A & D only (3) A, B & E only (4) B, C & E only

  1. (1) A & E only
  2. (2) A & D only
  3. (3) A, B & E only
  4. (4) B, C & E only

Answer

Correct answer: Option 1

Solution

The iodoform test is used to identify compounds containing the CH3CO- group (methyl ketones) or CH3CH(OH)- group (secondary alcohols with a methyl group at the alpha position). The reaction proceeds as follows: R-C(=O)-CH3 or R-CH(OH)-CH3 undergoes reaction with NaOH and I2 to give R-COONa + CHI3 (yellow precipitate). Analysis of each pair: (A) Methanol (CH3OH) and Ethanol (CH3CH2OH): Methanol has CH3- group which can give iodoform test when oxidized to formaldehyde and further, but ethanol gives CH3CH2- which doesn't give iodoform test. These can be differentiated. (B) CH3COOH and CH3CH2COOH: Both are carboxylic acids without CH3CO- or CH3CH(OH)- groups. Neither gives iodoform test. Cannot differentiate. (C) Cyclohexene and cyclohexanone: Cyclohexene is an alkene (doesn't give iodoform test). Cyclohexanone is a cyclic ketone without the CH3CO- group (doesn't give iodoform test). Cannot differentiate. (D) Diethyl ether and Pentan-3-one: Diethyl ether (CH3CH2-O-CH2CH3) doesn't give iodoform test. Pentan-3-one (CH3-CO-CH2CH3) is a ketone but not a methyl ketone - doesn't give iodoform test. Cannot differentiate. (E) Anisole and acetone: Anisole (C6H5-OCH3) doesn't have CH3CO- or CH3CH(OH)- group. Acetone (CH3-CO-CH3) is a methyl ketone and gives positive iodoform test. These can be differentiated. Therefore, the pairs that can be differentiated are A and E only.
