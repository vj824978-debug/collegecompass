Dilution processes of different aqueous solutions, with water, are given in LIST-I. The effects of dilution of the solutions on [H+] are given in LIST-II. (Note: Degree of dissociation (α) of weak acid and weak base is << 1; degree of hydrolysis of salt <<1; [H+] represents the concentration of H+ ions) [TABLE] LIST-I | LIST-II P. (10 mL of 0.1 M NaOH + 20 mL of 0.1 M acetic acid) diluted to 60 mL | 1. the value of [H+] does not change on dilution Q. (20 mL of 0.1 M NaOH + 20 mL of 0.1 M acetic acid) diluted to 80 mL | 2. the value of [H+] changes to half of its initial value on dilution R. (20 mL of 0.1 M HCl + 20 mL of 0.1 M ammonia solution) diluted to 80 mL | 3. the value of [H+] changes to two times of its initial value on dilution S. 10 mL saturated solution of Ni(OH)₂ in equilibrium with excess solid Ni(OH)₂ is diluted to 20 mL (solid Ni(OH)₂ is still present after dilution). | 4. the value of [H+] changes to (1/sqrt(2)) times of its initial value on dilution | 5. the value of [H+] changes to sqrt(2) times of its initial value on dilution [END TABLE] Match each process given in LIST-I with one or more effect(s) in LIST-II. The correct option is

Matrix match · Marks: +4 / -1.0

  1. (A) (A) P → 4; Q → 2; R → 3; S → 1
  2. (B) (B) P → 4; Q → 3; R → 2; S → 3
  3. (C) (C) P → 1; Q → 4; R → 5; S → 3
  4. (D) (D) P → 1; Q → 5; R → 4; S → 1

Answer

Correct answer: D

Solution

K_w/K_a = [CH₃COO⁻][OH⁻]/[CH₃COOH] [OH⁻] = (K_w/K_a × C)^(1/2) (P) is a buffer, so [H⁺] does not change on dilution, as [salt] = [acid]. (Q) contains only CH₃COONa So CH₃COO⁻ + H₂O ⇌ CH₃COOH + OH⁻ [OH⁻] = √(K_a × C) ⟹ [H⁺] decreases by √2 times (R) is also salt hydrolysis So, NH₄⁺ + H₂O ⇌ NH₃OH + H⁺ [H⁺] = √(K_c/K_b × C) So, C is made 1/2 so, [H⁺] becomes 1/√2 (S) it is a solubility equilibria So dilution does not effect [H⁺] or [OH⁻]
