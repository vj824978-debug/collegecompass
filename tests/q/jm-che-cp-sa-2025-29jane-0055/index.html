Which among the following halides will generate the most stable carbocation in Nucleophillic substitution reaction?

  1. (1) Br-CH=CH-CH2 (allyl bromide)
  2. (2) C6H5-CH2-Br (benzyl bromide)
  3. (3) C6H11-Br (cyclohexyl bromide)
  4. (4) Ph-C(Br)(Ph)2 (triphenylmethyl bromide)

Answer

Correct answer: Option 4

Solution

Stability order of carbocation: Ph-C(+)(Ph)2 > C6H5-CH(+) > C6H11(+) > CH=CH-CH2(+) The triphenylmethyl cation (trityl cation) from option (4) is the most stable carbocation because: 1. It is a tertiary carbocation (three groups attached to the positively charged carbon) 2. The three phenyl groups provide extensive resonance stabilization through pi-electron delocalization 3. The positive charge is delocalized over the aromatic rings, significantly stabilizing the carbocation 4. Option (2) - benzyl carbocation is also stabilized by resonance through one benzene ring 5. Option (3) - cyclohexyl carbocation is a secondary/primary aliphatic carbocation with no resonance stabilization 6. Option (1) - allyl carbocation has only allylic resonance stabilization Therefore, the triphenylmethyl bromide (option 4) will generate the most stable carbocation and is the answer.
