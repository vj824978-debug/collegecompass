All structures given below are of vitamin C. Most stable of them is:

  1. (1) HO-CH2 with H and OH groups on a 5-membered ring containing O, with a C=O group and OH groups
  2. (2) HO-CH2 with H and OH groups on a 5-membered ring containing O, with OH groups and a C=O group in the ring
  3. (3) HO-CH2 with H and OH groups on a 5-membered ring with two C=O groups and one OH group
  4. (4) HO-CH2 with H and OH groups on a 5-membered ring containing O, with C=O groups and OH groups

Answer

Correct answer: Option 1

Solution

Most stable structure of vitamin C is: The structure shown in the answer has a HO-CH2 group attached to a 5-membered ring (lactone ring) with an OH group in the axial position, an H in the equatorial position, and OH, OH groups on the ring with one C=O group. This is L-ascorbic acid (vitamin C), which is the most stable form of vitamin C. The presence of the enediol functional group (two OH groups adjacent to a C=C double bond in the ring system) in structure (1) makes it the most stable and biologically active form of vitamin C. Structure (2) has too many oxygen functionalities disrupting the stability. Structure (3) and (4) have additional ketone groups that make them less stable oxidized forms (like dehydroascorbic acid derivatives).
