The increasing order of the acidity of the α-hydrogen of the following compounds is:

  1. (1) (C) < (A) < (B) < (D)
  2. (2) (B) < (C) < (A) < (D)
  3. (3) (A) < (C) < (D) < (B)
  4. (4) (D) < (C) < (A) < (B)

Answer

Correct answer: Option 4

Solution

To determine the acidity of α-hydrogen, we need to consider the stability of the conjugate anion (carbanion) formed after deprotonation. The stability of the carbanion depends on: 1. Electron-withdrawing groups attached to the α-carbon (stabilize the negative charge) 2. Conjugation with carbonyl or other electron-deficient groups 3. Cross-conjugation effects Analyzing each compound: (A) Simple acetone (CH3-CO-CH3): Single carbonyl group provides moderate stabilization through cross-conjugation. (B) Dibenzoylmethane (Ph-CO-CH2-CO-Ph): Two phenyl ketone groups on either side of the central CH2. The α-hydrogen has maximum conjugation with both carbonyl groups, forming a highly stable carbanion with extended conjugation. (C) Methyl ester (has -OMe group): Electron-donating -OMe group (through resonance) reduces the electron-withdrawing ability compared to pure ketone. (D) Dimethylamino compound (has -NMe2 group): The -NMe2 is strongly electron-donating through resonance, making it the least acidic. Stability order of conjugate anions (carbanions): D (least stable - electron-donating NMe2) < C (electron-donating OMe) < A (single carbonyl) < B (two carbonyls with extended conjugation and cross-conjugation) Therefore: (D) < (C) < (A) < (B)
