The correct sequence of reagents for the above conversion of X to Y is:

  1. (1) (i) NaOH (aq) (ii) Jones reagent (iii) H3O+
  2. (2) (i) B2H6/H2O2 (ii) NaOEt (iii) Jones reagent
  3. (3) (i) Jones reagent (ii) NaOEt (iii) Hot KMnO4 / KOH
  4. (4) (i) NaOEt (ii) B2H6 / H2O2 (iii) Jones reagent

Answer

Correct answer: Option 4

Solution

To convert benzene with a methyl group (X) to benzene with a carboxylic acid group (Y), we need to: 1. First convert the methyl group (-CH3) to a more reactive intermediate 2. Then oxidize the side chain to carboxylic acid The correct sequence is: (i) NaOEt: Eliminate a hydrogen from the methyl group to form a benzene ring with a double bond (CH=CH2 or vinyl group). This is elimination reaction. (ii) B2H6 / H2O2: This is hydroboration-oxidation. B2H6 adds to the double bond, followed by H2O2 oxidation to give a primary alcohol (-CH2OH). After this step, we have: benzene-CH2-OH (iii) Jones reagent: This is a strong oxidizing agent (CrO3 in H2SO4) that oxidizes primary alcohols to carboxylic acids. benzene-CH2-OH + Jones reagent → benzene-COOH The solution diagram shows: - Starting with phenylbromide derivatives - NaOEt elimination gives vinyl benzene - B2H6/H2O2 hydroboration-oxidation gives benzyl alcohol - CrO3/H+ oxidation gives phenylacetic acid (benzene-CH2-COOH) However, the question shows the target Y is benzene-COOH (directly attached carboxylic acid), which matches the final product after Jones oxidation.
