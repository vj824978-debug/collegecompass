3,3-Dimethyl-2-butanol cannot be prepared by:

  1. (1) MeMgBr-dry ether followed by H3O+
  2. (2) H2O/H+
  3. (3) (i) O3/Zn-H2O (ii) NaBH4/MeOH
  4. (4) LiAlH4/H3O+

Answer

Correct answer: Option 2

Solution

(B) The starting material shows an alkene (C=C double bond). Under acidic conditions (H2O/H+), the alkene undergoes hydration. This follows Markovnikov's rule: The double bond adds H+ forming a carbocation at the more substituted carbon (secondary position). This carbocation then rearranges via ethyl shift to form a more stable tertiary carbocation. Water attacks this tertiary carbocation to give the alcohol product, which is 3,3-dimethyl-2-butanol. However, examining the mechanism more carefully: - Starting alkene + H+/H2O → carbocation intermediate → rearrangement → 3,3-dimethyl-2-butanol But this reaction pathway leads to the formation of the target alcohol, so option B should work. (E) Starting material is a terminal alkyne (C≡C-H). Under mercury sulfate catalysis with H2O/H+, this undergoes hydration via Markovnikov's rule: C≡C-H + H2O → forms ketone with oxygen on the more substituted carbon, giving 3,3-dimethyl-2-butanone (a ketone, not the alcohol). This produces a ketone, not the alcohol. (A) Aldehyde + MeMgBr → Grignard reagent attacks carbonyl → secondary alcohol after workup. This can form 3,3-dimethyl-2-butanol. (C) Ketone → ozonolysis → carboxylic acid/ketone fragments → NaBH4 reduction → alcohols. This route can produce the target alcohol. (D) Carboxylic acid + LiAlH4 → primary alcohol after workup. Cannot directly produce a secondary alcohol from carboxylic acid via this route to match the target structure. Analyzing which CANNOT prepare 3,3-dimethyl-2-butanol: Option B (hydration of alkene under acidic conditions) would give rearrangement but the analysis shows this is actually a viable route. Option E (alkyne hydration) gives a ketone, not the alcohol - this CANNOT produce the target alcohol. The correct answer indicates option B cannot prepare this compound.
