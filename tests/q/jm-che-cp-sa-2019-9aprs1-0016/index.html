The correct IUPAC name of the following compound is: [benzene ring with NO2 at position 1, Cl at position 2, and CH3 at position 4]

  1. (1) 5-chloro-4-methyl-1-nitrobenzene
  2. (2) 2-chloro-1-methyl-4-nitrobenzene
  3. (3) 2-methyl-5-nitro-1-chlorobenzene
  4. (4) 3-chloro-4-methyl-1-nitrobenzene

Answer

Correct answer: Option 2

Solution

To determine the correct IUPAC name, we need to: 1. Identify the three substituents on the benzene ring: - NO2 (nitro group) - Cl (chloro group) - CH3 (methyl group) 2. Determine priority by alphabetical order for numbering: - Chloro (C comes first alphabetically) - Methyl (M comes second) - Nitro (N comes third) 3. Number the positions to give lowest locants to the substituents in alphabetical order: - Start numbering from the position of the highest priority group in IUPAC name - For benzene derivatives with multiple substituents, we number to minimize the locant numbers - Place Cl at position 1 (highest priority alphabetically among the three) - Place CH3 at position 2 (gives lower numbers than other arrangements) - Place NO2 at position 4 4. Verify: This gives positions 1, 2, and 4 5. Write the name in alphabetical order of substituents: - 2-chloro-1-methyl-4-nitrobenzene - Wait, we must list in alphabetical order: chloro, methyl, nitro - 2-chloro-1-methyl-4-nitrobenzene is the correct arrangement Actually, using standard IUPAC nomenclature where we number to give the lowest set of locants: - Cl at position 1 - CH3 at position 2 - NO2 at position 4 - Alphabetically: chloro comes before methyl comes before nitro - Name: 2-chloro-1-methyl-4-nitrobenzene The answer is option [2].
