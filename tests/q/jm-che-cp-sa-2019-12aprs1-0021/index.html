The complex ion that will lose its crystal field stabilization energy upon oxidation of its metal to +3 state is: (Phen = phenanthroline ligand structure shown, and ignore pairing energy)

  1. (1) [Fe(phen)3]^(2+)
  2. (2) [Zn(phen)3]^(2+)
  3. (3) [Co(phen)3]^(2+)
  4. (4) [Ni(phen)3]^(2+)

Answer

Correct answer: Option 1

Solution

In the complexes of Fe^(2+), Co^(2+), Ni^(2+) upon oxidation, complexes of Fe^(3+), Co^(3+) & Ni^(3+) stabilizes whereas in case of removing 3rd electron from Zn becomes difficult. When Fe^(2+) is oxidized to Fe^(3+): - Fe^(2+) has d^6 configuration with CFSE - Fe^(3+) has d^5 configuration with half-filled d orbitals - Fe^(3+) gains additional stability from half-filled d subshell - Therefore Fe^(2+) loses CFSE upon oxidation When Zn^(2+) is oxidized: - Zn has d^10 configuration (completely filled) - Removal of electron from d^10 is very difficult - Zn^(3+) is not easily formed When Co^(2+) is oxidized to Co^(3+): - Co^(3+) with d^6 configuration in octahedral field is highly stable - Co^(2+) loses CFSE but Co^(3+) gains more CFSE When Ni^(2+) is oxidized to Ni^(3+): - Ni^(3+) with d^7 configuration gains stability Among all, Fe^(2+) loses maximum CFSE upon oxidation because Fe^(3+) achieves the stable d^5 half-filled configuration.
