A vertical closed cylinder is separated into two parts by a frictionless piston of mass m and of negligible thickness. The piston is free to move along the length of the cylinder. The length of the cylinder above the piston is l_1, and that below the piston is l_2, such that l_1 > l_2. Each part of the cylinder contains n moles of an ideal gas at equal temperature T. If the piston is stationary, its mass, m, will be given by: (R is universal gas constant and g is the acceleration due to gravity)

  1. (1) (nRT/g)[(l_1 - l_2)/(l_1*l_2)]
  2. (2) (RT/g)[(2l_1 + l_2)/(l_1*l_2)]
  3. (3) (nRT/g)[(1/l_2) + (1/l_1)]
  4. (4) (RT/ng)[(l_1 - 3l_2)/(l_1*l_2)]

Answer

Correct answer: Option 1

Solution

For the piston to be in equilibrium, the net force on it must be zero. Forces acting on the piston: - Weight: mg (downward) - Pressure force from gas above: F_1 = p_1*A (downward) - Pressure force from gas below: F_2 = p_2*A (upward) where A is the cross-sectional area of the piston. Equilibrium condition: Mg + F_1 = F_2 Mg + p_1*A = p_2*A Mg = (p_2 - p_1)*A Using ideal gas law for each part: For upper part: p_1*V_1 = nRT, where V_1 = A*l_1 So p_1 = nRT/(A*l_1) For lower part: p_2*V_2 = nRT, where V_2 = A*l_2 So p_2 = nRT/(A*l_2) Substituting into equilibrium equation: Mg = (p_2 - p_1)*A Mg = [nRT/(A*l_2) - nRT/(A*l_1)]*A Mg = nRT[1/l_2 - 1/l_1] Mg = nRT[(l_1 - l_2)/(l_1*l_2)] Therefore: m = (nRT/g)[(l_1 - l_2)/(l_1*l_2)]
