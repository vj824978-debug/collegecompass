CaCO3(s) + 2HCl(aq) → CaCl2(aq) + CO2(g) + H2O(l). Consider the above reaction, what mass of CaCl2 will be formed if 250 ml of 0.76 M HCl reacts with 1000g of CaCO3? (Given: Molar mass of Ca, C, O, H and Cl are 40, 12, 16, 1 and 35.5 g/mol respectively)

  1. (1) 10.545 g
  2. (2) 3.908 g
  3. (3) 5.272 g
  4. (4) 2.636 g

Answer

Correct answer: Option 1

Solution

Step 1: Calculate moles of HCl = 0.250 L × 0.76 M = 0.19 mol. Step 2: Calculate molar mass of CaCO3 = 40 + 12 + 3(16) = 100 g/mol. Moles of CaCO3 = 1000/100 = 10 mol. Step 3: From stoichiometry, 2 mol HCl produces 1 mol CaCl2. Moles of HCl (0.19) is limiting reagent. Moles of CaCl2 formed = 0.19/2 = 0.095 mol. Step 4: Molar mass of CaCl2 = 40 + 2(35.5) = 111 g/mol. Mass of CaCl2 = 0.095 × 111 = 10.545 g.
