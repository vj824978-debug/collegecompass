An ionic micelle is formed on the addition of: excess water to liquid

  1. (1) N with H3C and CH3 groups attached to imidazole ring with PF6^- counterion, excess water to liquid
  2. (2) sodium stearate to pure toluene
  3. (3) N with H3C and CH3 groups attached to imidazole ring with long alkyl chain and SO4^- counterion
  4. (4) liquid diethyl ether to aqueous NaCl solution

Answer

Correct answer: Option 3

Solution

Correct Ans. is (3) An ionic micelle is formed when an ionic surfactant (amphiphilic molecule with both hydrophobic and hydrophilic regions) is added to an appropriate solvent. Analyzing each option: (1) Ionic liquid (imidazolium PF6^-) + excess water: This forms a homogeneous ionic liquid solution, not a micelle, as the PF6^- anion is not sufficiently lipophilic to drive micelle formation in water. (2) Sodium stearate + pure toluene: Sodium stearate is an anionic surfactant that forms micelles in aqueous media, not in nonpolar solvents like toluene. In toluene, the ionic surfactant would not aggregate into micelles. (3) Imidazolium surfactant with long alkyl chain + SO4^- counterion + excess water: This is a typical cationic ionic surfactant. The long alkyl chain is hydrophobic while the imidazolium head group with SO4^- counterion is hydrophilic. Upon addition to water, the molecules self-assemble to form ionic micelles with the hydrophobic chains directed inward and the ionic heads directed outward in contact with water. (4) Diethyl ether + aqueous NaCl solution: These are immiscible liquids that form separate phases; no micelle formation occurs. Option (3) contains a proper ionic surfactant with both hydrophobic alkyl chain and hydrophilic ionic head group, which forms ionic micelles in water.
