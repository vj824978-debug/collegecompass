The correct order of stability for the following alkoxides is: (A) Structure with O^(-) bonded to carbon with NO2 group and methyl group (B) Structure with O^(-) bonded to carbon with NO2 group and double bond to C (C) Structure with O^(-) bonded to carbon in conjugation with NO2 group through C=C double bond

  1. (1) (C) > (A) > (B)
  2. (2) (B) > (C) > (A)
  3. (3) (B) > (A) > (C)
  4. (4) (C) > (B) > (A)

Answer

Correct answer: Option 4

Solution

To determine the stability of alkoxides, we need to analyze the factors that stabilize the negative charge on oxygen. Structure (A): O^(-)-CH(NO2)-CH3 The negative charge is stabilized by the electron-withdrawing NO2 group attached directly to the carbon bearing the oxygen. This provides inductive stabilization. Structure (B): O^(-)-C(=O)-NO2 (structure shows a resonance form) The negative charge is stabilized by resonance with the NO2 group. The negative charge can be delocalized into the pi system, providing resonance stabilization. Structure (C): O^(-)-CH=CH-NO2 The negative charge on the oxygen can be stabilized by: (1) Mesomeric effect of NO2 group through conjugation via C=C double bond, (2) The negative charge can delocalize through the extended conjugated system (O^(-) - C=C - NO2). Order of stabilization: - Structure (C) is most stable because the negative charge can be delocalized through the entire conjugated π-system (O^(-) - C=C - N(O)=O), providing maximum resonance stabilization through the extended conjugation. - Structure (B) is next because it has resonance stabilization but through a more localized system. - Structure (A) is least stable because it relies only on the inductive effect of the directly attached NO2 group. Therefore, the stability order is: (C) > (B) > (A)
