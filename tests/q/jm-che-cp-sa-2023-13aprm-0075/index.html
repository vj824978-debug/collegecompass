2-Methyl propyl bromide reacts with C2H5O and gives 'A' whereas on reaction with C2H5OH it gives 'B'. The mechanism followed in these reactions and the products 'A' and 'B' respectively are:

  1. (1) SN2, A = iso-butyl ethyl ether; SN1, B = tert-butyl ethyl ether
  2. (2) SN1, A = tert-butyl ethyl ether; SN1, B = 2-butyl ethyl ether
  3. (3) SN2, A = 2-butyl ethyl ether; SN2, B = iso-butyl ethyl ether
  4. (4) SN1, A = tert-butyl ethyl ether; SN2, B = iso-butyl ethyl ether

Answer

Correct answer: Option 1

Solution

2-Methyl propyl bromide (isobutyl bromide) is a primary alkyl bromide. With C2H5O (ethoxide ion, a strong nucleophile), it undergoes SN2 mechanism giving iso-butyl ethyl ether (A) as the product with inversion of configuration. With C2H5OH (ethanol, a weak nucleophile and polar protic solvent), it would favor ionization but as a primary substrate the reaction still proceeds predominantly via SN2 or minor SN1 pathways. However, the given answer indicates SN1 mechanism giving tert-butyl ethyl ether (B), which suggests rearrangement occurs forming a more stable secondary carbocation intermediate that leads to tert-butyl product.
