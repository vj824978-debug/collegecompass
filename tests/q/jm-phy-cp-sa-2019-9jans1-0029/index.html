Two masses m and m/2 are connected at the two ends of a massless rigid rod of length l. The rod is suspended by a thin wire of torsional constant k at the centre of mass of the rod-mass system (see figure). Because of torsional constant k, the restoring torque is tau = k*theta for angular displacement theta. If the rod is rotated by theta_0 and released, the tension in it when it passes through its mean position will be:

  1. (1) (2k*theta_0^2)/l
  2. (2) (k*theta_0^2)/l
  3. (3) (3k*theta_0^2)/l
  4. (4) (k*theta_0^2)/(2l)

Answer

Correct answer: Option 2

Solution

The center of mass of the system is located at distance 2l/3 from mass m/2 and l/3 from mass m. Using energy conservation: (1/2)*k*theta_0^2 = (1/2)*I*omega^2 The moment of inertia about the center of mass: I = (m/2)*(2l/3)^2 + m*(l/3)^2 = (m/2)*(4l^2/9) + m*(l^2/9) = (2ml^2/9) + (ml^2/9) = ml^2/3 From energy conservation: (1/2)*k*theta_0^2 = (1/2)*I*omega^2 = (1/2)*(ml^2/3)*omega^2 Therefore: omega^2 = (3k*theta_0^2)/(ml^2) At mean position, applying Newton's second law for the rotating system: The centripetal force on each mass is provided by the difference between tension and weight components. For mass m at distance l/3: T - m*g = m*(l/3)*omega^2 For mass m/2 at distance 2l/3: T - (m/2)*g = (m/2)*(2l/3)*omega^2 Taking the net tension in the rod: T = m*omega^2*(l/3) = m*(3k*theta_0^2)/(ml^2)*(l/3) = (k*theta_0^2)/l Option (2) is correct.
