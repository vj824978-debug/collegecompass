The actual value of resistance R, shown in the figure is 30Ω. This is measured in an experiment as shown using the standard formula R = V/I, where V and I are the readings of the voltmeter and ammeter, respectively. If the measured value of R is 5% less, then the internal resistance of the voltmeter is -

  1. (1) 570 Ω
  2. (2) 600 Ω
  3. (3) 350 Ω
  4. (4) 35 Ω

Answer

Correct answer: Option 1

Solution

Given: - Actual value of resistance R = 30Ω - Measured value of R is 5% less than actual value The measured resistance is: R_mes = 30 - 30 × (5/100) = 30[1 - 5/100] = 28.5Ω From the circuit, the voltmeter has internal resistance R_V and is connected in parallel with R. The ammeter reads the total current through both R and R_V. The measured resistance is: R_mes = V/I = (R_V × R)/(R_V + R) Substituting R_mes = 28.5Ω and R = 30Ω: 28.5 = (R_V × 30)/(R_V + 30) 28.5(R_V + 30) = R_V × 30 28.5R_V + 28.5 × 30 = R_V × 30 28.5R_V + 855 = 30R_V 855 = 30R_V - 28.5R_V 855 = 1.5R_V R_V = 855/1.5 = (28.5/1.5) × 30 = 19 × 30 = 570Ω
