At 300 K, the vapour pressure of a solution containing 1 mole of n-hexane and 3 moles of n-heptane is 550 mm of Hg. At the same temperature, if one more mole of n-heptane is added to this solution, the vapour pressure of the solution increases by 10 mm of Hg. What is the vapour pressure in mm Hg of n-heptane in its pure state?

Answer

Correct answer: 600

Solution

Using Raoult's Law for ideal solutions: Initial solution: 1 mole of n-hexane (A) and 3 moles of n-heptane (B) Total moles = 4 550 = P°_A × (1/4) + P°_B × (3/4) Multiplying by 4: 2200 = P°_A + 3P°_B ... (i) After adding 1 more mole of n-heptane: Total moles = 5 (1 mole A and 4 moles B) New vapour pressure = 550 + 10 = 560 mm of Hg 560 = P°_A × (1/5) + P°_B × (4/5) Multiplying by 5: 2800 = P°_A + 4P°_B ... (ii) Subtracting equation (i) from equation (ii): 2800 - 2200 = P°_A + 4P°_B - P°_A - 3P°_B 600 = P°_B Therefore, P°_B = 600 mm of Hg Verification: From equation (i): 2200 = P°_A + 3(600) P°_A = 2200 - 1800 = 400 mm of Hg Check with initial condition: 550 = 400(1/4) + 600(3/4) = 100 + 450 = 550 ✓ Check with second condition: 560 = 400(1/5) + 600(4/5) = 80 + 480 = 560 ✓
