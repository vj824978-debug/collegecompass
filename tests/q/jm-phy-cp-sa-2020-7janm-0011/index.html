A satellite of mass m is launched vertically upwards with an initial speed u from the surface of the earth. After it reaches height R(R = radius of the earth), it ejects a rocket of mass m/10, so that subsequently the satellite moves in a circular orbit. The kinetic energy of the rocket is (G is the gravitational constant; M is the mass of the earth):

  1. (1) 5m(u^2 - 119GM/(200R))
  2. (2) m/20(u - sqrt(2GM/(3R)))^2
  3. (3) 3m/8(u + sqrt(5GM/(6R)))^2
  4. (4) m/20(u^2 + 113GM/(200R))

Answer

Correct answer: Option 1

Solution

Using energy conservation 1/2 mu^2 - GMm/R = -GMm/(2R) + 1/2 mv^2 u^2 - 2GM/R = -GM/R + v^2 u^2 - GM/R = v^2 Again after ejecting rocket vector P_i = vector P_f For satellite of mass m at height R with velocity v_1 (upward): m × sqrt(GM/(2R)) = (9m/10) × v_2 (momentum conservation along tangent) 9m/10 × sqrt(GM/(2R)) = m/10 × v_2 (momentum conservation along tangent) v_2 = 9 × sqrt(GM/(2R)) mv = m/10 × v_1 (momentum conservation along radius) v_1 = 10v K.E. of rocket = 1/2 × m/10 × (v_1^2 + v_2^2) = 1/2 × m/10 × (100v^2 - GM/R + 81 × GM/(2R)) = m/20 × (100u^2 - 100GM/R + 81GM/(2R)) = m/20 × (100u^2 - 100GM/R + 81GM/(2R)) = m(100u^2 - 200GM + 81GM)/(200R) = 5m(u^2 - 119GM/(200R))
