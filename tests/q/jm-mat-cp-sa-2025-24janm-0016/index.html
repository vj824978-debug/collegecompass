For some n ≠ 10, let the coefficients of the 5th, 6th and 7th terms in the binomial expansion of (1 + x)^(n+4) be in A.P. Then the largest coefficient in the expansion of (1 + x)^(n+4) is:

  1. (1) 70
  2. (2) 35
  3. (3) 20
  4. (4) 10

Answer

Correct answer: Option 2

Solution

(1 + x)^(n+4) ^(n+4)C_4, ^(n+4)C_5, ^(n+4)C_6 → A.P. ⟹ 2 × ^(n+4)C_5 = ^(n+4)C_4 + ^(n+4)C_6 ⟹ 4 × ^(n+4)C_5 = (^(n+4)C_4 + ^(n+4)C_5) + (^(n+4)C_5 + ^(n+4)C_6) ⟹ 4 × ^(n+4)C_5 = ^(n+5)C_5 + ^(n+5)C_6 ⟹ 4 × (n+4)!/(5!·(n-1)!) = (n+5)!/(5!·n!) + (n+6)!/(6!·n!) ⟹ 4 = ((n+6)(n+5))/(6n) ⟹ n^2 + 11n + 30 = 24n ⟹ n^2 - 13n + 30 = 0 ⟹ n = 3, 10(rejected) ∴ n ≠ 10 ∴ Largest binomial coefficient in expansion of (1 + x)^7 (∵ n + 4 = 7) is coeff. of middle term ⟹ ^7C_4 = ^7C_3 = 35
