The one giving maximum number of isomeric alkenes on dehydrohalogenation reaction is (excluding rearrangement)

  1. (1) 1-Bromo-2-methylbutane
  2. (2) 2-Bromopentane
  3. (3) 2-Bromo-3, 3-dimethylpentane
  4. (4) 2-Bromopropane

Answer

Correct answer: Option 2

Solution

In dehydrohalogenation (E2 elimination), we need to remove H and Br from adjacent carbons to form C=C double bonds. The number of isomeric alkenes depends on the number of β-hydrogens available and the possible positions of the double bond. (1) 1-Bromo-2-methylbutane: Has Br on C1. β-hydrogens available on C2 only (geminal to Br). This gives 1 alkene product as shown in the mechanism. (2) 2-Bromopentane: Has Br on C2. β-hydrogens available on C1 and C3. Removal of H from C1 gives one alkene, removal of H from C3 gives another isomer (cis/trans). This yields 3 isomeric alkenes (E and Z isomers, or different position isomers). As shown in the solution, 2-bromopentane gives 3 alkene products. (3) 2-Bromo-3,3-dimethylpentane: Has Br on C2. β-hydrogens on C1 and C3. However, due to the bulky dimethyl groups at C3, there is steric hindrance. This limits the number of possible products. (4) 2-Bromopropane: Has Br on C2 (secondary). β-hydrogens on C1 and C3. However, with only 3 carbons total, only 1 alkene product is possible (propene). From the solution shown, 2-bromopentane gives 3 different isomeric alkenes, which is the maximum among all options. Answer: [2]
