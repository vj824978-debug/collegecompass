The tangent and the normal lines at the point (sqrt(3),1) to the circle x^2 + y^2 = 4 and the x-axis form a triangle. The area of this triangle (in square units) is -

  1. (1) 4/sqrt(3)
  2. (2) 1/3
  3. (3) 1/sqrt(3)
  4. (4) 2/sqrt(3)

Answer

Correct answer: Option 1

Solution

Given circle: x^2 + y^2 = 4 with point P(sqrt(3), 1) First, find the equation of tangent at P(sqrt(3), 1): For circle x^2 + y^2 = 4, tangent at point (x_0, y_0) is: xx_0 + yy_0 = 4 At P(sqrt(3), 1): sqrt(3)x + y = 4 Slope of radius OP = 1/sqrt(3) Slope of tangent = -sqrt(3) (perpendicular to radius) Slope of normal = 1/sqrt(3) (same as radius) Equation of tangent: sqrt(3)x + y = 4 Find point A where tangent meets x-axis (y = 0): sqrt(3)x + 0 = 4 x = 4/sqrt(3) So A = (4/sqrt(3), 0) Equation of normal at P(sqrt(3), 1) with slope 1/sqrt(3): y - 1 = (1/sqrt(3))(x - sqrt(3)) y = (1/sqrt(3))x - 1 + 1 y = (1/sqrt(3))x Find point B where normal meets x-axis (y = 0): 0 = (1/sqrt(3))x x = 0 So B = (0, 0) = O The triangle is formed by: - Tangent line: sqrt(3)x + y = 4 - Normal line: y = (1/sqrt(3))x - x-axis: y = 0 Vertices of triangle: - O = (0, 0) - A = (4/sqrt(3), 0) - P = (sqrt(3), 1) Area of triangle OPA = (1/2) × base × height = (1/2) × (4/sqrt(3)) × 1 = 2/sqrt(3)
