A sound source S is moving along a straight track with speed v and is emitting sound of frequency v₀ (see figure). An observer is standing at a finite distance, at the point O, from the track. The time variation of frequency heard by the observer is best represented by: (t₀ represents the instant when the distance between the source and observer is minimum)

  1. (1) Graph showing frequency dipping below v₀ at t₀ then returning to v₀
  2. (2) Graph showing frequency jumping down from above v₀ to below v₀ at t₀
  3. (3) Graph showing frequency starting above v₀, crossing v₀ at t₀, then dropping below v₀
  4. (4) Graph showing frequency peaking above v₀ at t₀ then dropping to v₀

Answer

Correct answer: Option 3

Solution

The Doppler effect for a moving source and stationary observer is given by: f_observed = (v_sound / (v_sound - v cos θ)) × f₀ where θ is the angle between the source velocity and the line joining source to observer. At t < t₀: The source is approaching the observer. The angle θ is such that cos θ is positive and decreases as the source gets closer. As θ decreases, cos θ increases, making the denominator smaller, so f_observed increases above f₀. At t = t₀: The distance is minimum, meaning the source is moving perpendicular to the line joining source and observer. At this instant, θ = 90°, so cos θ = 0. Therefore: f_observed = (v_sound / v_sound) × f₀ = f₀ At t > t₀: The source is moving away from the observer. The angle θ becomes obtuse, and cos θ becomes negative. The denominator increases, so f_observed decreases below f₀. Therefore, the frequency starts above v₀, equals v₀ at t₀, and then decreases below v₀. This behavior is represented by graph (3).
