With the increase in the pressure of an ideal gas, the volume falls off more rapidly in an isothermal process in comparison to the adiabatic process. Reason (R): In isothermal process, PV = constant, while in adiabatic process PV^γ = constant. Here γ is the ratio of specific heats, P is the pressure and V is the volume of the ideal gas.

  1. (1) Both (A) and (R) are true but (R) is NOT the correct explanation of (A)
  2. (2) (A) is true but (R) is false
  3. (3) Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A).
  4. (4) (A) is false but (R) is true

Answer

Correct answer: Option 3

Solution

For isothermal process: PV = constant Taking differential: V dP + P dV = 0 So dP/dV = -P/V For adiabatic process: PV^γ = constant Taking differential: V^γ dP + P·γV^(γ-1) dV = 0 So dP/dV = -γP/V Comparing the slopes: |dP/dV|_adiabatic = γ|dP/dV|_isothermal Since γ > 1 for all gases, the magnitude of slope is greater for adiabatic process. This means for the same change in pressure (dP), the change in volume (dV) is smaller in adiabatic process and larger in isothermal process. Alternatively, for the same decrease in volume, pressure increases more rapidly in adiabatic process than in isothermal process. Conversely, when pressure increases, volume decreases more rapidly in isothermal process compared to adiabatic process. Both the graph and the mathematical analysis confirm that: - Assertion (A) is TRUE: Volume falls off more rapidly in isothermal process when pressure increases - Reason (R) is TRUE: The given relationships PV = constant and PV^γ = constant are correct - Reason (R) correctly explains Assertion (A) through the mathematical comparison of dP/dV slopes
