For the given figures, choose the correct options: Circuit (a): 40 Ω resistor with 220 V, 50 Hz AC source Circuit (b): 40 Ω resistor, 40 mH inductor, and 0.5 µF capacitor in series with 220 V, 50 Hz AC source

  1. (1) The rms current in circuit (b) can be larger than that in (a)
  2. (2) At resonance, current in (b) is less than that in (a)
  3. (3) The rms current in figure (a) is always equal to that in figure (b)
  4. (4) The rms current in circuit (b) can never be larger than that in (a)

Answer

Correct answer: Option 1

Solution

For (a), i = V/R = 220/40 = 5.5 A For (b): X_C = 1/(2π f C) = 1/(2π × 50 × 0.5 × 10^(-6)) = 10^6/(50π) Ω X_L = 2π f L = 2π × 50 × 50 × 10^(-3) = 50π Ω Since X_C > X_L, the impedance is greater than 40 Ω. i_rms = 220/Z Therefore, i_rms(b) < i_rms(a) However, at resonance (when X_L = X_C), Z = R = 40 Ω, and i_rms = 220/40 = 5.5 A, which equals that in (a). At non-resonant frequencies, impedance Z > 40 Ω, making i_rms(b) < i_rms(a). But the current in (b) can be equal to or less than that in (a), depending on the frequency. At resonance it equals (a), at other frequencies it is less. Since the question asks about given frequency of 50 Hz and circuit (b) has X_C > X_L making Z > 40 Ω, the current in (b) is less than (a) at this frequency. However, at different frequencies where resonance might occur or where impedance equals 40 Ω, current in (b) can equal current in (a). Therefore, current in (b) can be larger than or equal to that in (a) depending on frequency conditions.
