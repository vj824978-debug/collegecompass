The variation of displacement with time of a particle executing free simple harmonic motion is shown in the figure. The potential energy U(x) versus time (t) plot of the particle is correctly shown in figure:

  1. (1) Figure (A)
  2. (2) Figure (B)
  3. (3) Figure (C)
  4. (4) Figure (D)

Answer

Correct answer: Option 2

Solution

For a particle in simple harmonic motion, displacement x = A*sin(omega*t + phi). Potential energy U(x) = (1/2)*k*x^2, which is proportional to x^2. From the top displacement graph, we can identify positions A, B, C which represent different phases of motion. At positions where displacement is maximum (A and C), potential energy is maximum. At position B where displacement is zero, potential energy is minimum. The potential energy varies as the square of displacement, so it oscillates with twice the frequency of displacement and is always positive (never goes below zero). Examining the options: (A) shows U oscillating symmetrically around zero - incorrect. (B) shows U with maxima at A' and B and minima at C - this matches the pattern where U is maximum when |x| is maximum and minimum when x is zero. (C) shows inverted pattern - incorrect. (D) shows asymmetric pattern - incorrect. The correct answer is (B) because potential energy reaches maximum at the extreme positions (A' corresponds to maximum displacement in first half period, B corresponds to maximum displacement in opposite direction) and minimum at equilibrium position (C).
