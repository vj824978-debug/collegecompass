Which one of the following reaction sequences will give an azo dye?

  1. (1) Nitrobenzene (i) Sn/HCl (ii) NaNO2/HCl (iii) β-naphthol, NaOH
  2. (2) Benzenesulfonic acid (i) SOCl2 (ii) NH3 (iii) benzyl chloride
  3. (3) Benzonitrile (i) 70% H2SO4 (ii) PCl3 (iii) aniline
  4. (4) Aniline (i) HCl/NaNO2 (ii) toluene

Answer

Correct answer: Option 1

Solution

To form an azo dye, we need to create an azo compound (N=N) linkage between an aromatic diazonium salt and an aromatic compound with an electron-donating group (like a phenol or aniline derivative). Analyzing each option: (1) Nitrobenzene reaction sequence: - Sn/HCl: Reduces NO2 to NH2, giving aniline (C6H5NH2) - NaNO2/HCl: Converts aniline to benzenediazonium chloride (C6H5N2+ Cl-) - β-naphthol, NaOH: The diazonium salt couples with β-naphthol (which has an -OH group, making it electron-rich) to form an azo dye This is the correct sequence for azo dye formation. (2) Benzenesulfonic acid to benzamide to coupling with methylbenzene: - SOCl2: Converts SO3H to SOCl group - NH3: Forms benzamide (C6H5CONH2) - Benzyl chloride coupling: Does not form an azo dye; no azo group formation (3) Benzonitrile to aniline: - 70% H2SO4: Hydrolyzes CN to COOH - PCl3: Converts COOH to COCl - Aniline addition: Forms an amide, not an azo dye (4) Aniline to diazonium salt: - HCl/NaNO2: Forms benzenediazonium chloride - Toluene: Toluene lacks the necessary electron-donating group (like -OH or -NR2) for azo coupling Option (1) correctly produces an azo dye through diazotization of aniline and coupling with β-naphthol.
