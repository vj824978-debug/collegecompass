To check the principle of multiple proportions, a series of pure binary compounds (P_m Q_n) were analyzed and their composition is tabulated below. The correct option(s) is(are) [Table with 3 compounds showing Weight % of P and Weight % of Q: Compound 1: P=50, Q=50 Compound 2: P=44.4, Q=55.6 Compound 3: P=40, Q=60]

Multiple correct (MSQ) · Marks: +4 / -2.0

  1. (A) If empirical formula of compound 3 is P₂Q₄, then the empirical formula of compound 2 is P₂Q₅.
  2. (B) If empirical formula of compound 3 is P₃Q₅, and atomic weight of element P is 20, then the atomic weight of Q is 45.
  3. (C) If empirical formula of compound 2 is PQ, then the empirical formula of compound 1 is P₂Q₁.
  4. (D) If atomic weight of P and Q are 70 and 35, respectively, then the empirical formula of compound 1 is P₂Q.

Answer

Correct answer: BC

Solution

As per the law of multiple proportion, if P and Q form two or more compounds, then the ratio of the masses of one of the element (P) that combines with fix mass of Q bear a simple whole number ratio. Now [TABLE 1: Compound compositions] Compound | % of P | % of Q 1 | 50 | 50 2 | 44.4 | 55.6 3 | 40 | 60 OR [TABLE 2: Compound compositions] Compound | % of P | % of Q 1 | 50 | 50 2 | 40 | 50 3 | 33.33 | 50 (A) Given that formula of compound 3 is P₃Q₄ and that of compound 2 is P₃Q₅ Then compound 3 ⟹ P₁₅/₄ Q₅ Compound 2⟹ P₃ Q₅ So, ratio of masses of P in 3 and 2 which combines with fixed mass of Q = 15/4 : 3 = 5/4 But from table 2, ratio of masses of P in 3 and 2 = 33.33/40 = 5/6 Since two ratio are not same, so option (A) is incorrect. (B) Empirical formula of compound P | 40/20 = 2 | 3/2 Q | 60/45 = 4/3 | 1 So, empirical formula of compound 3 is P₃Q₂ (C) Compound 2⟹ PQ or P₂O₄ Compound 3 ⟹ P₃Q₄ So, ratio of masses of P in 2 and 3 which combines with fix mass of Q is = 4 : 5 which resembles table 2. (D) Empirical form of compound should be PQ₂ and not P₃Q
