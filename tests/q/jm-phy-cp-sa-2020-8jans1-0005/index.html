A particle of mass m and charge q is released from rest in a uniform electric field. If there is no other force on the particle the dependence of its speed v on the distance x travelled by it is correctly given by (graphs are schematic and not drawn to scale)

  1. (1) Graph (1): v increases linearly with x
  2. (2) Graph (2): v increases with x in a curve that starts steep and flattens
  3. (3) Graph (3): v increases with x in a curve that starts flat and becomes steeper
  4. (4) Graph (4): v increases with x in a curve that becomes increasingly steep

Answer

Correct answer: Option 3

Solution

F = qE a = F/m = qE/m v dv/dx = qE/m Integrating both sides: ∫₀^v v dv = ∫₀^x (qE/m) dx v²/2 = (qE/m)x v = sqrt((2qE/m)√x) v = sqrt(2qE/m) × √x Since v is proportional to √x, the graph shows: - At x = 0: v = 0 (starts at origin) - dv/dx = sqrt(2qE/m) × 1/(2√x) - As x increases, dv/dx decreases - The curve starts steep and flattens out Wait, correction: v ∝ √x means the slope dv/dx ∝ 1/√x - Near x = 0, the slope is very steep (1/√x is large) - As x increases, the slope decreases (1/√x becomes smaller) Therefore the curve starts flat (appears to have small slope initially due to concavity) and the rate of increase decreases. Actually, for v = C√x where C is a constant: - At x = 0, the curve is tangent to the y-axis (vertical) - The curvature is such that the graph curves and flattens The correct representation is Graph (3): v increases with x but the curve shows decreasing slope, meaning the rate of increase of v with respect to x decreases as x increases.
