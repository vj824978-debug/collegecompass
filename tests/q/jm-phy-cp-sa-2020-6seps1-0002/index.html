For the given input voltage waveform V_in(t), the output voltage waveform V_o(t), across the capacitor is correctly depicted by :

  1. (1) V_o(t) waveform showing rise to 2V at 5μs, then drop to 0V, rise again to 2V at 10μs, drop at 15μs
  2. (2) V_o(t) waveform showing rise to 3V at 5μs, then rise to 2V at 10μs, reaching 3V at 15μs
  3. (3) V_o(t) waveform showing exponential rise to 2V approaching asymptotically from 0 to 15μs
  4. (4) V_o(t) waveform showing rise to 2V at 5μs, drop to lower value at 10μs, rise again at 15μs

Answer

Correct answer: Option 2

Solution

Given circuit: Input voltage switch between +5V and 0V with period 5μs each. Capacitor C = 10nF and Resistor R = 1kΩ. Time constant RC = 1000 × 10×10^(-9) = 10×10^(-6) = 10μs At t = 5μs (charging phase from 0V to +5V): V_o(t) = V_in(1 - e^(-t/RC)) V_o(5μs) = 5(1 - e^(-5×10^(-6)/(10×10^(-6)))) V_o(5μs) = 5(1 - e^(-0.5)) = 5(1 - 0.606) = 5(0.394) ≈ 2V At t = 10μs (discharging phase from +5V to 0V): Now V_in = 0, so capacitor discharges: V_o(t) = V_o(5μs) × e^(-(t-5μs)/RC) V_o(10μs) = 2 × e^(-5×10^(-6)/(10×10^(-6))) V_o(10μs) = 2 × e^(-0.5) = 2 × 0.606 = 1.21V At t = 15μs (charging again from 1.21V to +5V): V_o(t) = 5 - (5 - 1.21)e^(-(t-10μs)/RC) V_o(15μs) = 5 - 3.79 × e^(-0.5) V_o(15μs) = 5 - 3.79 × 0.606 ≈ 5 - 2.30 = 2.79V ≈ 3V The output shows exponential charging and discharging with capacitor voltage reaching approximately 2V after first 5μs, dropping to about 1.2V at 10μs, and rising to about 3V at 15μs, matching option (2).
