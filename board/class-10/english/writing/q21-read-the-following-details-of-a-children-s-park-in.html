Read the following details of a children’s park in your colony:  covered on all four sides with 2 gates …

CBSE 2025QP 2/5/1Q55 marks
Question 21 · Class 10 English · Writing
Question as text
(a) Read the following details of a children’s park in your colony: [5]  covered on all four sides with 2 gates  skating rink and basketball court in the park  swings in one corner for younger children  open ground for colony functions  specific timings Analyse the details of the park and write an analytical paragraph in about 120 words about its suitability as a children’s park. OR (b) There is an alarming increase in the percentage of students suffering from obesity. Given below are some of the findings of a survey conducted to identify causes: [5]  unhealthy diet – 40%  lack of exercise – 30%  stress and anxiety – 25%  genetic factors – 5% Analyse the causes for obesity.
Answer
Answer as text
(a) Value points:  Topic sentence: The children’s park in our colony is well planned and highly suitable for children of all age groups.  Safety: enclosed on all four sides with only 2 gates — controlled entry and exit, children cannot run onto the road.  Sports facilities: skating rink and basketball court promote physical activity and team games for older children.  Swings in a separate corner keep younger children safe from the games of older ones.  Open ground can be used for colony functions — promotes community bonding (but may limit play on those days).  Specific timings ensure supervision, cleanliness and safety.  Conclusion: With its safety features and variety of facilities, the park is ideal for the all-round development and recreation of children. (b) Value points:  Topic sentence: A survey conducted to identify the causes of rising obesity among students shows that lifestyle factors are mainly responsible.  Unhealthy diet is the biggest cause (40%), followed by lack of exercise (30%); together they account for 70%.  Stress and anxiety contribute 25%; genetic factors only 5% — so 95% of cases stem from controllable factors.  Age groups: the 8–12 years group is the most affected (28%), followed by 13–15 years (25%) and 5–8 years (20%).  The 16–18 years (14%) and 0–5 years (13%) groups are the least affected.  Conclusion: Since obesity is largely lifestyle-related and peaks among pre-teens and young teens, healthy eating, regular exercise and stress management must be promoted by parents and schools.
