Question 9 · Class 10 English · Writing
Question as text
Note: All details presented in the questions are imaginary and created for assessment purpose. (A) You are Sonia / Sonu, residing at 67/98 Malkaganj, Sonepat. Compose a letter in about 120 words to the Head Librarian of your child’s school drawing attention to the poor library facilities. Give suggestions on how things can be improved to inculcate reading habits among children. OR (B) As Sharmila Sahani, a conscientious resident of 25/22, Rajpur, Fahetabad, you have recently come across numerous newspaper articles featuring tragic incidents where lives have been lost due to reckless driving and not following the traffic rules. Write a letter to the editor of a national daily, shedding light on this issue and give suggestions on what can be done.
Answer
Answer as text
(A) Letter to the Head Librarian. Format: sender’s address (67/98 Malkaganj, Sonepat); date; receiver: The Head Librarian, (name of school), (city); Subject: Poor library facilities in the school / Suggestions to improve the library; salutation: Sir/Madam; closing: Yours sincerely/faithfully, Sonia/Sonu (parent of . . . , Class . . . ). Content points: introduce self as parent and state concern; problems: outdated and too few books, few age-appropriate books and magazines, inadequate seating, poor lighting/ventilation, no regular library period or issuing system; effect: children lose interest in reading and turn to screens; suggestions: buy new story books, newspapers, magazines and e-books; a weekly library period; reading clubs, book fairs, book reviews and reading competitions; a cosy reading corner and longer timings; request prompt action and offer parents’ cooperation. (B) Letter to the Editor. Format: sender’s address (25/22, Rajpur, Fahetabad); date; receiver: The Editor, (name of national daily), (city); Subject: Rising road accidents due to reckless driving; salutation: Sir/Madam; closing: Yours truly/faithfully, Sharmila Sahani. Content points: request to publish views on the issue; frequent news of fatal accidents; causes: overspeeding, drunken driving, jumping red lights, using mobile phones while driving, underage driving, not wearing helmets/seat belts, lax enforcement; consequences: loss of precious lives, families devastated; suggestions: strict enforcement and heavy fines, CCTV cameras and speed checks, cancellation of licences of repeat offenders, road-safety awareness drives in schools and media, better roads, signage and traffic police presence; appeal to citizens and authorities to act responsibly.
