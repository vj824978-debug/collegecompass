Question as text
Note: All details presented in the questions are imaginary and created for assessment purpose. (a) You are Garima/Gopal, a resident of B/66, Mall Road, Guwahati and a volunteer of ‘Greenpeace India’. Write a letter to the editor of ‘National Times’ expressing your concern about the grave and irreparable damage that environmental pollution is causing to the society. In megacities, rapid urbanization and rampant industrialization has played a key role in increasing the pressure on the limited resources. Discuss how environmental degradation can be curbed with the development, implementation, and enforcement of environmental laws, regulations and policies. OR (b) Obesity has become a global issue. The lack of physical fitness programmes and extra-curriculars has created significant problems that could affect future health of youngsters. 39 percent of individuals around the world are overweight and 13 percent are obese. You are Shiksha/Sumit of Ramjas School, Ranchi. You believe that efforts by the government will help to turn the tide. Write a letter to the Ministry of Health for implementing compulsory programmes at school level to emphasize need for proper nutrition and fitness from a young age.
Answer
Answer as text
(a) Letter to the editor – Format: sender’s address (B/66, Mall Road, Guwahati), date, The Editor, National Times, (city); Subject: Grave damage caused by environmental pollution / need for strict environmental laws; Sir/Madam; body; Yours faithfully, Garima/Gopal (Volunteer, Greenpeace India). Content points: (1) concern at the alarming rise in air, water and soil pollution and its grave, irreparable damage to health and society; (2) causes – rapid urbanization and rampant industrialization in megacities, vehicles, industrial waste, over-use of limited resources; (3) need to develop strong, updated environmental laws and policies (emission norms, waste-management rules, protection of green cover and water bodies); (4) strict implementation and enforcement – monitoring, penalties on polluting industries, environmental impact assessment before projects; (5) role of citizens, NGOs and media – awareness drives, tree plantation, reduce-reuse-recycle; (6) appeal to authorities to act urgently for a sustainable future. (b) Letter to the Ministry of Health – Format: sender’s address (Ramjas School, Ranchi), date, The Secretary/Minister, Ministry of Health and Family Welfare, Government of India, New Delhi; Subject: Request to make nutrition and fitness programmes compulsory in schools; Sir/Madam; body; Yours faithfully, Shiksha/Sumit. Content points: (1) obesity a global issue – 39% of people overweight, 13% obese; rising among youngsters; (2) causes – lack of physical fitness programmes and extra-curriculars, junk food, sedentary screen-based lifestyle; (3) consequences – diabetes, heart disease, low self-esteem, poor academic performance, burden on future health; (4) suggestions – compulsory daily physical activity/sports periods, yoga, nutrition education in curriculum, healthy canteen menus and ban on junk food near schools, regular health check-ups (BMI), trained PE teachers and facilities; (5) involve parents through awareness; (6) belief that government efforts can turn the tide – request urgent action.
