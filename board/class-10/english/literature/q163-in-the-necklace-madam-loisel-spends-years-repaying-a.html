Question as text
In ‘The Necklace’, Madam Loisel spends years repaying a debt for a necklace that was never valuable. How does this irony expose the illusion of wealth and the consequences of chasing appearances?
Answer
Answer as text
Value points:  Matilda was discontented with her modest life and dreamt of luxury.  To look rich at the ball she borrowed a diamond necklace from Madame Forestier and was admired; she then lost it.  Instead of admitting the loss, the Loisels bought a replacement for 36,000 francs, borrowing heavily.  Ten years of hardship: dismissed the maid, moved to a garret, Matilda did hard household work, Loisel worked late; she aged and lost her beauty.  Irony: Madame Forestier revealed the necklace was false, worth at most 500 francs.  Message: wealth and appearances can be illusions; vanity, false pride and dishonesty led to ruin; contentment and honesty matter more.
