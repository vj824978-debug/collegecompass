Question as text
Matilda’s character embodies the journey of change or progression throughout the story, encapsulating the transformations she endured as a consequence of her experiences, trials, and engagements with other characters. In the light of the above information, trace the transformation seen in Matilda’s character in the story. (The Necklace)
Answer
Answer as text
Value points:  At first Matilda is a pretty but discontented, vain woman who dreams of luxury and is unhappy with her modest life as a clerk’s wife.  She is not happy even with the invitation to the ball; she demands a new dress (400 francs) and borrows a diamond necklace from her friend Mme Forestier.  At the ball she enjoys admiration, but she loses the necklace.  She and Loisel borrow 36,000 francs to replace it; they dismiss the servant, move to a cheap attic and work hard for ten years to repay the debt.  Matilda transforms into a hardworking, responsible, practical woman who does all the heavy housework; she loses her beauty and looks old.  At the end she learns from Mme Forestier that the necklace was fake (worth at most 500 francs) — the irony of her sacrifice.  Conclusion: vanity and false pride brought suffering, but hardship made her mature and honest.
