In ‘The Ball Poem’, Berryman explores the themes of loss, growing up, and transformation.

CBSE 2025QP 2/1/1Q10(b)The Ball Poem / Nelson Mandela: Long Walk to Freedom6 marks
Question 144 · Class 10 English · Literature
Question as text
In ‘The Ball Poem’, Berryman explores the themes of loss, growing up, and transformation. Mandela also experienced loss of freedom and suffering along with his countrymen during his growing years on the basis of his race and colour. Compare and contrast the commonality of themes in both the texts. (The Ball Poem and Nelson Mandela – Long Walk to Freedom)
Answer
Answer as text
Value points:  Common theme: loss leads to growing up and inner transformation.  In ‘The Ball Poem’, a boy loses his ball in the harbour; the poet lets him bear the loss alone so that he learns the “epistemology of loss” — that loss is part of life and money cannot buy back what is lost.  Through this small loss the boy matures and learns responsibility, stands up and accepts it.  Mandela as a boy felt free, but as he grew up he realised that his freedom, and that of his people, had been taken away because of their race and colour.  This loss transformed him from a frightened young man into a bold one who sacrificed family and personal freedom to fight apartheid.  Contrast: the boy’s loss is personal and small; Mandela’s loss is collective and political, and his response is a lifelong struggle that ends in the freedom of his nation.  Conclusion: both show that facing loss with courage leads to maturity and strength.
