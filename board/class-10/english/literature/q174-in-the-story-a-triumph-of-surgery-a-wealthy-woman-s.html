CBSE 2025QP 2/5/1Q11(b)A Triumph of Surgery6 marks
Question 174 · Class 10 English · Literature
Question as text
In the story, ‘A Triumph of Surgery’, a wealthy woman’s nonsensical behaviour, is depicted. Do you think such individuals are merely displaying foolishness or could their actions potentially, inflict harm upon others? Support your answer from the text. [6]
Answer
Answer as text
Value points:  Mrs Pumphrey, a rich widow, loved her dog Tricki Woo blindly and pampered him with excess food — cream cakes, chocolates, malt and cod-liver oil, Horlicks at night.  This is not mere foolishness: her misguided love actually harmed Tricki, who became grossly fat, lethargic, refused food and suffered from “flop-bot” and attacks of vomiting, gasping for breath; his life was in danger.  She ignored the vet’s advice to cut his diet and give exercise, showing irresponsibility.  Mr Herriot had to take Tricki to his surgery; with simple food, water, exercise and the company of other dogs, Tricki recovered in two weeks — proving that her pampering was the cause.  Such people may also spoil children or others in their care, harm their health and development, and burden others (doctors, caretakers).  Conclusion: excessive, unwise affection is harmful; love must be guided by good sense — the “triumph of surgery” was really a triumph of common sense.
