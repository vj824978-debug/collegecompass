Out of the experience of an extraordinary human disaster that lasted too long, must be born a society of…

CBSE 2026QP 2/2/1Q6(a)Nelson Mandela: Long Walk to Freedom5 marks
Question 25 · Class 10 English · Literature
Question as text
Read the following extract and answer the questions that follow : [5] Out of the experience of an extraordinary human disaster that lasted too long, must be born a society of which all humanity will be proud. We, who were outlaws not so long ago, have today been given the rare privilege to be host to the nations of the world on our own soil. We thank all of our distinguished international guests for having come to take possession with the people of our country of what is, after all, a common victory for justice, for peace, for human dignity. (Nelson Mandela : Long Walk to Freedom) (i) Which of the following options justifies the statement made by Mandela, “We, who were outlaws . . . ” ? [1] (A) The international leaders who came to attend the ceremony (B) Due to Apartheid, many countries broke diplomatic relations with South Africa (C) The Commonwealth countries and their people (D) The people who fought in the Anglo-Boer War (ii) Complete the sentence appropriately : [1] Mandela thanked all the distinguished international guests because . (iii) In the above lines, Mandela mentions ‘extraordinary human disaster’. What is the significance of this phrase in his speech ? [2] (iv) The tone of Mandela in the above excerpt is : [1] 1. unifying 2. determined 3. aggressive 4. pessimistic 5. hopeless Select the correct option : (A) 1, 2 and 4 (B) 2 and 4 (C) 1 and 2 (D) 4 and 5
Answer
Answer as text
(i) (B) Due to Apartheid, many countries broke diplomatic relations with South Africa. (ii) . . . they had come to take possession, along with the people of South Africa, of what was a common victory for justice, peace and human dignity. (iii) It refers to apartheid — the long years of racial discrimination, oppression and suffering of the black people of South Africa. Mandela uses it to acknowledge that painful past and to say that out of it must be born a new, just society of which all humanity would be proud. (iv) (C) 1 and 2 (unifying and determined).
