Question as text
In ‘The Book That Saved the Earth’, how does the play use humour and irony to comment on both futuristic technology and human assumptions? What message do you think the author conveys through the Martians’ misinterpretation of a nursery rhyme?
Answer
Answer as text
Value points:  The play is narrated by a historian of the future, recalling the Martian attempt to invade Earth in the twentieth century.  Humour: Think-Tank, the vain Martian leader with a huge mental capacity, insults his assistant Noodle; the Martians decide books are sandwiches and try eating them; they “decode” a book of nursery rhymes (Mother Goose).  Irony: despite their advanced space technology, the “intelligent” Martians misread a simple children’s book.  Think-Tank sees himself in Humpty Dumpty (an egg-shaped wall-sitter) and fears a “great fall”; he concludes Earthlings are highly advanced and dangerous.  In panic he calls off the invasion and flees — a nursery-rhyme book saves the Earth.  Message: arrogance and hasty assumptions lead to wrong conclusions; technology without wisdom is useless; books and knowledge are powerful.
