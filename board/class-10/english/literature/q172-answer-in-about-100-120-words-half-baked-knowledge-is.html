Answer in about 100-120 words: Half-baked knowledge is always dangerous.

Question 172 · Class 10 English · Literature
Question as text
Answer in about 100-120 words: Half-baked knowledge is always dangerous. Rushing to conclusions without going deep into the details of things always leads to uninvited chaos and disasters. Explain the above mentioned statements in the light of Think-Tank’s interpretation of the rhymes in the book ‘Mother Goose’. (The Book that saved the Earth)
Answer
Answer as text
 Think-Tank, the arrogant Martian leader, claimed to be all-knowing but had only superficial knowledge of Earth.  Finding the book ‘Mother Goose’ in an Earth library, he misread nursery rhymes as secret codes of an advanced civilisation.  “Hey diddle diddle, the cow jumped over the moon” – he concluded that Earthlings had a space programme so advanced that even cows travelled to the moon, and that a dog laughed, so even animals were intelligent.  “Mary, Mary, quite contrary” – he took silver bells and cockle shells to be weapons and plans of attack.  “Humpty Dumpty” – the egg-shaped Humpty, who resembled him, had a great fall; he took it as a threat to himself.  In panic he called off the invasion and ordered the Martians to flee from Earth.  Noodle’s sensible suggestions were ignored; Think-Tank’s hasty, half-baked conclusions led to chaos – and, ironically, saved the Earth.
