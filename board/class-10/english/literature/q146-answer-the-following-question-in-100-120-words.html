CBSE 2025QP 2/2/1Q10(b)A Tiger in the Zoo / How to Tell Wild Animals6 marks
Question 146 · Class 10 English · Literature
Question as text
Answer the following question in 100–120 words. Explore the contrasting images of the tiger in ‘A Tiger in the Zoo’ and the tiger in ‘How to Tell Wild Animals’.
Answer
Answer as text
V alue points: (1) In ‘A Tiger in the Zoo’ the tiger is caged and helpless: he stalks the few steps of his cage in quiet rage, ignoring visitors, staring at the brilliant stars — a picture of captivity and frustration. (2) The poet contrasts this with how he should be in the wild: lurking in the shadow of long grass near the water hole, hunting deer, terrorising villages with his snarls and fangs. (3) The tone there is serious and sympathetic, protesting against caging wild animals. (4) In ‘How to Tell Wild Animals’ (Carolyn Wells) the Bengal tiger is free and ferocious: a noble wild beast, “with black stripes on a yellow ground”; if he eats you, you will know it is the tiger. (5) Here the tone is light and humorous; the danger is described playfully. (6) Contrast: captive, powerless, angry tiger vs. free, majestic, dangerous tiger; serious protest vs. playful humour.
