What, what is the boy now who has lost his ball.

CBSE 2026QP 2/5/1Q7AThe Ball Poem5 marks
Question 9 · Class 10 English · Literature
Question as text
Read the following extract and answer the questions that follow: What, what is the boy now who has lost his ball. What, what is he to do. I saw it go Merrily bouncing, down the street and then Merrily over – there it is in the water!” No use to say ’O there are other balls’. (The Ball Poem by John Berryman) (i) What does the repetition of the word “merrily” suggest in the context of the ball’s movement? [1] (ii) How does this moment contrast the mood of the poem before and after the ball falls into the water? (Answer in about 40 words) [2] (iii) Fill in the blank with the correct word from the bracket: [1] The poet uses the ball as a symbol of the boy’s . (childhood/adventure) (iv) The line, ‘No use to say, oh there are other balls’ signifies: [1] (A) Words of consolation are inappropriate. (B) The boy must be left alone and play with other toys. (C) The boy should not be left alone. (D) No ball will be as expensive as the one the boy has lost.
Answer
Answer as text
(i) It suggests the carefree, joyful, playful movement of the ball, reflecting the boy’s happy, innocent childhood — which contrasts with the sudden loss that follows. (ii) Before the ball falls, the mood is light and joyful (the ball goes “merrily bouncing”). Once it falls into the water the mood turns to shock, grief and helplessness; the boy stands rigid and trembling, realising for the first time what loss means. (iii) childhood. (iv) (A) Words of consolation are inappropriate.
