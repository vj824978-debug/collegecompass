Question as text
Evaluate the similarities and differences between the tiger in “A Tiger in the Zoo” and the dragon in “The Tale of Custard the Dragon”.
Answer
Answer as text
Value points:  Both are animals that are powerful by nature but are confined and live in a human setting – the tiger in a concrete cage, Custard as Belinda’s pet in a little white house.  Both are misjudged or not allowed to be what they really are.  The tiger: a fierce, strong animal, full of quiet rage, pacing his cage; he longs for the jungle and freedom, and ignores the visitors; he stares at the brilliant stars with his brilliant eyes – a serious poem about cruelty of captivity.  Custard: looks fearsome (sharp teeth, spikes, mouth like a fireplace, dagger-like toes) but is called a coward and mocked; he cries for a ‘nice safe cage’.  Difference in temperament: the tiger is truly ferocious and yearns for freedom; Custard is timid but shows real courage when he fights and eats the pirate.  Difference in tone: ‘A Tiger in the Zoo’ is serious and sympathetic; ‘Custard the Dragon’ is a humorous ballad with rhyme and repetition.  Message: appearances can deceive; animals deserve understanding and freedom.
