Question as text
Answer in 100–120 words : Discuss how the writers use language to create humour in the poem ‘The Tale of Custard the Dragon’ and the play ‘The Proposal’.
Answer
Answer as text
Value points: (1) Custard the Dragon: Ogden Nash uses a ballad form with a lively rhyme and rhythm (house/mouse, Ink/Blink, Mustard/Custard) and funny invented words (“realio, trulio”). (2) Humorous names and pet descriptions: “Ink”, “Blink”, “Mustard” and the dragon “Custard” with “big sharp teeth” and “spikes on top”, yet called a “cowardly dragon” who cries for a “nice safe cage”. (3) Irony and exaggeration: the “brave” Belinda, Ink and Blink run away from the pirate, while timid Custard gobbles him up; afterwards they again boast of bravery and tease Custard. (4) The Proposal: Chekhov’s one-act farce uses comic misunderstandings — Lomov comes to propose to Natalya but the conversation turns into a quarrel over Oxen Meadows and then over whose dog (Guess or Squeezer) is better. (5) Exaggerated language and insults (“pettifogger”, “intriguer”, “blind hen”, “turnip-ghost”), Lomov’s comic hypochondria (palpitations, numb foot) and Chubukov’s sudden changes of mood create humour. (6) Ironical ending: Lomov “faints”, is pushed into accepting, and the couple begin to quarrel again as Chubukov calls for champagne. (7) Conclusion: both writers use exaggeration, irony and playful language to entertain while gently satirising human behaviour.
