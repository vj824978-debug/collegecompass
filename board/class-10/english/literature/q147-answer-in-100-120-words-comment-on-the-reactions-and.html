Answer in 100–120 words : Comment on the reactions and feelings of Lencho and Nelson Mandela when they faced challenges in their lives. (A Letter to God and Nelson Mandela : Long Walk to Freedom)
Answer
Answer as text
Value points: (1) Lencho: a hardworking farmer whose ripe corn field was destroyed by a hailstorm; he was deeply sad (“not even a locust will remain”) and felt his family would go hungry. (2) He did not lose hope; his solid faith in God made him write a letter asking for a hundred pesos to sow his field again. (3) When he received only seventy pesos (collected by the postmaster and staff), he was angry and suspected the post office employees of being “a bunch of crooks” — his faith in God remained unshaken but he distrusted people. (4) Mandela: faced oppression under apartheid and spent many years in prison; he sacrificed family life and personal freedom for his people. (5) He did not lose courage or become bitter; he believed courage is the triumph over fear and that hatred is learnt and so love can also be taught; he saw even a glimmer of humanity in his guards. (6) He emerged without hatred, became South Africa’s first black President and worked for freedom and reconciliation. (7) Conclusion: both faced hardship with hope and faith — Lencho in God, Mandela in humanity and freedom — though Lencho reacted with suspicion while Mandela responded with forgiveness.
