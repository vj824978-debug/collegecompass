Question as text
Answer in about 100–120 words : Hari’s character evolves from being deceitful and opportunistic to remorseful and seeking redemption. In the light of the above information, describe how his decision to return the money signifies a moral awakening in him and a newfound appreciation for Anil’s kindness and mentorship. (The Thief’s Story)
Answer
Answer as text
Value points: (1) Hari Singh, a fifteen-year-old experienced thief, flattered Anil to get work, planning to rob him; he even cheated him daily on the shopping. (2) Anil was kind and trusting: he gave Hari shelter and food, taught him to cook, write his name and promised to teach him more; he knew of the petty cheating but said nothing. (3) When Anil brought home six hundred rupees, Hari stole it and ran to the station to catch the Lucknow train, but he could not board it. (4) He realised that by stealing he had lost the chance to become educated — “a clever man” — and that Anil’s trust was more valuable than the money. (5) He recalled that Anil never expressed sorrow at losing money but would at losing trust; this awakened his conscience and he felt remorse. (6) He returned in the rain, slipped the damp notes back under the mattress; Anil knowingly gave him a fifty-rupee note and said they would start writing sentences — a silent forgiveness. (7) Conclusion: returning the money marks Hari’s moral awakening, his value for education and trust, and the beginning of a new honest life.
