“Conserving energy is essential for sustainable development.” Support the statement with suitable arguments.

CBSE 2026QP 32/5/1Q175 marks
Question 8 · Class 10 Social Science · Geography: Minerals and Energy Resources
Question as text
(a) “Conserving energy is essential for sustainable development.” Support the statement with suitable arguments. OR (b) “Minerals are found in various forms and types, each having distinct properties.” Support the statement with suitable arguments.
Answer
Answer as text
(a) (i) Energy is a basic requirement for economic development; every sector (agriculture, industry, transport, commerce, households) needs inputs of energy. (ii) Consumption of energy has been rising steadily since Independence, while the main sources (coal, petroleum, natural gas) are finite and non-renewable. (iii) Burning fossil fuels causes air pollution and environmental damage, so their unchecked use is not sustainable. (iv) India is one of the least energy-efficient countries, so a cautious approach is needed: promote energy conservation and increase the use of renewable sources (solar, wind, tidal, biomass). (v) Simple steps help: using public transport instead of individual vehicles, switching off electricity when not in use, using power-saving devices; “energy saved is energy produced”. OR (b) (i) In igneous and metamorphic rocks, minerals occur in cracks, crevices, faults or joints; smaller occurrences are veins and larger ones lodes, e.g. tin, copper, zinc, lead. (ii) In sedimentary rocks, minerals occur in beds or layers formed by deposition and accumulation, e.g. coal, some iron ore, gypsum, potash salt, sodium salt. (iii) Decomposition of surface rocks and removal of soluble constituents leaves a residual mass containing ores, e.g. bauxite. (iv) Alluvial deposits in sands of valley floors and the base of hills form ‘placer deposits’ of minerals not corroded by water, e.g. gold, silver, tin, platinum. (v) Ocean waters contain vast quantities of minerals (common salt, magnesium, bromine) and ocean beds are rich in manganese nodules. Minerals are also classified by properties as metallic (ferrous, non-ferrous, precious), non-metallic and energy minerals.
