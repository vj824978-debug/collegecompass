Why is the French Revolution of 1789 considered the first clear expression of nationalism ?

CBSE 2026QP 32/3/1Q85 marks
Question 6 · Class 10 Social Science · History: The Rise of Nationalism in Europe
Question as text
(a) Why is the French Revolution of 1789 considered the first clear expression of nationalism ? Explain with examples. [5] OR (b) How did nationalism, aligned with imperialism, lead Europe to disaster in 1914 ? Explain with examples. [5]
Answer
Answer as text
(a) 1. It transferred sovereignty from the monarchy to a body of French citizens; the people were now to constitute the nation and shape its destiny. 2. The ideas of la patrie (the fatherland) and le citoyen (the citizen) emphasised a united community enjoying equal rights under a constitution. 3. A new French flag, the tricolour, replaced the royal standard; the Estates General was elected by active citizens and renamed the National Assembly. 4. New hymns were composed, oaths taken and martyrs commemorated in the name of the nation; a centralised administrative system made uniform laws for all citizens. 5. Internal customs duties and dues were abolished and a uniform system of weights and measures adopted; French was made the common language of the nation, and regional dialects discouraged. The revolutionaries also declared it their mission to liberate the peoples of Europe from despotism. (b) 1. After 1871 the major source of nationalist tension in Europe was the Balkans, a region of geographical and ethnic variation under the control of the Ottoman Empire. 2. The spread of romantic nationalism together with the disintegration of the Ottoman Empire made the region very explosive; Balkan peoples used history to claim independence. 3. The Balkan states were fiercely jealous of each other and each hoped to gain more territory at the expense of the others. 4. The big European powers – Russia, Germany, England and Austro-Hungary – were rivals over trade, colonies, and naval and military might, and each tried to counter the others’ hold over the Balkans. 5. This led to a series of wars in the region, finally culminating in the First World War (1914); nationalism, aligned with imperialism, thus led Europe to disaster.
