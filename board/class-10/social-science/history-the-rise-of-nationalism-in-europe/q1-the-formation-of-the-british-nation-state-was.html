“The formation of the British Nation-State was different from the rest of Europe.” Justify the statement with…

CBSE 2026QP 32/1/1Q75 marks
Question 1 · Class 10 Social Science · History: The Rise of Nationalism in Europe
Question as text
(A) “The formation of the British Nation-State was different from the rest of Europe.” Justify the statement with suitable arguments. OR (B) “Ideas of national unity in the early nineteenth century Europe were closely allied to the ideology of liberalism.” Justify the statement with suitable arguments.
Answer
Answer as text
(A) (i) In Britain the nation-state was not the result of a sudden upheaval or revolution but of a long-drawn-out process. (ii) There was no British nation before the 18th century; the people were English, Welsh, Scot or Irish, with their own cultural and political traditions. (iii) The English nation steadily grew in wealth and power; through the Glorious Revolution of 1688 the English Parliament seized power from the monarchy and became the instrument for building a nation-state with England at its centre. (iv) The Act of Union (1707) between England and Scotland formed the United Kingdom of Great Britain; Scottish culture and political institutions were suppressed, Catholic clans repressed and the Gaelic language banned. (v) Ireland, divided between Catholics and Protestants, was forcibly incorporated in 1801 after a failed revolt (Wolfe Tone, 1798); a new British identity was promoted through the Union Jack, the national anthem (God Save Our Noble King) and the English language. (B) (i) Liberalism (from Latin liber, free) stood for freedom of the individual and equality of all before the law, which the new middle classes linked with a unified nation. (ii) Politically it meant government by consent: an end to autocracy and clerical privileges, a constitution and representative government through parliament. (iii) It stressed the inviolability of private property (though suffrage was limited to propertyowning men, as under the Napoleonic Code). (iv) Economically it meant freedom of markets and abolition of state-imposed restrictions on the movement of goods and capital; the many states, customs barriers, currencies and weights and measures were obstacles to trade. (v) Hence the Zollverein (1834, initiated by Prussia) abolished tariff barriers and reduced the number of currencies from over thirty to two, and railways improved mobility; this economic unity strengthened the wave of nationalist feeling.
