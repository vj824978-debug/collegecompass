How are primary sectors different from secondary sectors?

CBSE 2026QP 32/4/1Q385 marks
Question 11 · Class 10 Social Science · Economics: Sectors of the Indian Economy
Question as text
(a) How are primary sectors different from secondary sectors? Explain with examples. OR (b) How are organized sectors different from unorganized sectors? Explain with examples.
Answer
Answer as text
(a) (i) Primary sector produces goods by exploiting natural resources; secondary sector transforms natural products into other forms through manufacturing. (ii) Examples: agriculture, dairy, fishing, forestry, mining (primary); making sugar from sugarcane, cloth from cotton, bricks, construction (secondary). (iii) Primary sector is also called the agriculture and related sector; secondary sector is called the industrial sector. (iv) Primary activities depend largely on nature (weather, soil); secondary activities depend on factories, machines and workshops. (v) Primary sector supplies raw materials to the secondary sector (e.g. cotton for textile mills); in India the primary sector still employs the largest share of workers. (b) (i) Organised sector: enterprises registered with the government and following its rules; unorganised sector: small, scattered units largely outside government control. (ii) Organised sector gives regular, assured work with fixed hours; unorganised sector jobs are low-paid, irregular and not secure. (iii) Organised workers get paid leave, holidays, provident fund, medical benefits and overtime pay; unorganised workers get no such benefits. (iv) Organised workers are protected by laws such as the Factories Act and Minimum Wages Act; in the unorganised sector rules are not followed and workers can be dismissed any time. (v) Examples: government employees, workers of large factories and companies (organised); small farmers, street vendors, casual and construction labourers, domestic workers (unorganised).
