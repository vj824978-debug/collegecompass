How had globalization begun long before sixteenth century?

CBSE 2026QP 32/1/1Q52 marks
Question 2 · Class 10 Social Science · History: The Making of a Global World
Question as text
(A) How had globalization begun long before sixteenth century? Explain. OR (B) How did trade help to spread diseases during the pre-modern world? Explain.
Answer
Answer as text
(A) (i) Since ancient times travellers, traders, priests and pilgrims travelled vast distances carrying goods, money, values, skills, ideas, inventions and even germs. As early as 3000 BCE an active coastal trade linked the Indus valley civilisations with present-day West Asia. (ii) Cowries from the Maldives reached China and East Africa; the silk routes linked Asia with Europe and northern Africa; foods such as noodles and potatoes travelled between continents — all showing an early interlinked world. (B) (i) Traders, travellers and conquerors carried germs along trade routes; in the sixteenth century the Spanish conquerors carried smallpox germs to America, where the original inhabitants had no immunity. (ii) Smallpox spread deep into the continent, wiping out whole communities and paving the way for conquest; thus the pre-modern linking of the world through trade also spread deadly diseases.
