Compare the Belgian model and the Sri Lankan model of power sharing.

CBSE 2026QP 32/2/1Q265 marks
Question 4 · Class 10 Social Science · Political Science: Power Sharing
Question as text
(a) Compare the Belgian model and the Sri Lankan model of power sharing. [5] OR (b) Why is power sharing necessary in a democracy ? Explain. [5]
Answer
Answer as text
(a) (i) Belgium accommodated its linguistic diversity; Sri Lanka adopted majoritarianism in favour of the Sinhala community. (ii) Belgium: equal number of Dutch- and French-speaking ministers in the central government; Sri Lanka: Sinhala made the only official language (1956). (iii) Belgium: many powers given to state governments, not subordinate to the Centre; Sri Lanka: preference to Sinhalese in university positions and government jobs, Buddhism protected by the state. (iv) Belgium: special government in Brussels with equal representation, and a ‘community government’ elected by people of each language community. (v) Result: Belgium avoided civic strife and stayed united; in Sri Lanka Tamils felt alienated, leading to distrust and a civil war. (b) Prudential reasons: (i) It reduces the possibility of conflict between social groups. (ii) Social conflict often leads to violence and political instability; power sharing ensures stability of the political order. (iii) Imposing the will of the majority undermines the unity of the nation (tyranny of the majority). Moral reasons: (iv) It is the very spirit of democracy – people affected by policies must have a say in how they are governed. (v) People have a right to be consulted; a legitimate government is one where citizens acquire a stake through participation.
