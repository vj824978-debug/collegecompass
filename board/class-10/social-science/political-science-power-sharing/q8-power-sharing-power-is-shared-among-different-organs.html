Power Sharing Power is shared among different organs of government, such as the legislature, executive and…

CBSE 2026QP 32/4/1Q284 marks
Question 8 · Class 10 Social Science · Political Science: Power Sharing
Question as text
Read the given source carefully and answer the questions that follow: Power Sharing Power is shared among different organs of government, such as the legislature, executive and judiciary. Let us call this horizontal distribution of power because it allows different organs of government placed at the same level to exercise different powers. Such a separation ensures that none of the organs can exercise unlimited power. Each organ checks the others. This results in a balance of power among various institutions. Last year, we studied that in a democracy, even though ministers and government officials exercise power, they are responsible to the Parliament or State Assemblies. Similarly, although judges are appointed by the executive, they can check the functioning of executive or laws made by the legislatures. This arrangement is called a system of checks and balances. 28.1 Explain the term ‘Balance of Power’. [1] 28.2 Why is the judiciary considered independent in a democracy? [1] 28.3 How is power shared among different organs of government? [2]
Answer
Answer as text
28.1 A situation in which no organ of government can exercise unlimited power because each organ checks the others, so power is balanced among the institutions. 28.2 Although judges are appointed by the executive, they can check the functioning of the executive and the laws made by the legislatures. 28.3 Through horizontal distribution of power: the legislature makes laws, the executive implements them and the judiciary interprets them, each at the same level exercising different powers. Ministers are responsible to Parliament/State Assemblies and courts can check the executive and legislature — a system of checks and balances.
