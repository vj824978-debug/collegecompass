‘A’ and ‘B’ are two salts used for washing purposes.

CBSE 2025QP 31/3/1Q374 marks
Question 20 · Class 10 Science · Carbon and its Compounds
Question as text
‘A’ and ‘B’ are two salts used for washing purposes. Salt ‘A’ is used for bathing also. Four test tubes I, II, III and IV as mentioned below are taken. I. Rain water + solution of salt ‘A’ II. Rain water + solution of salt ‘B’ III. Tubewell water + solution of salt ‘A’ IV. Tubewell water + solution of salt ‘B’ The test tubes are shaken one by one almost identically for the same time and the lengths of foam formed in each test tube is noted. (a) In which one of the four test tubes is the foam formed the minimum ? [1] (b) Differentiate between salt A and salt B. [1] (c) (i) What are esters ? What happens when an ester reacts with an alkali (say sodium hydroxide) ? Give chemical equation for the reaction. [2] OR (c) (ii) What is the cause of hardness of water ? Sometimes it is observed that while bathing foam is formed with difficulty and an insoluble substance is formed. Name this substance and write the cause of its formation. [2]
Answer
Answer as text
(a) Test tube III (hard tubewell water with soap A). (b) A is soap – sodium/potassium salt of a long-chain carboxylic acid, forms scum with hard water. B is detergent – ammonium/sulphonate salt of long-chain carboxylic acid, works well even in hard water (no scum). (c) (i) Esters are sweet-smelling compounds formed by the reaction of a carboxylic acid with an alcohol (in presence of an acid catalyst). With NaOH an ester is hydrolysed to the alcohol and the sodium salt of the carboxylic acid (saponification): CH 3COOC2H5 + NaOH−−→CH3COONa + C2H5OH. OR (c) (ii) Hardness is due to dissolved calcium and magnesium salts (hydrogencarbonates, chlorides, sulphates). The insoluble substance is scum: soap reacts with Ca 2+/Mg2+ ions to form insoluble calcium/magnesium salts of fatty acids, so more soap is wasted before foam forms.
