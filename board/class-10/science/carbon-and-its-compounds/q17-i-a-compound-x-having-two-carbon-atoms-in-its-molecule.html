Question 17 · Class 10 Science · Carbon and its Compounds
Question as text
(a) (i) A compound ‘X’ having two carbon atoms in its molecule turns blue litmus red and 5 – 8% solution of ‘X’ in water is widely used as a preservative. Identify the compound ‘X’ and write its structure. [1 1 2 ] (ii) Compare its pH nature with a mineral acid. [ 1 2 ] (iii) ‘X’ on reacting with alcohols produces sweet smelling compounds, used in making perfumes. Name the reaction and write its chemical equation. [1 1 2 ] (iv) When sodium carbonate is added to ‘X’, a colourless gas is produced which turns lime water milky. Write the chemical equation for the reaction giving the name of the salt produced. [1 1 2 ] OR (b) (i) Differentiate between saturated and unsaturated hydrocarbons by giving one example each, with a structural formula. (ii) Write the method of converting an unsaturated hydrocarbon into a saturated hydrocarbon. Name the industry where this reaction is commonly used. (iii) Write the name and structure of a hydrocarbon having double bond and four carbon atoms in its one molecule.
Answer
Answer as text
(a) (i) X = ethanoic acid (acetic acid), CH 3COOH (5–8% solution is vinegar). Structure: H C H H C O O H (ii) It is a weak acid, so its pH is higher (less acidic) than that of a mineral acid like HCl of the same concentration. (iii) Esterification: CH 3COOH + C2H5OH acid−−→CH3COOC2H5 + H2O (ethyl ethanoate, an ester). (iv) 2 CH3COOH + Na2CO3−−→2 CH3COONa + H2O + CO2↑ ; salt: sodium ethanoate (sodium acetate); CO2 turns lime water milky. OR (b) (i) Saturated hydrocarbons have only single C–C bonds, e.g. ethane C 2H6; unsaturated hydrocarbons have at least one double/triple C–C bond, e.g. ethene C 2H4. H C H H C H H H H C H C H H ethane (saturated) ethene (unsaturated) (ii) Hydrogenation: adding hydrogen in the presence of a catalyst such as nickel or palladium, e.g. CH2 ––CH2 +H2 Ni−−→CH3 –CH 3. Used in the vegetable oil (vanaspati ghee) industry to make vegetable ghee from oils. (iii) Butene (but-1-ene), C 4H8, i.e. CH 2 ––CH–CH 2 –CH 3: H C H C H C H H C H H H
