The combining capacity of various elements depends on the number of valence electrons.

CBSE 2025QP 31/5/1Q374 marks
Question 25 · Class 10 Science · Carbon and its Compounds
Question as text
The combining capacity of various elements depends on the number of valence electrons. Also the reactivity of elements is explained as their tendency to attain a completely filled outer shell, that is, to attain a noble gas configuration. This may be either through gain of electrons or loss of electrons or sharing of electrons. (a) An element A has atomic number 16, how will it attain its nearest noble gas configuration ? (b) Write the number of (i) single and (ii) double covalent bonds in a molecule of butene (C 4H8). (c) (A) Explain the formation of a molecule of ammonia (NH 3), using electron dot structure. (Atomic number of nitrogen is 7) OR (c) (B) Why does carbon share its valence electrons with other atoms of carbon or with atoms of other elements ?
Answer
Answer as text
(a) A (sulphur) has configuration 2, 8, 6; it gains 2 electrons (forming A 2– , 2, 8, 8) to attain the argon configuration (or shares 2 electrons in covalent compounds). (b) Butene, CH 2 ––CH–CH 2 –CH 3: (i) 10 single bonds (8 C–H + 2 C–C); (ii) 1 double bond (C=C). (c) (A) N (2, 5) has 5 valence electrons and needs 3 more; it shares one electron pair with each of three H atoms, forming three N–H single bonds; N gets an octet (with one lone pair) and each H gets a duplet. Electron dot: three shared pairs between N and the three H atoms, one lone pair on N. OR (c) (B) Carbon has 4 valence electrons. It cannot gain 4 electrons (C 4– : six protons cannot hold ten electrons) nor lose 4 electrons (C 4+: needs a lot of energy). So it attains the noble gas configuration by sharing its valence electrons, forming covalent bonds.
