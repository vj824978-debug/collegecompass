Why do we balance a chemical equation?

CBSE 2025QP 31/4/1Q273 marks
Question 27 · Class 10 Science · Chemical Reactions and Equations
Question as text
(A) Why do we balance a chemical equation? Name and state the law that suggests the balancing of a chemical equation? Balance the following chemical equation: Zn + H3PO4−−→Zn3(PO4)2 + H2 OR (B) Define a precipitation reaction. Give its example and also express the reaction that occurs in the form of a balanced chemical equation.
Answer
Answer as text
(A) A chemical equation is balanced so that the number of atoms of each element is the same on both sides, as mass can neither be created nor destroyed. Law of conservation of mass: in a chemical reaction the total mass of reactants equals the total mass of products. 3 Zn + 2 H3PO4−−→Zn3(PO4)2 + 3 H2 (B) A reaction in which an insoluble substance (precipitate) is formed is called a precipitation reaction. Example: sodium sulphate solution + barium chloride solution gives a white precipitate of barium sulphate: Na2SO4(aq) + BaCl2(aq)−−→BaSO4(s)↓ + 2 NaCl(aq)
