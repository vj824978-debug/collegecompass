CBSE 2025QP 31/3/1Q365 marks
Question 23 · Class 10 Science · Light - Reflection and Refraction
Question as text
(a) (i) “In refraction of light through a rectangular glass slab, the emergent ray is always parallel to the direction of the incident ray.” Why ? Explain with the help of a ray diagram. What happens when a ray of light falls normally on one of the faces of a rectangular glass prism ? Draw diagram. (ii) An object is placed at a distance of 30 cm from the optical centre of a concave lens of focal length 20 cm. Use Lens formula to determine the position of the image formed in this case. OR (b) (i) A student wishes to study the image formation by a concave mirror using candle flame as object. State the type of the image formed by the mirror and mention the change in the image formed, if any, that he observes when the candle flame is gradually moved away from the pole of the mirror. Draw a ray diagram to show the image formation when the object distance is nearly equal to the radius of curvature of the mirror. (ii) A convex mirror used for rear-view on an automobile has a focal length of 3·0 m. If a bus is located at 6·0 m from this mirror, use mirror formula to find the position of the image of the bus as seen in the mirror.
Answer
Answer as text
(a) (i) The extent of bending at the air–glass surface (entry) is equal and opposite to that at the glass–air surface (exit), since the two faces are parallel and the media are the same; so the angle of emergence equals the angle of incidence and the emergent ray is parallel to the incident ray (only laterally displaced). Diagram: glass slab with incident ray, refracted ray bending towards normal, emergent ray bending away from normal parallel to incident ray; ∠i = ∠e, lateral displacement marked. A ray falling normally (i = 0◦) passes through without any bending. Diagram: ray perpendicular to the face going straight through the slab. (ii) u =−30 cm, f =−20 cm. 1 v = 1 f + 1 u =− 1 20− 1 30 =− 5 60, so v =−12 cm: image 12 cm from the lens on the same side as the object (virtual, erect, diminished). OR (b) (i) For candle beyond F the image is real and inverted (it can be obtained on a screen). As the flame is moved away from the pole (from just beyond F towards and beyond C), the real image moves closer to the mirror (towards F) and becomes smaller (enlarged → same size at C → diminished). (Between P and F the image is virtual, erect and enlarged, and grows larger as the object approaches F.) Diagram: object at C; a ray parallel to the axis reflects through F, a ray through F reflects parallel; image formed at C, real, inverted, same size. (ii) f = +3.0 m, u =−6.0 m. 1 v = 1 f− 1 u = 1 3 + 1 6 = 1 2, so v = +2.0 m: image 2 m behind the mirror (virtual, erect, diminished).
