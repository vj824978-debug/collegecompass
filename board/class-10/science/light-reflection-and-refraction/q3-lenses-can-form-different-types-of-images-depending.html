Lenses can form different types of images depending upon their focal length and position of object.

CBSE 2026QP 31/1/1Q384 marks
Question 3 · Class 10 Science · Light - Reflection and Refraction
Question as text
Read the following passage and answer the questions given : Lenses can form different types of images depending upon their focal length and position of object. A convex lens can create real, inverted or virtual, erect images, while a concave lens forms only virtual and diminished images. The focal length determines the power of lens. Convex lenses have positive focal length while concave lenses have negative focal length by convention. When lenses are placed together, their combined power is determined by the sum of their individual powers. Ray diagrams help to visualize how light converges or diverges through lens to form an image. (a) A convex lens of focal length 20 cm is used to form an image. If an object is placed at 40 cm from the lens, what will be the position and nature of image ? [1] (b) Illustrate the formation of image with the help of ray diagram, when the object is placed between the optical centre and principal focus of concave lens. [1] (c) (i) A lens combination consists of a convex lens of focal length 30 cm and a concave lens of focal length 15 cm placed together. Find the equivalent focal length and power of this lens combination. [2] OR (c) (ii) Two lenses are placed in contact. One is a concave lens with focal length 2 m and the other is a convex lens with focal length 1.5 m. What type of lens will the combination behave as (convex or concave) ? Give reason. [2]
Answer
Answer as text
(a) u =−40 cm = 2 F : 1 v = 1 f + 1 u = 1 20− 1 40 = 1 40, v = +40 cm. Image at 2 F2 (40 cm behind the lens), real, inverted, same size. (b) Diagram: concave lens, object between O and F 1; ray parallel to axis diverges and appears to come from F1; ray through O goes undeviated; image is virtual, erect, diminished, between F 1 and O on the object side. (c)(i) P1 = 100 30 = +3.33 D, P2 = 100 −15 =−6.67 D; P = P1 + P2 =−3.33 D. f = 1 P =−0.30 m =−30 cm (behaves as a concave lens). OR (c)(ii) P =−1 2 + 1 1.5 =−0.5 + 0.67 = +0 .17 D ( f = +6 m). The net power is positive, so the combination behaves as a convex (converging) lens; the convex lens has the shorter focal length, i.e. greater power.
