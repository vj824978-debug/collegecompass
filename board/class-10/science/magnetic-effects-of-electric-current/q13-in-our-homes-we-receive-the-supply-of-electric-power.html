Question as text
In our homes, we receive the supply of electric power through a main supply also called mains, either supported through overhead electric poles or by underground cables. In our country the potential difference between the two wires (live wire and neutral wire) of this supply is 220 V. (a) Write the colours of the insulation covers of the line wires through which supply comes to our homes. [1] (b) What should be the current rating of the electric circuit (220 V) so that an electric iron of 1 kW power rating can be operated ? [1] (c) (i) What is the function of the earth wire ? State the advantage of the earth wire in domestic electric appliances such as electric iron. [2] OR (c) (ii) List two precautions to be taken to avoid electrical accidents. State how these precautions prevent possible damage to the circuit/appliance. [2]
Answer
Answer as text
(a) Live wire: red insulation; neutral wire: black insulation (earth wire: green). (b) I = P V = 1000 220 ≈ 4.5 A, so a circuit of current rating 5 A is needed. (c) (i) The earth wire (green) connects the metallic body of the appliance to a metal plate deep in the earth; it provides a low-resistance conducting path for current. Advantage: if live wire touches the metal body due to leakage, the current flows to earth and the user does not get a severe electric shock. OR (c) (ii) (1) Use an electric fuse (of proper rating) in each circuit – it melts and breaks the circuit when excess current flows due to overloading or short-circuiting. (2) Do not connect too many appliances to a single socket (avoid overloading) and use earthing for metal-bodied appliances – this prevents excess current/overheating and damage to appliances and wiring.
