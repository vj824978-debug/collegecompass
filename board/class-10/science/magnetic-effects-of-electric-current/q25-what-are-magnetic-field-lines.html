What are magnetic field lines ?

CBSE 2025QP 31/6/1Q365 marks
Question 25 · Class 10 Science · Magnetic Effects of Electric Current
Question as text
(a) What are magnetic field lines ? How is the direction of magnetic field at a point determined ? Draw the pattern of magnetic field lines of the magnetic field produced by a current carrying circular loop. Mark on it the direction of (i) current and (ii) magnetic field lines. Name the two factors on which the magnitude of the magnetic field due to a current carrying coil depends. OR (b) Why can’t two magnetic field lines cross each other ? Draw magnetic field lines showing the direction of the magnetic field due to a current carrying long straight solenoid. State the conclusion which can be drawn from the pattern of magnetic field lines inside the solenoid. Name any two factors on which the magnitude of the magnetic field due to this solenoid depends.
Answer
Answer as text
(a) Magnetic field lines are the imaginary closed curves along which the north pole of a small compass would move in a magnetic field. The direction of the field at a point is the direction in which the north pole of a compass needle placed there points, i.e. the tangent to the field line at that point. Diagram: a circular loop with current marked; near each point of the wire the field lines are concentric circles, which become larger and nearly straight towards the centre; at the centre the lines are straight and perpendicular to the plane of the loop. Directions given by the right-hand thumb rule (current going up on one side gives lines anticlockwise around it, and opposite on the other side). Factors: the current flowing through the coil, and the number of turns of the coil (field also decreases as the radius increases). OR (b) If two field lines crossed, the compass needle at the point of intersection would point in two directions at once, which is impossible; so they never cross. Diagram: a solenoid with current direction marked; field lines inside are parallel straight lines; outside they curve from one end (N) to the other (S) like those of a bar magnet. Conclusion: the magnetic field inside the solenoid is uniform (same at all points). Factors (any two): number of turns of the solenoid; strength of current; nature of core material (soft iron core increases it).
