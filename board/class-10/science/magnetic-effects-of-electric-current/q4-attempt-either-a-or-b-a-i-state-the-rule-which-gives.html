Question 4 · Class 10 Science · Magnetic Effects of Electric Current
Question as text
Attempt either (a) or (b): (a) (i) State the rule which gives the direction of force acting on a current-carrying conductor placed in a magnetic field. (ii) What is a fuse? Why is it called a safety device? (iii) Why are electrical appliances with metallic bodies connected to the mains through a three-pin plug, whereas an electric bulb is connected with a two-pin plug? (iv) What will be the direction of force experienced by the conductor AB due to the magnetic field, when current flows in the direction shown? Name the law you applied to find the direction. [5] OR (b) (i) Draw the pattern of magnetic field lines through and around a current-carrying circular loop. (ii) State the rule to find out the direction of the magnetic field inside and around the circular loop. (iii) Give reasons: (I) Why should a burnt-out fuse be replaced by another fuse of identical rating only, in an electric circuit? (II) Why is soft iron-core used in making an electromagnet? [5]
Answer
Answer as text
(a)(i) Fleming’s left-hand rule: stretch the thumb, forefinger and middle finger of the left hand mutually perpendicular; if the forefinger points along the magnetic field and the middle finger along the current, the thumb gives the direction of the force (motion) on the conductor. (ii) A fuse is a wire of a material of low melting point (and suitable resistance) connected in series with the circuit. When an unduly high current flows (overloading/short-circuit), it heats up, melts and breaks the circuit, protecting the appliances and wiring from damage and fire – hence a safety device. (iii) The third pin connects the metallic body to the earth wire, which provides a low-resistance path for any leakage current, keeping the body at earth potential and preventing a severe shock. A bulb has no exposed metallic body that can become live, so earthing is not needed and a two-pin plug suffices. (iv) Current is from B to A (upwards) and the field is from left to right, so the force on AB is perpendicular to the plane of the paper, directed into the paper (inwards). Law applied: Fleming’s left-hand rule. OR (b)(i) Diagram: a circular loop carrying current, with field lines as concentric circles around each point where the wire crosses the plane; the circles become larger away from the wire, and near the centre of the loop the lines are nearly straight and parallel, perpendicular to the plane of the loop (all pointing in the same direction inside the loop). (ii) Right-hand thumb rule: hold the current-carrying wire in the right hand with the thumb pointing along the current; the curled fingers give the direction of the magnetic field lines around it. Applied to each part of the loop, all segments give field in the same direction inside the loop. (iii)(I) A fuse of higher rating will not melt when the current exceeds the safe limit, so appliances may be damaged; one of lower rating will melt even at normal current. Only the identical rating protects the circuit properly. (II) Soft iron is easily and strongly magnetised when current flows through the coil and loses its magnetism as soon as the current is switched off, so it makes a strong temporary magnet.
