Question 14 · Class 10 Science · Magnetic Effects of Electric Current
Question as text
(a) The given figure shows the current passing through the straight conductor XY. (i) Copy the diagram and draw the magnetic field lines when current flows from conductor X to Y. (ii) Name and state the rule used in determining the direction of the magnetic field lines in the situation given above. (iii) State Fleming’s left hand rule. Using this rule, determine the direction of force applied on an electron entering a uniform magnetic field as shown in the figure. OR (b) (i) Define the term solenoid. Draw the pattern of the magnetic field lines in and around a current carrying straight solenoid. Mark on the pattern the (i) direction of current, (ii) direction of field lines near the ends of the solenoid, and (iii) region where the magnetic field is uniform. (ii) How would you make an electromagnet using a current carrying solenoid ?
Answer
Answer as text
(a) (i) Diagram: concentric circles on the plane Q centred on the wire XY, closer together near the wire. With current downward (X to Y), the field lines are clockwise when viewed from above. (ii) Right-hand thumb rule: if a current-carrying straight conductor is held in the right hand with the thumb pointing in the direction of current, the fingers wrapped around the conductor give the direction of the magnetic field lines. (iii) Fleming’s left-hand rule: stretch the thumb, forefinger and middle finger of the left hand mutually perpendicular; if the forefinger points along the magnetic field and the middle finger along the current, the thumb gives the direction of motion/force on the conductor. The electron moves upward (in the plane of paper), so the conventional current is downward; field is to the right. Hence the force on the electron is perpendicular to the plane of the paper, directed outward (towards the reader). OR (b) (i) A solenoid is a coil of many circular turns of insulated copper wire wrapped closely in the shape of a cylinder. Diagram: field lines like a bar magnet’s – parallel straight lines inside the solenoid (uniform field), emerging from one end (N) and entering the other end (S) outside; mark current direction in the turns, arrows on field lines leaving the N end and entering the S end, and the uniform region inside. (ii) Place a soft iron core inside the solenoid and pass current; the strong field inside magnetises the core, forming an electromagnet (magnetic only while current flows).
