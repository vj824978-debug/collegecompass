Question 23 · Class 10 Science · Magnetic Effects of Electric Current
Question as text
In order to obtain magnetic field lines around a bar magnet, a student performed an experiment using a magnetic compass and a bar magnet. The magnet was placed on a sheet of white paper fixed on a drawing board. Using magnetic needle he obtained on the paper a pattern of magnetic field lines (as shown below) around the bar magnet. (a) By convention, the field lines emerge from north pole and merge at south pole. Why ? Give reason. (b) State the relationship between strength of the magnetic field and the degree of closeness of the field lines. (c) (A) (i) No two field lines can ever intersect each other. Give reason. (ii) The magnetic field in a given region is uniform. Draw a diagram to represent it. OR (c) (B) Draw the pattern of the magnetic field lines through and around a current carrying solenoid. What does the pattern of field lines inside the solenoid represent ?
Answer
Answer as text
(a) The direction of a field line is the direction in which the north pole of a compass needle moves; it is repelled by the N-pole and attracted by the S-pole of the magnet, so outside the magnet field lines emerge from N and merge at S (inside the magnet they go from S to N, forming closed curves). (b) The closer the field lines, the stronger the magnetic field (the field is strongest near the poles, where lines are most crowded). (c) (A) (i) If two field lines intersected, the compass needle at that point would point in two directions, i.e. the field would have two directions at one point, which is not possible. (ii) Diagram: parallel, equally spaced straight lines with arrows all in the same direction. OR (c) (B) Diagram: solenoid with field lines like those of a bar magnet — parallel straight lines inside, emerging from one end (N) and curving round outside to enter the other end (S). The parallel straight lines inside show that the magnetic field inside the solenoid is uniform (same at all points).
