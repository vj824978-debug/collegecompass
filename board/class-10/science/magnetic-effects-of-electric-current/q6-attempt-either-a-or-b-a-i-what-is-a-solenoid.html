Attempt either (a) or (b): (a) (i) What is a solenoid?

CBSE 2026QP 31/3/1Q395 marks
Question 6 · Class 10 Science · Magnetic Effects of Electric Current
Question as text
Attempt either (a) or (b): (a) (i) What is a solenoid? Draw a diagram showing the magnetic field pattern due to a currentcarrying solenoid (inside and outside). (ii) Name any two factors on which the magnitude of the magnetic field due to a current-carrying solenoid depends. (iii) A current-carrying solenoid is stretched and converted into a straight wire carrying current. Draw a diagram showing the magnetic field pattern around it and mark the direction of the magnetic field. OR (b) (i) With the help of a diagram, show that a current-carrying conductor placed in an external magnetic field, experiences a force. Indicate the direction of the field, current and force clearly. (ii) The diagram given below shows an electron moving in a uniform magnetic field. (I) What will be the direction of the force acting on the electron? (II) What will happen to the direction of the force acting on the electron, if the direction of the magnetic field as well as the direction of motion of the electron, both are reversed?
Answer
Answer as text
(a) (i) A solenoid is a coil of many circular turns of insulated copper wire wrapped closely in the shape of a cylinder. Diagram: field lines inside the solenoid are parallel straight lines (uniform field); outside, they curve from one end (N) to the other (S), like a bar magnet; direction of current and N, S ends marked. (ii) (1) number of turns (per unit length) of the solenoid; (2) strength of current through it (also: nature of core material, e.g. soft iron). (iii) Diagram: concentric circles around the straight wire in a plane perpendicular to it, closer together near the wire; direction given by the right-hand thumb rule (e.g. current upward⇒ field lines anticlockwise when seen from above). OR (b) (i) Diagram/activity: an aluminium rod suspended horizontally between the poles of a horseshoe magnet (e.g. north pole below, south pole above, so the field is vertically upward); when current flows through the rod it is displaced; field, current and force are mutually perpendicular, as given by Fleming’s left-hand rule (forefinger – field, middle finger – current, thumb – force). Reversing the current reverses the displacement. (ii) (I) The electron moves upward, so the conventional current is downward; the field is towards the left. By Fleming’s left-hand rule the force on the electron is perpendicular to the plane of the paper, directed into the paper. (II) Reversing both the field and the direction of motion leaves the direction of force unchanged – it remains into the plane of the paper.
