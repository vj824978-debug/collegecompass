Question 14 · Class 10 Science · The Human Eye and the Colourful World
Question as text
(A) (i) How does the change in curvature of the eye lens helps us in the process of seeing the nearby objects clearly ? (ii) State the range of the power of accommodation of a normal human eye. OR (B) Draw a ray diagram to show the correction of eye defect of an old man who can not see an object placed closer than 1 m from his eye, clearly.
Answer
Answer as text
(A) (i) For nearby objects the ciliary muscles contract, the eye lens becomes thicker (more curved), its focal length decreases, so the image of the near object is formed on the retina. (ii) From 25 cm (near point) to infinity (far point). OR (B) The defect is hypermetropia (presbyopia), near point N ′ at 1 m. Diagram: (a) object at N (25 cm) – rays focus behind the retina; (b) a convex lens placed before the eye makes rays from N appear to come from N ′ (1 m), so the image forms on the retina. Labels: N, N ′, convex lens, retina.
