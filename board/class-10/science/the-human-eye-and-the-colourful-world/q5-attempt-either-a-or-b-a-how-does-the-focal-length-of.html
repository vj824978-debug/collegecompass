Attempt either (a) or (b): (a) How does the focal length of eye lens change as the distance of the object from the eyes is altered? Explain. OR (b) Why is the concave lens used as a corrective lens for a myopic eye?
Answer
Answer as text
(a) By accommodation, through the ciliary muscles. For a distant object the ciliary muscles relax, the lens becomes thin and its focal length increases; for a nearby object the muscles contract, the lens becomes thicker and its focal length decreases. Thus the image always forms on the retina. OR (b) In myopia the image of a distant object forms in front of the retina (excessive curvature of eye lens or elongated eyeball). A concave lens of suitable power diverges the incoming rays so that it forms a virtual image of the distant object at the far point of the eye; the eye then focuses the image on the retina.
