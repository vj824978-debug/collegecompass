Question 24 · Class 10 Science · How do Organisms Reproduce?
Question as text
(a) (i) Identify the parts ‘X’ and ‘Y’ in the figure given below : (ii) Name the yellowish coloured structures produced by the part labelled as ‘Y’. (iii) Write the name of the process by which these are transferred to the part labelled as ‘X’. (iv) Explain the process of seed formation in a flowering plant. OR (b) (i) Name the type of asexual mode of reproduction shown in the given figure. (ii) Identify the unicellular organism in the diagram. (iii) List any two advantages of asexual reproduction over sexual reproduction. (iv) Name and explain any one mode of asexual reproduction observed in Hydra.
Answer
Answer as text
(a) (i) X – stigma; Y – anther. (ii) Pollen grains. (iii) Pollination. (iv) After pollination a pollen tube grows from the pollen grain on the stigma, through the style, to the ovule in the ovary. The male germ cell fuses with the female germ cell (egg) in the ovule (fertilisation) to form a zygote. The zygote divides repeatedly to form an embryo within the ovule; the ovule develops a tough coat and becomes the seed, while the ovary grows into the fruit. OR (b) (i) Binary fission (longitudinal, along a definite plane). (ii) Leishmania. (iii) Only one parent is needed; it is faster, producing many offspring quickly (offspring are identical, preserving useful traits). (iv) Budding: a bud develops as an outgrowth at a specific site due to repeated cell division; it grows into a tiny individual and, when mature, detaches from the parent to live independently. (Or regeneration: a cut body piece grows into a complete organism.)
