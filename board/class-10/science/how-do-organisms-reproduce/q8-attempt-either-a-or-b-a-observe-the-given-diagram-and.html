Question 8 · Class 10 Science · How do Organisms Reproduce?
Question as text
Attempt either (a) or (b): (a) Observe the given diagram and answer the questions that follow: (i) Give the name of the structure and type of reproduction shown in the above diagram. (ii) State one advantage and one disadvantage of the above mentioned method of reproduction. (iii) Give reason: Asexual reproduction lacks genetic variation. (iv) Why have higher organisms resorted to sexual reproduction in spite of its complexity? OR (b) Observe the given diagram and answer the questions that follow: (i) Give the name and type of reproduction shown in the above diagram. (ii) Comment on the processes taking place in stage P. (iii) Name an organism which reproduces by the above mentioned method. (iv) Name the method that can be used to raise plants that have lost their capacity to produce seeds. List any two advantages of growing plants by such a method.
Answer
Answer as text
(a) (i) Leaf of Bryophyllum; asexual reproduction by vegetative propagation (buds in the notches along the leaf margin develop into new plants). (ii) Advantage: plants are produced quickly, bear flowers and fruits earlier, and all are genetically identical to the parent (desirable traits preserved). Disadvantage: no genetic variation, so the plants cannot adapt to changing conditions and may all be wiped out by a disease. (iii) Only one parent is involved and new individuals are formed by mitotic division/DNA copying of the parent’s body cells without fusion of gametes, so offspring are nearly identical to the parent. (iv) Sexual reproduction combines DNA from two individuals and involves meiosis, producing new combinations of variations; variation helps species survive in changing environments and is the basis for evolution. OR (b) (i) Multiple fission (in Plasmodium); asexual reproduction. (ii) In stage P, under unfavourable conditions the cell secretes a protective cyst wall; inside the cyst the nucleus divides repeatedly to form many daughter nuclei, and each nucleus gets surrounded by a bit of cytoplasm, forming many daughter cells. When conditions are favourable the cyst breaks and the daughter cells are released. (iii) Plasmodium (malarial parasite). (iv) Vegetative propagation (e.g. layering, grafting, cutting) – used for banana, orange, rose, jasmine. Advantages: plants bear flowers and fruits earlier than those from seeds; all plants are genetically similar to the parent, so desirable characters are preserved.
