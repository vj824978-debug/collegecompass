Question as text
In xylem tissue, vessels and tracheids of the roots, stems and leaves are interconnected to form a continuous system of water-conducting channels reaching all parts of the plant. Explain how this is achieved.
Answer
Answer as text
Water moves up through this continuous xylem system as follows: (1) Root cells in contact with soil actively take up ions; this creates a difference in ion concentration between the root and the soil, so water moves into the root xylem from the soil, forming a steady column of water pushed upward (root pressure) – important mainly at night. (2) Water evaporates from the aerial parts (leaves) through stomata (transpiration); this loss creates a suction that pulls water up from the xylem cells of the roots (transpiration pull) – the major driving force during the day. (3) Because the vessels and tracheids are connected end to end from root to leaf, the water column is unbroken, so water and dissolved minerals reach all parts of the plant.
