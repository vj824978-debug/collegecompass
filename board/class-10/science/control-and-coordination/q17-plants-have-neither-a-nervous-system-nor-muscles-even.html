Plants have neither a nervous system nor muscles, even then they respond to stimuli.

CBSE 2025QP 31/1/1Q293 marks
Question 17 · Class 10 Science · Control and Coordination
Question as text
Plants have neither a nervous system nor muscles, even then they respond to stimuli. For example, the leaves of chhui-mui (touch-me-not) plant when touched begin to fold up and droop. (a) How is the information communicated in “touch-me-not” plants ? (b) What enables the plant cells to bring out the observable response ? (c) Differentiate the movement mentioned above from the movement of tendrils in a pea plant.
Answer
Answer as text
(a) By electrical-chemical means from cell to cell (there is no specialised tissue for conduction). (b) Cells change shape by changing the amount of water in them, so they swell or shrink. (c) Touch-me-not: movement in response to touch, independent of growth, quick. Pea tendrils: growth-dependent, slow directional movement – the part away from the support grows faster, so the tendril coils around the support (thigmotropism).
