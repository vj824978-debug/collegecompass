Explain how did the experiment conducted by S.L. Miller substantiate that life evolved from preexisting non-living organism molecules. [3]
Answer
Answer as text
In 1953, S.L. Miller recreated the conditions of the primitive Earth on a laboratory scale: a closed flask containing CH 4, H2, NH3 and water vapour (a reducing atmosphere) at 800 ° C. Electric discharge (spark) from electrodes was passed through the mixture, as an energy source like lightning. After about a week he observed the formation of amino acids; similar experiments yielded sugars, nitrogen bases, pigments and fats. This showed that organic molecules can form abiotically from inorganic substances, supporting the Oparin–Haldane idea of chemical evolution, i.e. that the first life arose from pre-existing non-living organic molecules.
