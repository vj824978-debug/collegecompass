Question as text
With the help of an algebraic equation, how did Hardy-Weinberg explain that in a given population the frequency of occurrence of alleles of a gene is supposed to remain the same through generations? [2]
Answer
Answer as text
In a diploid population let the frequency of allele A be p and of allele a be q; then p + q = 1. With random mating the expected genotype frequencies are AA = p2, aa = q2, Aa = 2pq. Hence (p + q)2 = p2 + 2pq + q2 = 1. In the absence of selection, mutation, gene flow or genetic drift, p and q (and so these genotype frequencies) stay constant through generations.
