How does the process of Natural Selection affect Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium?

CBSE 2025Outside Delhi Set 1Q253 marks
Question 5 · Class 12 Biology · Evolution
Question as text
How does the process of Natural Selection affect Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium? Explain with the help of graphs.
Answer
Answer as text
Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium (allele frequencies constant over generations) holds only when no evolutionary force acts. Natural selection gives some genotypes greater reproductive success, so allele/genotype frequencies change and the equilibrium is disturbed, i.e. evolution occurs. It acts in three ways: (i) Stabilising selection: the mean (average) phenotype is favoured; more individuals acquire the mean value; the curve becomes narrower and taller around the same peak. (ii) Directional selection: one extreme phenotype is favoured; more individuals acquire a value other than the mean; the peak shifts towards one side. (iii) Disruptive selection: both extremes are favoured over the mean; the curve develops two peaks (bimodal). Diagram: three graphs of number of individuals (y-axis) against phenotype (x-axis), each showing the original curve and the curve after selection as described above, labelled stabilising, directional and disruptive.
