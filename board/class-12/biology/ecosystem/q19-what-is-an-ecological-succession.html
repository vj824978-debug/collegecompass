What is an ecological succession?

CBSE 2020Outside Delhi Set 2Q265 marksOut of current syllabus
Question 19 · Class 12 Biology · Ecosystem
Question as text
(a) What is an ecological succession? (b) Differentiate between primary and secondary succession. Why is secondary succession faster than primary succession? Explain with suitable examples. (c) What are pioneer species? Give examples of pioneer species in Xerarch and Hydrarch successions respectively. [5]
Answer
Answer as text
(Out of syllabus; no official answer.) (a) Ecological succession is the gradual and fairly predictable change in species composition of an area, leading finally to a climax community in equilibrium with the environment. (b) Primary succession begins in an area where no living organisms or soil existed (bare rock, newly cooled lava, new pond); it is very slow (soil must be formed). Secondary succession begins where a community was destroyed (abandoned farmland, burnt or cut forest, flooded land) but soil remains. It is faster because soil, nutrients and seeds/propagules are already present. (c) Pioneer species are the first species to invade a bare area. Xerarch: lichens (on bare rock); Hydrarch: phytoplankton (in water).
