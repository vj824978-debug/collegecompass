Immunity in our body is of two types: (i) Innate immunity and (ii) acquired immunity.

CBSE 2025Outside Delhi Set 1Q294 marks
Question 9 · Class 12 Biology · Human Health and Disease
Question as text
Immunity in our body is of two types: (i) Innate immunity and (ii) acquired immunity. Innate immunity is a non-specific defence mechanism, whereas acquired immunity is pathogen-specific; it is called specific immunity too. Acquired immunity is characterised by memory. Antibodies are specific to antigens and there are different types of antibodies produced in our body: they are IgA, IgE, IgG and IgM. It shows primary response when it encounters the pathogen for the first time and secondary response during the subsequent encounters with the same Antigen/Pathogen. (a) Name the two types of specialised cells which carry out the primary and secondary immune response. (b) Why is the antibody-mediated immunity also called as humoral immune response? Attempt either sub-part (c) or (d): (c) The organ transplants are often rejected if not taken from suitable compatible persons. (i) Mention the characteristic of our immune system that is responsible for the graft rejection. (ii) Name the type of immune response and the cell involved in it. OR (d) How is active immunity different from passive immunity?
Answer
Answer as text
(a) B-lymphocytes and T-lymphocytes (memory B and T cells give the secondary response). (b) Antibodies produced by B-cells are found in the blood/body fluids (humors), so this response is called humoral immune response. (c) (i) Ability of the immune system to distinguish ‘self’ from ‘non-self’. (ii) Cell-mediated immune response (CMI), by T-lymphocytes. OR (d) Active immunity: the body produces its own antibodies on exposure to antigen (infection/vaccine); slow, long-lasting, with memory. Passive immunity: ready-made antibodies are given (e.g. colostrum, placenta, antitoxin); quick but short-lived.
