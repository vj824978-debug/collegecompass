Question as text
Describe the process of secondary treatment given to municipal waste water (sewage) before it can be released into fresh waterbodies. Mention another benefit provided by this process.
Answer
Answer as text
Secondary (biological) treatment: – Primary effluent is passed into large aeration tanks, constantly agitated mechanically with air pumped in; useful aerobic microbes grow vigorously as flocs (bacteria held by fungal filaments). – The microbes consume the major part of the organic matter, significantly reducing the BOD. – When BOD is sufficiently reduced, the effluent is passed into a settling tank where flocs sediment as activated sludge; a small part is returned to the aeration tank as inoculum. – The major part of the sludge is pumped into anaerobic sludge digesters, where anaerobic bacteria digest the bacteria and fungi of the sludge. – The effluent from the secondary treatment plant is released into natural water bodies (rivers, streams). Another benefit: the digestion produces biogas (methane, H 2S, CO 2), which can be used as a source of energy.
