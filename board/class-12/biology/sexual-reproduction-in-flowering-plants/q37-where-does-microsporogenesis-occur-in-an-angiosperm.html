Where does microsporogenesis occur in an angiosperm?

CBSE 2020Outside Delhi Set 2Q275 marks
Question 37 · Class 12 Biology · Sexual Reproduction in Flowering Plants
Question as text
(a) Where does microsporogenesis occur in an angiosperm? Describe the process of microsporogenesis. (b) Draw a labelled diagram of the two-celled male gametophyte of an angiosperm. How is the three-celled male gametophyte different from it? [5]
Answer
Answer as text
(a) In the microsporangia (pollen sacs) of the anther. Cells of the sporogenous tissue act as pollen/microspore mother cells; each undergoes meiosis to form a microspore tetrad; as the anther matures and dehydrates the microspores separate and develop into pollen grains. (b) Diagram: 2-celled pollen grain with labels exine, intine, germ pore, vegetative cell (nucleus), generative cell. Difference: in the 3-celled stage the generative cell has already divided mitotically into two male gametes, so the pollen has a vegetative cell and two male gametes (2-celled: vegetative cell + generative cell; shed in over 60% of angiosperms).
