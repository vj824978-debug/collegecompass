Question 24 · Class 12 Biology · Sexual Reproduction in Flowering Plants
Question as text
(b) Observe the picture of Commelina plant bearing two types of flowers given above. (i) Identify the two types of flowers labelled ‘A’ and ‘B’ in the picture. [1] (ii) Compare the two types of flowers with reference to: (1) characteristic feature (2) modes of pollination [2] (iii) List any two ‘out breeding devices’ in flowering plants. Explain why do plants develop such devices. [2]
Answer
Answer as text
(i) A – Chasmogamous flower; B – Cleistogamous flower. (ii) (1) Chasmogamous flowers are open with exposed anthers and stigma; cleistogamous flowers never open and the anthers and stigma lie close together. (2) Chasmogamous flowers can be selfor cross-pollinated (by agents); cleistogamous flowers are invariably autogamous (self-pollinated), giving assured seed set. (iii) Any two: pollen release and stigma receptivity not synchronised; anther and stigma placed at different positions; self-incompatibility; production of unisexual flowers (monoecy/dioecy). Plants develop them to discourage self-pollination, because continued self-pollination causes inbreeding depression; cross-pollination maintains variation.
