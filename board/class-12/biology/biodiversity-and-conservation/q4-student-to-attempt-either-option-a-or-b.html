CBSE 2025Outside Delhi Set 1Q335 marks
Question 4 · Class 12 Biology · Biodiversity and Conservation
Question as text
Student to attempt either option – (A) or (B). (A) (i) Explain giving three reasons why tropics show greatest levels of species diversity. (ii) Draw a graph showing species-area relationship. Name the naturalist who studied such relationship. Write the observation made by him. OR (B) (i) The world is facing the accelerated rate of species extinctions due to human activities. Explain any three major causes of biodiversity losses. (ii) Describe ‘Ex situ’ approach for conserving biodiversity. Give any two examples.
Answer
Answer as text
(A) (i) Tropics have greatest species diversity because: (1) they remained relatively undisturbed for millions of years (no glaciations), giving long evolutionary time for diversification; (2) environments are less seasonal, relatively constant and predictable, promoting niche specialisation; (3) more solar energy is available, giving higher productivity. (ii) Diagram: species–area curve – species richness (S) on y-axis vs area (A) on x-axis, a curve rising and levelling off (labelled S = CAZ), and a straight line on log–log scale (labelled log S = log C + Z log A). Naturalist: Alexander von Humboldt. Observation: within a region, species richness increases with increasing explored area, but only up to a limit; the relationship is a rectangular hyperbola (straight line on log scale), with Z usually 0.1–0.2. OR (B) (i) Any three of the “Evil Quartet”: habitat loss and fragmentation (e.g. tropical rain forest cover reduced from 14% to 6%; fragmentation harms animals needing large territories and migratory animals); over-exploitation (e.g. Steller’s sea cow, passenger pigeon became extinct through overharvesting); alien species invasions (e.g. Nile perch in Lake Victoria wiped out over 200 cichlid species; Parthenium, Lantana, water hyacinth); co-extinctions (e.g. extinction of a host fish also eliminates its unique parasites). (ii) Ex situ conservation: threatened animals and plants are taken out of their natural habitat and protected in special settings where they are cared for and bred. Examples: zoological parks, botanical gardens, wildlife safari parks, seed banks, cryopreservation of gametes (any two).
