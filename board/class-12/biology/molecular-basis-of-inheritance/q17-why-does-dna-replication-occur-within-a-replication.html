Why does DNA replication occur within a replication fork and not in its entire length simultaneously?

CBSE 2024Delhi Set 1Q263 marks
Question 17 · Class 12 Biology · Molecular Basis of Inheritance
Question as text
(a) Why does DNA replication occur within a replication fork and not in its entire length simultaneously? (b) “DNA replication is continuous and discontinuous on the two strands within the replication fork.” Explain with the help of a schematic representation. [3]
Answer
Answer as text
(a) DNA is very long and the two strands cannot be separated along the whole length at once because of the very high energy needed; so replication occurs in a small opening, the replication fork. (b) DNA polymerase works only in the 5 ′→3′ direction. On the template with 3 ′→5′ polarity the new (leading) strand is made continuously; on the other template (5 ′→3′) the lagging strand is made discontinuously as Okazaki fragments, later joined by DNA ligase.
