What are ‘SNPs’? Where are they located in a human cell? State any two ways the discovery of SNPs can be of importance to humans. [3]
Answer
Answer as text
SNPs: single nucleotide polymorphisms – single-base DNA differences among individuals. Location: in the DNA of the chromosomes (nucleus); about 1.4 million locations of single-base differences were identified in humans. Importance (any two): help find chromosomal locations of disease-associated sequences; help trace human history/evolution; can be used in personalised diagnosis and treatment, and in forensics/identification.
