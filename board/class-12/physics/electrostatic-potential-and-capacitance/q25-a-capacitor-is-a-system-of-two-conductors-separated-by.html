Question as text
A capacitor is a system of two conductors separated by an insulator. In practice, the two conductors have charges Q and−Q with potential difference V = V1−V2 between them. The ratio Q V is a constant, denoted by C and is called the capacitance of the capacitor. It is independent of Q orV . It depends only on the geometrical configuration (shape, size, separation) of the two conductors and the medium separating the conductors. When a parallel plate capacitor is charged, the electric field E0 is localised between the plates and is uniform throughout. When a slab of a dielectric is inserted between the charged plates (charge density σ), the dielectric is polarised by the field. Consequently opposite charges appear on the faces of the slab, near the plates, with surface charge density of magnitude σp. For a linear dielectric σp is proportional to E0. Introduction of a dielectric changes the electric field, and hence, the capacitance of a capacitor, and hence, the energy stored in the capacitor. Like resistors, capacitors can also be arranged in series or in parallel or in a combination of series and parallel.
