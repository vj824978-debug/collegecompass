Write two important characteristics of equipotential surfaces.

CBSE 202055/5/15 marks
Question 34 · Class 12 Physics · Electrostatic Potential and Capacitance
Question as text
(a) Write two important characteristics of equipotential surfaces. (b) A thin circular ring of radius r is charged uniformly so its linear charge density becomes λ. Derive an expression for the electric field at a point P at a distance x from it along the axis of the ring. Hence, prove that for large distance (x≫r), the ring behaves as a point charge. OR (a) State Gauss’s law on electrostatics and derive an expression for the electric field due to a long, straight and thin uniformly charged wire (linear charge densityλ) at a point lying at a distance r from the wire. (b) The magnitude of electric field (in NC −1) in a region varies with the distance r (in m) as E = 10r + 5 By how much, does the electric potential increase in moving from point at r = 1 m to a point at r = 10 m.
Answer
Answer as text
(a) No work is done in moving a charge on it; ⃗E is normal to it at every point (also: two equipotentials never intersect; closer where field is strong). (b) Derivation: axial components of fields of elements add, perpendicular cancel: E = Z dq 4πε0(r2 +x2)· x√ r2 +x2⇒E = 1 4πε0 (2πrλ)x (r2 +x2)3/2 along the axis; for x≫r: E = Q 4πε0x2 , Q = 2πrλ (point charge). OR: (a) Gauss’s law: H ⃗E·d⃗S =qenc/ε0. Derivation: cylindrical Gaussian surface of radius r, length l; flux only through curved surface: E(2πrl) =λl/ε0⇒E = λ 2πε0r, radially outward. (b)|∆V| = Z 10 (10r + 5)dr = [5r2 + 5r]10 1 = 550− 10 = 540 V. With ⃗E radially outward, V actually decreases by 540 V (increase =−540 V).
