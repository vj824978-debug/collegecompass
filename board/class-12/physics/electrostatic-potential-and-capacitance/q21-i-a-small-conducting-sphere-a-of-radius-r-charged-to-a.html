Question as text
(a) (i) A small conducting sphere A of radius r charged to a potential V, is enclosed by a spherical conducting shell B of radius R. If A and B are connected by a thin wire, calculate the final potential on sphere A and shell B. (ii) Write two characteristics of equipotential surfaces. A uniform electric field of 50 N C −1 is set up in a region along + x axis. If the potential at the origin (0 , 0) is 220 V, find the potential at a point (4 m, 3 m). OR (b) (i) What is difference between an open surface and a closed surface? Draw elementary surface vectord⃗S for a spherical surface S. (ii) Define electric flux through a surface. Give the significance of a Gaussian surface. A charge outside a Gaussian surface does not contribute to total electric flux through the surface. Why? (iii) A small spherical shell S 1 has point charges q1 =−3µC, q2 =−2µC and q3 = 9µC inside it. This shell is enclosed by another big spherical shell S 2. A point charge Q is placed in between the two surfaces S1 and S2. If the electric flux through the surface S 2 is four times the flux through surface S1, find charge Q.
Answer
Answer as text
(a)(i) q = 4πε0rV ; on connecting, all charge goes to outer shell: VA =VB = q 4πε0R = Vr R . (ii) Field is normal to an equipotential surface at every point; no work is done moving a charge on it (they never intersect; closer where field is strong). V = 220− 50× 4 = 20 V. (b)(i) Open surface has a boundary edge and does not enclose a volume; closed surface encloses a volume (no edge). d⃗S drawn normal to the sphere’s surface element, pointing outward. (ii) ϕ = H ⃗E·d⃗S (number of field lines crossing); Gaussian surface is a closed surface used to apply Gauss’s law (ϕ =qenc/ε0), chosen to exploit symmetry. Field lines of an outside charge enter and leave the surface equally, so net flux zero. (iii) ϕ1 = (−3− 2 + 9)µC/ε0 = 4µC/ε0; ϕ2 = (4 +Q)/ε0 = 4ϕ1⇒Q = 12µC.
