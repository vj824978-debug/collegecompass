Question 15 · Class 12 Physics · Electrostatic Potential and Capacitance
Question as text
(a) (i) Explain the following statements giving reason: (I) An equipotential surface through a point is normal to the electric field at that point. (II) When a dielectric is placed in an external electric field, the electric field inside the dielectric is less than that outside it. (III) The potential difference between the plates of a charged parallel plate capacitor decreases when its plates are brought closer. (ii) Obtain an expression for the work done to dissociate the system of three charges q,−4q and 2q placed at the vertices A, B and C respectively of an equilateral triangle of side ‘ a’. OR (b) (i) Answer the following giving reason: (I) The electron drift speed is estimated to be only a few mm/s for currents in the range of a few amperes. How, then, is the current established almost the instant a circuit is closed? (II) A low voltage supply from which one needs high currents must have very low internal resistance. Why? (III) The assertion that V =IR is a statement of Ohm’s law is not true. Why? (ii) Two cells of emfs 12 V and 6 V are connected in parallel as shown in the figure. Their internal resistances are 1 Ω and 0.5 Ω respectively. Calculate the emf and internal resistance of the equivalent cell between points A and B.
Answer
Answer as text
(a)(i)(I) Work along an equipotential surface is zero, dW =q⃗E·d⃗l = 0 for every displacement on it, so ⃗E⊥ surface (if ⃗E had a tangential component, potential would vary along the surface). (II) The external field polarises the dielectric; induced surface charges set up an opposing field ⃗Ep, so Enet =E0−Ep =E0/K <E0. (III) Q stays constant; C =ε0A/d increases as d decreases, so V =Q/C =Qd/(ε0A) decreases (field E =σ/ε0 is unchanged, V =Ed). (ii) U = 1 4πε0a[q(−4q) +q(2q) + (−4q)(2q)] =− 10q2 4πε0a; work to dissociate W =−U = 10q2 4πε0a. (b)(i)(I) On closing the circuit the electric field is set up throughout the conductor almost instantly (at nearly the speed of light), so free electrons everywhere start drifting at once; the current does not wait for electrons to travel from the cell. (II) Maximum current I =ε/(R +r); with low ε, a large I is possible only if r is very small (also less I2r loss/heating). (III) V =IR defines resistance and holds for any conductor; Ohm’s law is the statement that R is constant (independent of V , I) at fixed physical conditions, i.e. V ∝I; many devices do not obey it. (ii) Both cells have positive terminal towards A: εeq = ε1/r1 +ε2/r2 1/r1 + 1/r2 = 12 + 12 1 + 2 = 8 V; req = r1r2 r1 +r2 = 0.5 1.5 = 1 3 Ω≈ 0.33 Ω (A at higher potential).
