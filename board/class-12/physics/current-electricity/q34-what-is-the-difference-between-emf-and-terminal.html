What is the difference between ‘emf’ and ‘terminal voltage’ of a cell?

CBSE 202555/6/13 marks
Question 34 · Class 12 Physics · Current Electricity
Question as text
What is the difference between ‘emf’ and ‘terminal voltage’ of a cell? Two cells of emfs E1 and E2 and internal resistances r1 and r2 are connected in parallel. Derive an expression for the emf and internal resistance of the equivalent cell.
Answer
Answer as text
emf is the potential difference across the cell terminals in open circuit (no current; work done per unit charge by the cell); terminal voltage V =E−Ir is the p.d. when current flows (less than emf on discharge). Derivation: I =I1 +I2 = E1−V r1 + E2−V r2 givesV =Eeq−Ireq with Eeq = E1r2 +E2r1 r1 +r2 , req = r1r2 r1 +r2 .
