Two batteries of emf’s 3 V & 6 V and internal resistances 0.2 Ω & 0.4 Ω are connected in parallel.

CBSE 202555/1/13 marks
Question 26 · Class 12 Physics · Current Electricity
Question as text
(a) Two batteries of emf’s 3 V & 6 V and internal resistances 0.2 Ω & 0.4 Ω are connected in parallel. This combination is connected to a 4 Ω resistor. Find: (i) the equivalent emf of the combination (ii) the equivalent internal resistance of the combination (iii) the current drawn from the combination OR (b) (i) A conductor of length l is connected across an ideal cell of emf E. Keeping the cell connected, the length of the conductor is increased to 2 l by gradually stretching it. If R and R′ are initial and final values of resistance and vd and v′ d are initial and final values of drift velocity, find the relation between (i) R′ and R and (ii) v′ d and vd. (ii) When electrons drift in a conductor from lower to higher potential, does it mean that all the ‘free electrons’ of the conductor are moving in the same direction?
Answer
Answer as text
(a)(i) Eeq = E1r2 +E2r1 r1 +r2 = 3× 0.4 + 6× 0.2 0.6 = 4 V. (ii) req = r1r2 r1 +r2 = 0.08 0.6 = 2 15≈ 0.13 Ω. (iii) I = 4 4 + 2/15 = 30 31≈ 0.97 A. (b)(i) Volume constant: l→ 2l, A→A/2, so R′ = 4R. vd = eEτ m = eVτ ml with V fixed, so v′ d =vd/2. (ii) No. Free electrons still move randomly in all directions (thermal motion); only a small net drift is superposed, opposite to the field (towards higher potential).
