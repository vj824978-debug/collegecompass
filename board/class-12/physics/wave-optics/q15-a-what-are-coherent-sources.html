(a) What are coherent sources?

CBSE 202655/2/15 marks
Question 15 · Class 12 Physics · Wave Optics
Question as text
(A) (a) What are coherent sources? Why they are necessary for observing stable interference pattern? Draw a graph showing the variation of intensity of light with the position on the screen in Young’s double-slit experiment. (b) Find the intensity of light at a point on the screen when two interfering waves of the same intensity (I0) have a path difference of (i) λ 4 and (ii) λ 3 . OR (B) (a) Draw a labelled ray diagram of a refracting telescope when it forms image of a distant object at infinity. Derive expression for its magnifying power. (b) (i) In a telescope the objective has much larger aperture than the eye piece. Why? (ii) Write two advantages of reflecting telescope over refracting telescope.
Answer
Answer as text
(A)(a) Coherent sources: constant (time-independent) phase difference, same frequency. Needed so that positions of maxima/minima stay fixed (otherwise intensity averages out, no stable pattern). Graph: cos 2-type fringes, equally spaced maxima 4I0 and minima zero, equal widths. (b) I = 4I0 cos2(ϕ/2): (i) ϕ =π/2, I = 2I0; (ii) ϕ = 2π/3, I =I0. (B)(a) Diagram: parallel rays from distant object through objective (focal length fo) forming image at its focus, which coincides with the focus of eyepiece (fe); emergent rays parallel. Derivation: m =β/α =fo/fe (tube length fo +fe). (b)(i) Larger aperture collects more light (brighter image) and gives higher resolving power. (ii) No chromatic aberration; parabolic mirror free of spherical aberration; mirror lighter/easier to support and make (any two).
