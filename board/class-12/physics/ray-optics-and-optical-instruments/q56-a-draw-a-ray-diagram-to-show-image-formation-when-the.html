Question as text
(a) (a) Draw a ray diagram to show image formation when the concave mirror produces a real, inverted and magnified image of the object. (b) Obtain the mirror formula and write the expression for the linear magnification. (c) Explain two advantages of a reflecting telescope over a refracting telescope. OR (b) (a) Define a wavefront. Using Huygens’ principle, verify the laws of reflection at a plane surface. (b) In a single slit diffraction experiment, the width of the slit is made double the original width. How does this affect the size and intensity of the central diffraction band? Explain. (c) When a tiny circular obstacle is placed in the path of light from a distant source, a bright spot is seen at the centre of the obstacle. Explain why.
Answer
Answer as text
(a) Diagram: object between C and F; ray parallel to axis reflects through F, ray through C (or F) returns; real, inverted, magnified image beyond C. (b) Derivation: similar triangles BA B′A′ = PA PA′ = PF PA′−PF with sign convention⇒ 1 v + 1 u = 1 f ; m = h′ h =−v u. (c) Any two: no chromatic aberration; paraboloidal mirror removes spherical aberration; large mirror is lighter and easier to support; large aperture gives high light-gathering and resolving power. OR: (a) Wavefront: locus of points oscillating in the same phase. Huygens: plane wavefront AB meets mirror at A; while the disturbance from B travels BC =vτ , the wavelet from A has radius AE =vτ ; tangent CE is the reflected wavefront;△ABC∼=△CEA⇒i =r, rays and normal coplanar. (b) Width = 2λD a is halved; twice the light falls into half the area, so intensity of the central band increases (about four times). (c) Diffraction: light bends round the obstacle’s edge; all points on the circular rim are equidistant from the centre of the shadow, so the diffracted waves arrive in phase and interfere constructively there.
