CBSE 202555/2/15 marks
Question 31 · Class 12 Physics · Ray Optics and Optical Instruments
Question as text
(a) (i) A thin pencil of length ( f/4) is placed coinciding with the principal axis of a mirror of focal length f. The image of the pencil is real and enlarged, just touches the pencil. Calculate the magnification produced by the mirror. (ii) A ray of light is incident on a refracting face AB of a prism ABC at an angle of 45 ◦. The ray emerges from face AC and the angle of deviation is 15 ◦. The angle of prism is 30 ◦. Show that the emergent ray is normal to the face AC from which it emerges out. Find the refraction index of the material of the prism. OR (b) (i) Light consisting of two wavelengths 600 nm and 480 nm is used to obtain interference fringes in a double slit experiment. The screen is placed 1.0 m away from slits which are 1.0 nm apart. (1) Calculate the distance of the third bright fringe on the screen from the central maximum for wavelength 600 nm. (2) Find the least distance from the central maximum where the bright fringes due to both the wavelengths coincide. (ii) (1) Draw the variation of intensity with angle of diffraction in single slit diffraction pattern. Write the expression for value of angle corresponding to zero intensity locations. (2) In what way diffraction of light waves differs from diffraction of sound waves?
Answer
Answer as text
(a)(i) Concave mirror, pencil between f and 2f with one end at C (2f), whose image is also at C. Other end at u =− 7f 4 : v =− 1 f + 4 7f⇒v =−7f 3 . Image length 7f 3 − 2f = f 3 ; magnification = f/3 f/4 = 4 3 (image inverted). (ii) δ =i +e−A⇒ 15 = 45 +e− 30⇒e = 0, so emergent ray is normal to AC;r2 = 0⇒r1 =A = 30◦; n = sin 45◦ sin 30◦ = √ 2≈ 1.41. (b)(i) (taking d = 1.0 mm; see note) (1) y3 = 3λD d = 3× 600× 10−9× 1 10−3 = 1.8 mm. (2) n1(600) =n2(480)⇒n1 = 4, n2 = 5; y = 4× 600× 10−9 10−3 = 2.4 mm. (ii)(1) Graph: broad central maximum at θ = 0, secondary maxima of rapidly decreasing intensity on both sides, zeros at θ =±λ/a,±2λ/a,... ; minima at sinθ≈θ = nλ a , n =±1,±2,... (2) Light has very small wavelength, so it diffracts noticeably only at very small apertures/obstacles; sound has large wavelength (comparable to everyday objects) so it diffracts readily around them.
