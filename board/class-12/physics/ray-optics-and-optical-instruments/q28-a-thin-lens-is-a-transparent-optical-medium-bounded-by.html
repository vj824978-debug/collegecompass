A thin lens is a transparent optical medium bounded by two surfaces, at least one of which should be spherical. Applying the formula for image formation by a single spherical surface successively at the two surfaces of a lens, one can obtain the ‘lens maker formula’ and then the ‘lens formula’. A lens has two foci – called ‘first focal point’ and ‘second focal point’ of the lens, one on each side. (i) Consider the arrangement shown in figure. A black vertical arrow and a horizontal thick line with a ball are painted on a glass plate. It serves as the object. When the plate is illuminated, its real image is formed on the screen. Which of the following correctly represents the image formed on the screen? (ii) Which of the following statements is incorrect? (A) For a convex mirror magnification is always negative. (B) For all virtual images formed by a mirror magnification is positive. (C) For a concave lens magnification is always positive. (D) For real and inverted images, magnification is always negative. (iii) A convex lens of focal length ‘f’ is cut into two equal parts perpendicular to the principal axis. The focal length of each part will be: (A) f (B) 2 f (C) f 2 (D) f OR (iii) If an object in case (i) above is 20 cm from the lens and the screen is 50 cm away from the object, the focal length of the lens used is (A) 10 cm (B) 12 cm (C) 16 cm (D) 20 cm (iv) The distance of an object from first focal point of a biconvex lens is X1 and distance of the image from second focal point is X2. The focal length of the lens is (A) X1X2 (B) √X1 +X2 (C) √X1X2 (D) rX2 X1
Answer
Answer as text
(i) (C) real image is inverted both vertically and laterally (rotated by 180◦): arrow points down, ball on the opposite side. (ii) (A) is incorrect: a convex mirror always forms a virtual erect image, so m is always positive. (iii) (B) each half is plano-convex: 1 f = (µ− 1) 2 R becomes (µ− 1) 1 R, so focal length 2f. OR (iii) (B) u =−20 cm,v = +30 cm: 1 f = 1 30 + 1 20 = 1 12, f = 12 cm. (iv) (C) Newton’s formula X1X2 =f 2, f =√X1X2.
