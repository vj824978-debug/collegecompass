Question as text
Draw a graph showing the variation of number of particles scattered (N) with the scattering angle θ in Geiger-Marsden experiment Why only a small fraction of the particles are scattered at θ >90◦? Mention two limitations of Rutherford nuclear model of an atom.
Answer
Answer as text
Diagram: N (log scale) vs θ (0◦–180◦), falling steeply (N∝ 1/ sin4(θ/2)). Large-angle scattering needs a near head-on approach to the tiny, massive, positively charged nucleus, which very few α-particles make (most of the atom is empty). Limitations: cannot explain stability of the atom (an accelerated orbiting electron should radiate and spiral into the nucleus); cannot explain the discrete line spectra of atoms.
