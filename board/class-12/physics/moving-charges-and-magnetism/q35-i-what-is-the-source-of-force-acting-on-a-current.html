(i) What is the source of force acting on a current-carrying conductor placed in a magnetic field?

CBSE 202555/2/15 marks
Question 35 · Class 12 Physics · Moving Charges and Magnetism
Question as text
(a) (i) What is the source of force acting on a current-carrying conductor placed in a magnetic field? Obtain the expression for force acting between two long straight parallel conductors carrying steady currents and hence define ‘ampere’. (ii) A point charge q is moving with velocity ⃗ vin a uniform magnetic field ⃗B. Find the work done by the magnetic force on the charge. (iii) Explain the necessary conditions in which the trajectory of a charged particle is helical in a uniform magnetic field. OR (b) (i) A current carrying loop can be considered as a magnetic dipole placed along its axis. Explain. (ii) Obtain the relation for magnetic dipole moment ⃗M of current carrying coil. Give the direction of ⃗M. (iii) A current carrying coil is placed in an external uniform magnetic field. The coil is free to turn in the magnetic field. What is the net force acting on the coil? Obtain the orientation of the coil in stable equilibrium. Show that in this orientation the flux of the total field (field produced by the loop + external field) through the coil is maximum.
Answer
Answer as text
(a)(i) Source: Lorentz force q(⃗ vd× ⃗B) on the moving charge carriers. B1 = µ0I1 2πd , force per unit length f = µ0I1I2 2πd (attractive for parallel, repulsive for antiparallel currents). 1 A: current which in each of two infinitely long parallel conductors 1 m apart in vacuum produces a force of 2× 10−7 N m−1. (ii) Zero: ⃗F =q⃗ v× ⃗B⊥⃗ v, so W = 0. (iii) ⃗ vmust have components both parallel (v cosθ, uniform motion along ⃗B) and perpendicular (v sinθ, circular motion) to ⃗B, i.e. 0◦<θ< 90◦; pitch = 2πmv cosθ/qB. (b)(i) Field of a loop on its axis far away, B = µ0 4π 2IA x3 , has the same form as a bar magnet’s axial field µ0 4π 2m x3 ; one face acts as N, other as S. (ii) ⃗M =NI ⃗A, along the normal to the plane of the coil given by right-hand thumb rule (curl fingers along current). (iii) Net force zero in uniform field; torque ⃗ τ= ⃗M× ⃗B, U =−MB cosθ; stable equilibrium when ⃗M∥ ⃗B (θ = 0, U minimum). Then the loop’s own field through it is along ⃗M, i.e. along ⃗B, so the two fluxes add: total flux is maximum.
