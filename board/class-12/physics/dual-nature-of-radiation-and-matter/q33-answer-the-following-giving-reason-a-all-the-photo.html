Question as text
Answer the following giving reason: (a) All the photo electrons do not eject with the same kinetic energy when monochromatic light is incident on a metal surface. (b) The saturation current in case (a) is different for different intensity. (c) If one goes on increasing the wavelength of light incident on a metal surface, keeping its intensity constant, emission of photo electrons stop at a certain wavelength for this metal.
Answer
Answer as text
(a) Electrons come from different levels/depths; those below the surface lose energy in collisions before emerging, so only surface electrons get Kmax =hν−ϕ0; KE ranges from 0 to Kmax. (b) Intensity ∝ number of photons per second; each photon ejects at most one electron, so the number of photoelectrons (saturation current) ∝ intensity. (c) Photon energy hc/λ decreases with λ; beyond the threshold wavelength λ0 =hc/ϕ0 it is less than the work function, so no emission whatever the intensity.
