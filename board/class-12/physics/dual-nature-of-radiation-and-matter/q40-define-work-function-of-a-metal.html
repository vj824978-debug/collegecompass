Define ‘work function’ of a metal.

CBSE 202555/7/13 marks
Question 40 · Class 12 Physics · Dual Nature of Radiation and Matter
Question as text
(a) Define ‘work function’ of a metal. How can its value be determined from a graph between stopping potential and frequency of the incident radiation? (b) The work function of a metal is 2.4 eV. A stopping potential of 0.6 V is required to reduce the photocurrent to zero, in a photoelectric experiment. Calculate the wavelength of light used. [h = 6.63× 10−34 J s,c = 3× 108 m/s, e = 1.6× 10−19 C]
Answer
Answer as text
(a) Work function: minimum energy needed by an electron to escape from the metal surface. eV0 =hν−ϕ0⇒V0 = h eν−ϕ0 e : from the V0–ν straight line, ϕ0 =hν0 (ν0 = intercept on ν-axis), or ϕ0 =e×| intercept on V0-axis|. (b) E =ϕ0 +eV0 = 2.4 + 0.6 = 3.0 eV; λ = hc E = 6.63× 10−34× 3× 108 3.0× 1.6× 10−19 ≈ 4.14× 10−7 m = 414 nm.
