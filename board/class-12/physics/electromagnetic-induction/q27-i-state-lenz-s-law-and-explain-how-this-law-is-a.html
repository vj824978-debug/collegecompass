(i) State Lenz’s law and explain how this law is a consequence of conservation of energy principle.

CBSE 202555/4/15 marks
Question 27 · Class 12 Physics · Electromagnetic Induction
Question as text
(a) (i) State Lenz’s law and explain how this law is a consequence of conservation of energy principle. (ii) A square shaped loop of side l 2 is initially lying outside a region of uniform magnetic field ⃗B as shown in the figure. The loop is moved towards right with a constant velocity ⃗ vtill it goes out of the region of magnetic field. (I) What will be the directions of induced current when the loop enters the field and when it leaves the field? (II) Draw the plots showing the variation of magnetic flux ϕ linked with the loop with time t and variation of induced emf E with time t. Mark the relevant values of E, ϕ and t on the graphs. OR (b) (i) Differentiate between peak and rms values of alternating current. How are they related? (ii) A current element X is connected across an ac source of emf V =V0 sin 2πνt. It is found that the voltage leads the current in phase by π 2 radian. If element X was replaced by element Y, the voltage lags behind the current in phase by π 2 radian. (I) Identify elements X and Y by drawing phasor diagrams. (II) Obtain the condition of resonance when both elements X and Y are connected in series to the source and obtain expression for resonant frequency. What is the impedance value in this case?
Answer
Answer as text
(a)(i) Lenz: induced emf/current opposes the change in magnetic flux that produces it. If it aided the change, flux would grow on its own and electrical energy would be created without work; actually work must be done against the opposing force, and this work appears as electrical energy (conservation of energy). (ii)(I) ⃗B into the page: entering, inward flux increases, induced current anticlockwise; leaving, flux decreases, induced current clockwise. (II) Plots: ϕ = 0 for 0 <t<l/v ; rises linearly to ϕmax =Bl2/4 at t = 3l/2v; constant till t = 2l/v; falls linearly to 0 at t = 5l/2v. EMF: 0, then constant magnitude Blv/2 (E =−Blv/2) for l/v <t< 3l/2v, 0 for 3l/2v <t< 2l/v, +Blv/2 for 2l/v <t< 5l/2v, 0 after. (b)(i) Peak value I0: maximum instantaneous current; rms value: the steady current producing the same heat in a resistor in the same time; Irms =I0/ √ 2≈ 0.707I0. (ii)(I) Phasor diagrams: X: V leads I byπ/2, so X is a pure inductor; Y: V lags I byπ/2, so Y is a pure capacitor. (II) In series, Z =|XL−XC|; resonance when XL =XC, i.e. ωL = 1/ωC: ν0 = 1 2π √ LC . Impedance at resonance = 0 (ideal L and C; current becomes very large).
