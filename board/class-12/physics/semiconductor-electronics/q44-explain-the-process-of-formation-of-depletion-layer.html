Question as text
Explain the process of formation of ‘depletion layer’ and ‘potential barrier’ in a p-n junction region of a diode, with the help of a suitable diagram. Which feature of junction diode makes it suitable for its use as a rectifier?
Answer
Answer as text
At the junction, holes diffuse from p to n and electrons from n to p (concentration gradient), leaving uncovered immobile ions: negative acceptor ions on p-side, positive donor ions on n-side; this region devoid of mobile carriers is the depletion layer. The space charge sets up an electric field from n to p, causing drift opposite to diffusion; equilibrium when diffusion current = drift current; the resulting potential difference across the junction is the potential barrier (∼ 0.7 V Si, 0.3 V Ge). Diagram: p and n regions, depletion region with − and + ions, field direction and barrier potential. Rectifier: diode conducts (low resistance) only in forward bias and offers very high resistance in reverse bias (unidirectional conduction).
