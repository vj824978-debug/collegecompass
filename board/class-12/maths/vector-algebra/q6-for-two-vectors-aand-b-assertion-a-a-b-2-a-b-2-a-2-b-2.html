For two vectors⃗ aand⃗b Assertion (A):|⃗ a×⃗b|2 + (⃗ a·⃗b)2 =|⃗ a|2|⃗b|2 Reason (R):|⃗ a×⃗b| = (⃗ a·⃗b) tanθ, θ̸= π (A) Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true and the Reason (R) is the correct explanation of the Assertion (A). (B) Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true, but Reason (R) is not the correct explanation of the Assertion (A). (C) Assertion (A) is true, but Reason (R) is false. (D) Assertion (A) is false, but Reason (R) is true.
Answer
Answer as text
(A) or (B) A is Lagrange’s identity (sin 2θ + cos2θ = 1), true for all θ; R true since|⃗ a||⃗b| cosθ tanθ =|⃗ a||⃗b| sinθ, squaring R and adding (⃗ a·⃗b)2 gives A for θ̸= π 2 , so CBSE-style marking usually gives (A); strictly, R cannot explain the θ = π 2 case, hence (B) is also defensible.
