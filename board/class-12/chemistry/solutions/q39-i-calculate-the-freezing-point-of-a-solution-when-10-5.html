Solutions — Question 39 (CBSE 2026, 5 marks)

CBSE 202656/5/15 marks
Question 39 · Class 12 Chemistry · Solutions
Question as text
(a) (i) Calculate the freezing point of a solution when 10.5 g of MgBr2 was dissolved in 250 g of water, assuming MgBr2 undergoes complete dissociation. (Given : Molar mass of MgBr 2 = 184 g mol−1, Kf for water = 1.86 K kg mol−1) (ii) Write two differences between ideal and non-ideal solutions. OR (b) (i) A solution is prepared by dissolving 0.025 g of potassium sulphate in 2 L of water at 27 ◦C. Assuming potassium sulphate is completely dissociated, determine its osmotic pressure. (Given : R = 0.082 L atm K−1 mol−1, Molar mass of K 2SO4 = 174 g mol−1) (ii) What type of azeotrope will be formed by a solution of acetone and chloroform? Give reason.
Answer
Answer as text
(a)(i) n = 10.5/184 = 0.0571 mol;m = 0.0571/0.250 = 0.228 mol kg−1; i = 3; ∆Tf = 3× 1.86× 0.228≈ 1.27 K; freezing point≈− 1.27◦C (271.88 K). (ii) Ideal: obeys Raoult’s law at all concentrations, ∆mixH = 0, ∆mixV = 0; non-ideal: deviates from Raoult’s law, ∆mixH̸= 0, ∆mixV ̸= 0 (A–B interactions differ from A–A, B–B). (b)(i) n = 0.025/174 = 1.44× 10−4 mol; C = 7.18× 10−5 mol L−1; i = 3; π =iCRT = 3× 7.18× 10−5× 0.082× 300≈ 5.3× 10−3 atm. (ii) Maximum boiling azeotrope (negative deviation): H-bond between H of CHCl3 and O of acetone makes A–B attractions stronger, lowering the vapour pressure.
