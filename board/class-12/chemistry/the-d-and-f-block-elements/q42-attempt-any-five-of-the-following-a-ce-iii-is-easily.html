CBSE 20245 marks
Question 42 · Class 12 Chemistry · The d- and f-Block Elements
Question as text
Attempt any five of the following: (a) Ce(III) is easily oxidised to Ce(IV). Comment. (b) E◦(Mn2+/Mn) is −1.18 V. Why is this value highly negative in comparison to neighbouring d block elements? (c) Which element of 3d series has lowest enthalpy of atomisation and why? (d) What happens when sodium chromate is acidified? (e) Zn, Cd and Hg are soft metals. Why? (f) Why is permanganate titration not carried out in the presence of HCl? (g) The lower oxides of transition metals are basic whereas the highest are amphoteric/acidic. Give reason.
Answer
Answer as text
Any five: (a) Ce(IV) is 4 f 0 (noble-gas, Xe core), so Ce(III) (4 f 1) loses one electron easily. (b) Mn 2+ is 3d5 (half-filled, extra stable), so it is hard to reduce to Mn; also low enthalpy of atomisation of Mn. (c) Zn: 3d104s2, no unpaired electrons, weakest metallic bonding. (d) Yellow chromate becomes orange dichromate: 2 Na 2CrO4 + 2 H+−−→Na2Cr2O7 + 2 Na+ + H2O. (e) Completely filled ( n− 1)d10, no unpaired d electrons for metallic bonding, so metallic bonds are weak. (f) KMnO 4 oxidises HCl to Cl 2, so part of the oxidant is consumed and results are wrong. (g) In low oxidation states the oxide is ionic and gives O 2– /OH– (basic, e.g. MnO); in high oxidation states the metal is highly polarising, oxide is covalent and acidic (e.g. Mn 2O7).
