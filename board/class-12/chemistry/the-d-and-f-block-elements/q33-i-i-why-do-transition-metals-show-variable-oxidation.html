(i) (I) Why do transition metals show variable oxidation states?

CBSE 202656/5/15 marks
Question 33 · Class 12 Chemistry · The d- and f-Block Elements
Question as text
(a) (i) (I) Why do transition metals show variable oxidation states? (II) Out of Mn 2+ and Ti2+ which will be more paramagnetic and why? [Atomic No. : Ti = 22, Mn = 25] (III) Which ion is the strongest oxidising agent in the options given below : Cr3+, V3+, Mn3+ Give reason. [Atomic No. : Cr = 24, V = 23, Mn = 25] (ii) Complete and balance the following equations : (I) 2 MnO2 + 4 KOH + O2−−→ (II) 5 C2O42– + 2 MnO4 – + 16 H+−−→ OR (b) (i) What is meant by lanthanoid contraction? (ii) Why do transition metals form coloured compounds? (iii) Why are E◦ M2+/M values for Mn and Zn more negative than expected? (iv) Which is the most stable oxidation state of Cu and why? (v) Why is Ce 4+ in aqueous solution a good oxidising agent?
Answer
Answer as text
(a)(i)(I) Incompletely filled (n− 1)d orbitals; (n− 1)d and ns electrons have similar energies, so different numbers can take part in bonding. (II) Mn2+ (3d5, 5 unpaired) is more paramagnetic than Ti 2+ (3d2, 2 unpaired). (III) Mn 3+: 3d4→ 3d5 (stable half-filled Mn 2+) on reduction; E◦ Mn3+/Mn2+ = +1.57 V. (ii)(I) 2 MnO2 + 4 KOH + O2−−→2 K2MnO4 + 2 H2O; (II) 5 C2O42– + 2 MnO4 – + 16 H+−−→2 Mn2+ + 10 CO2 + 8 H2O. (b)(i) Steady decrease in atomic and ionic (Ln 3+) radii from La to Lu due to imperfect shielding by 4f electrons. (ii) Partly filled d orbitals: d–d transitions absorb visible light; complementary colour seen. (iii) Mn2+ (3d5) half-filled and Zn 2+ (3d10) completely filled configurations are extra stable. (iv) +2: Cu 2+(aq) is more stable than Cu +(aq) because its much more negative hydration enthalpy outweighs the second ionisation enthalpy. (v) Ce 4+ tends to return to the stable +3 state (E◦ Ce4+/Ce3+ = +1.74 V).
