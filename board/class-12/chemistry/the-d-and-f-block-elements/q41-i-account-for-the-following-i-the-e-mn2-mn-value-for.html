Question 41 · Class 12 Chemistry · The d- and f-Block Elements
Question as text
(a) (i) Account for the following: (I) The E◦ Mn2+/Mn value for manganese is highly negative, whereas E◦ Mn3+/Mn2+ is highly positive. 1 (II) Actinoids show wide range of oxidation states. 1 (III) Transition metals have high melting points. 1 (ii) Complete the following ionic equations: (I) 5 SO32– + 2 MnO4 – + 6 H+−−→ 1 (II) 2 MnO4 – + H2O + I–−−→ 1 OR (b) Answer the following questions: 5 × 1 = 5 (i) Name two elements of 3d series for which the third ionisation enthalpies are quite high. (ii) Out of KMnO 4 and K2MnO4, which one is paramagnetic and why? (iii) Write any one consequence of lanthanoid contraction. (iv) How do you prepare potassium manganate from pyrolusite ore? (v) Why is the ability of oxygen more than fluorine to stabilise higher oxidation states of transition metals?
Answer
Answer as text
(a)(i)(I) Mn2+ is 3d5 (stable half-filled): it is very stable and hard to reduce to Mn (highly negative), while Mn 3+ (3d4) readily gains an electron to reach stable 3d5 (highly positive). (II) 5f, 6d and 7s levels are of comparable energy, so variable numbers of electrons can be lost. (III) Strong metallic bonding from many unpaired d electrons (covalent d–d interactions). (ii)(I) 5 SO 32– + 2 MnO4 – + 6 H+−−→2 Mn2+ + 5 SO42– + 3 H2O; (II) 2 MnO4 – + H2O + I–−−→2 MnO2 + 2 OH– + IO3 – . (b)(i) Any two of Cu, Ni, Zn (also Mn, Mn 2+ 3d5). (ii) K 2MnO4: Mn(VI) is 3d1, one unpaired electron; KMnO 4 has Mn(VII) 3d0, diamagnetic. (iii) Zr and Hf have almost identical radii and properties, hard to separate (or basicity of Ln(OH) 3 decreases from La to Lu). (iv) Fuse MnO2 with KOH in air/oxidant: 2 MnO 2 + 4 KOH + O2−−→2 K2MnO4 + 2 H2O. (v) Oxygen can form multiple bonds (pπ–dπ) with the metal, e.g. Mn 2O7 (Mn +7) vs highest fluoride MnF 4 (+4).
