(i) The initial concentration of N 2O5 in the first order reaction : N2O5(g)−−→2 NO2(g) + 1 2 O2(g) was…

Question 39 · Class 12 Chemistry · Chemical Kinetics
Question as text
(a) (i) The initial concentration of N 2O5 in the first order reaction : N2O5(g)−−→2 NO2(g) + 1 2 O2(g) was 1.2×10−2 mol L−1. The concentration of N2O5 after 60 minutes was 0.2×10−2 mol L−1. Calculate the rate constant of the reaction at 318 K. [log 6 = 0.778] (3) (ii) Account for the following : (I) We cannot determine the order of a reaction by taking into consideration the balanced chemical equation. (1) (II) A bimolecular reaction may become kinetically of first order under a specified condition. (1) OR (b) (i) The rate of the chemical reaction doubles for an increase of 10 K in absolute temperature from 298 K. Calculate activation energy (Ea). [2.303R = 19.15 J K−1 mol−1, log 2 = 0.3] (3) (ii) For a reaction : 2 H 2O2 I – −−→2 H2O + O2 the proposed mechanism is as given below : (I) H2O2 + I–−−→H2O + IO– (slow) (II) H2O2 + IO–−−→H2O + I– + O2 (fast) (1) Write rate law for the reaction. (2) Write the overall order and molecularity of the reaction. (2)
Answer
Answer as text
(a)(i) k = 2.303 60 log 1.2× 10−2 0.2× 10−2 = 2.303× 0.778 60 ≈ 2.99× 10−2 min−1 (≈ 4.98× 10−4 s−1). (ii)(I) Order is an experimental quantity governed by the mechanism (slowest step), not by stoichiometric coefficients. (II) If one reactant is in large excess (e.g. water in acid hydrolysis of ethyl acetate), its concentration stays constant: pseudo first order. (b)(i) log 2 = Ea 2.303R· 10 298× 308; Ea = 0.3× 19.15× 91784 10 ≈ 5.27× 104 J mol−1 (≈ 52.7 kJ mol−1). (ii)(1) Rate = k[H2O2][I– ] (from slow step). (2) Order = 2 (1 w.r.t. each); molecularity of the rate-determining step = 2 (bimolecular).
