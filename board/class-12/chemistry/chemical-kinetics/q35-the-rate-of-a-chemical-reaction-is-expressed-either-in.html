The rate of a chemical reaction is expressed either in terms of decrease in the concentration of reactants or…

CBSE 202556/1/14 marks
Question 35 · Class 12 Chemistry · Chemical Kinetics
Question as text
The rate of a chemical reaction is expressed either in terms of decrease in the concentration of reactants or increase in the concentration of a product per unit time. Rate of the reaction depends upon the nature of reactants, concentration of reactants, temperature, presence of catalyst, surface area of the reactants and presence of light. Rate of reactions is directly related to the concentration of reactant. Rate law states that the rate of reaction depends upon the concentration terms on which the rate of reaction actually depends, as observed experimentally. The sum of powers of the concentration of the reactants in the Rate law expression is called order of reaction while the number of reacting species taking part in an elementary reaction which must collide simultaneously in order to bring about a chemical reaction is called molecularity of the reaction. Answer the following questions: (a) (i) What is a rate determining step? (ii) Define complex reaction. [1+1=2] (b) What is the effect of temperature on the rate constant of a reaction? [1] OR (b) Why is molecularity applicable only for elementary reactions whereas order is applicable for elementary as well as complex reactions? [1] (c) The conversion of molecule X to Y follows second order kinetics. If concentration of X is increased 3 times, how will it affect the rate of formation of Y? [1]
Answer
Answer as text
(a)(i) The slowest elementary step of a mechanism, which controls the overall rate. (ii) A reaction that takes place in more than one elementary step (a sequence of steps). (b) Rate constant increases with temperature (roughly doubles for a 10 K rise), as given by k =Ae−Ea/RT . OR (b) Molecularity counts species colliding in a single step, so it has no meaning for an overall multistep reaction; order is found experimentally from the overall rate law, so it applies to both. (c) Rate = k[X]2; tripling [X] makes the rate of formation of Y 9 times.
