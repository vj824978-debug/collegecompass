(a) How do you convert the following?

CBSE 20195 marks
Question 50 · Class 12 Chemistry · Alcohols, Phenols and Ethers
Question as text
(a) (a) How do you convert the following? (i) Phenol to Anisole. (ii) Ethanol to Propan-2-ol (b) Write mechanism for the following reaction. C2H5OH H2SO4 −−−−→443 K CH2 ––CH2 + H2O (c) Why Phenol undergoes electrophilic substitution more easily than benzene? OR (b) (a) Account for the following: (i) o-nitrophenol is more steam volatile than p-nitrophenol. (ii) t-butyl chloride on heating with sodium methoxide gives 2-methylpropene instead of t-butylmethylether. (b) Write the reaction involved in the following: (i) Reimer-Tiemann reaction (ii) Friedel-Crafts alkylation of phenol (c) Give simple chemical test to distinguish between ethanol and phenol.
Answer
Answer as text
(a) (a)(i) C 6H5OH NaOH−−−−→C6H5O– Na+ CH3I−−−→C6H5OCH3 (Williamson). (ii) CH3CH2OH PCC−−−→CH3CHO (i) CH3MgBr −−−−−−−−→ (ii) H3O+ CH3CH(OH)CH3. (b) Step 1: C 2H5OH + H+−−⇀↽−−C2H5OH2+ (fast); step 2: CH3CH2OH2+−−→CH3CH2+ + H2O (slow); step 3: CH3CH2+−−→CH2 ––CH2 + H+. (c) The lone pair of O of −OH is delocalised into the ring (+R effect), increasing electron density at o-/p-positions; ring is activated. (b) (a)(i) o-Nitrophenol has intramolecular H-bonding (no association), so it is steam volatile; p-nitrophenol is associated by intermolecular H-bonds. (ii) CH 3O– is a strong base and the 3◦ halide is hindered, so E2 elimination gives (CH 3)2C––CH2 rather than S N2 substitution. (b)(i) C6H5OH CHCl3, aq· NaOH −−−−−−−−−−−→ H3O+ salicylaldehyde (2-hydroxybenzaldehyde) via the benzal chloride intermediate. (ii) Phenol + CH 3Cl (anhyd. AlCl3) → 2-methylphenol + 4-methylphenol + HCl. (c) Neutral FeCl 3: phenol gives violet colour, ethanol does not (or iodoform test: ethanol gives yellow CHI3).
