Question 48 · Class 12 Chemistry · Biomolecules
Question as text
Carbohydrates are polyhydroxy aldehydes or ketones that represent enormous structural diversity in terms of the arrangement of atoms in space, resulting in hundreds of stereoisomers. Although the chemical properties of most stereoisomers may not be very different, their metabolic rate and utilization in biological systems is significantly different and known to influence the overall carbohydrate metabolism. Structural variants, which arise due to a different arrangement of atoms in three-dimensional space are known as stereoisomers. The number of stereoisomers can be theoretically estimated by using the formula 2n, where ‘n’ is the number of stereocenters or asymmetric (chiral) carbon atoms in a molecule. Out of these stereoisomers, there are some structures, which are mirror images of each other, and they are referred to as enantiomers. Answer the following questions: (a) Give chemical reactions to show the presence of an aldehydic group and straight chain in glucose. (b) (i) Define anomers. 1 OR (b) (ii) Draw the structure of β-D-Glucopyranose. 1 (c) Sucrose is known as invert sugar. Explain. 1
Answer
Answer as text
(a) Aldehyde group: glucose + Br 2 water→ gluconic acid (CHO−−→COOH), or with NH 2OH forms an oxime. Straight chain: glucose + HI, heat → n-hexane CH3(CH2)4CH3. (b) (i) Anomers: the two cyclic forms ( α and β) of a sugar differing only in configuration of the OH at C-1 (anomeric carbon). (ii) Haworth structure: six-membered pyranose ring (five C + O); at C-1 the OH is on the same side (up) as the CH 2OH at C-5; OH at C-2 down, C-3 up, C-4 down. (c) Sucrose is dextrorotatory; on hydrolysis it gives an equimolar mixture of D-(+)-glucose (+52.5◦) and D-(−)-fructose (−92.4◦), which is laevorotatory. The sign of rotation is inverted, so sucrose (strictly its hydrolysis product) is called invert sugar.
