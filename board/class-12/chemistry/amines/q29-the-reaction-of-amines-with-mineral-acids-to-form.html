The reaction of amines with mineral acids to form ammonium salts shows that these are basic in nature. Aliphatic amines are stronger bases than ammonia whereas aromatic amines are weaker bases than ammonia. Aliphatic and aromatic primary and secondary amines react with acid chlorides, anhydrides and esters by nucleophilic substitution reaction. The main problem encountered during electrophilic substitution reactions of aromatic amines is that of their high reactivity. Substitution tends to occur at ortho-and para-positions. Hinsberg reagent is used for the identification and distinction between primary, secondary and tertiary amines. Aryldiazonium salts, usually obtained from arylamines, undergo replacement of the diazonium group with a variety of nucleophiles to provide advantageous methods for producing aryl halides, cyanides, phenols and arenes. Answer the following questions: (a) (i) Why CH 3 –NH 2 is a stronger base than (CH 3)3N in aqueous solution? 2 × 1 (ii) Write structural formulae of the compound A and B: CH3CONH2 NaOBr−−−−→A C6H5COCl−−−−−−−→Base B (b) A compound ‘X’ with molecular formula C 3H9N reacts with Hinsberg reagent to give a product insoluble in alkali. Identify ‘X’. 1 OR (b) How can you convert aniline to benzonitrile? 1 (c) Why is –NH 2 group of aniline acetylated before carrying out nitration? 1
Answer
Answer as text
(a)(i) In water the protonated amine is stabilised by H-bonding (solvation); CH3NH3+ has three N–H for H-bonding, (CH 3)3NH+ only one, and 3◦ amine also suffers steric hindrance; so CH 3NH2 is stronger. (ii) A = CH 3NH2 (Hoffmann bromamide degradation); B = C6H5CONHCH3 (N-methylbenzamide). (b) X = N-methylethanamine CH 3CH2NHCH3 (2◦ amine). OR (b) Aniline NaNO2 + HCl−−−−−−−−−→273-–278 K C6H5N2+Cl– CuCN/KCN −−−−−−−→C6H5CN (Sandmeyer). (c) To control the strong activating effect of –NH 2 (prevent oxidation by HNO3, polysubstitution and formation of meta-directing anilinium ion), so that p-nitroacetanilide (then p-nitroaniline) is the main product.
