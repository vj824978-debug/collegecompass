Amines are usually formed from amides, imides, halides, nitro compounds, etc.

CBSE 202556/7/14 marks
Question 40 · Class 12 Chemistry · Amines
Question as text
Amines are usually formed from amides, imides, halides, nitro compounds, etc. They exhibit hydrogen bonding which influences their physical properties. In alkyl amines, a combination of electron releasing, steric and H-bonding factors influence the stability of the substituted ammonium cations in protic polar solvents and thus affect the basic nature of amines. Alkyl amines are found to be stronger bases than ammonia. Amines being basic in nature, react with acids to form salts. Aryldiazonium salts, undergo replacement of the diazonium group with a variety of nucleophiles to produce aryl halides, cyanides, phenols and arenes. Answer the following questions: (a) How can you convert the following? 2 (i) Ethanoic acid to methanamine (ii) Propanenitrile to 1-aminopropane (b) Why is pKb value of aniline more than that of methylamine? 1 (c) (i) Arrange the following in increasing order of their basic strength in aqueous solution: 1 CH3 –NH 2, (CH3)2NH, (CH3)3N OR (c) (ii) Give the structures of A and B in the following reaction: 1 C6H5NO2 Fe/HCl −−−−→A HNO2 −−−−→273 K B
Answer
Answer as text
(a)(i) CH3COOH SOCl2 −−−−→CH3COCl NH3 − −− →CH3CONH2 Br2/NaOH −−−−−−→CH3NH2 (Hoffmann bromamide). (ii) CH3CH2CN LiAlH4 (or H2/Ni, Na/C2H5OH) −−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−−→CH3CH2CH2NH2. (b) In aniline the N lone pair is delocalised into the ring and less available; in methylamine the CH3 +I effect increases electron density on N. So aniline is the weaker base (higher pKb). (c)(i) (CH 3)3N < CH3NH2 < (CH3)2NH. OR (ii) A = C 6H5NH2 (aniline); B = C 6H5N2+Cl– (benzenediazonium chloride).
