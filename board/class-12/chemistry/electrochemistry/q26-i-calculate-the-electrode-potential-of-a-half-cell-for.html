Question as text
(a) (i) Calculate the electrode potential of a half-cell for zinc electrode dipping in 0 ·01 M ZnSO4 solution at 25◦C. Given: E◦ Zn2+/Zn =−0· 76 V [log 10 = 1] (ii) Write anode, cathode and overall reaction involved in dry cell. (iii) Equilibrium constant (Kc) is related to E◦ cell, but not to Ecell. Why? [2+2+1=5] OR (b) (i) The conductivity of 0·001 M solution of acetic acid is 3· 905× 10−5 S cm−1. Calculate its molar conductivity and degree of dissociation (α). Given: λ◦ CH3COO− = 40· 9 S cm2 mol−1, λ◦ H+ = 349· 6 S cm2 mol−1 (ii) Give reasons for the following: (I) Why does a mercury cell deliver a constant voltage for its entire life? (II) Why is it necessary to use salt bridge in a galvanic cell?
Answer
Answer as text
(a)(i) Zn2+ + 2 e–−−→Zn; E =E◦− 0.059 2 log 1 [Zn2+] =−0.76− 0.0295× 2 =−0.819 V (≈− 0.82 V). (ii) Anode: Zn −−→Zn2+ + 2 e– ; cathode: MnO 2 + NH4+ + e–−−→MnO(OH) + NH3; overall: Zn + 2 MnO2 + 2 NH4+−−→Zn2+ + 2 MnO(OH) + 2 NH3. (iii) At equilibrium Ecell = 0; ∆rG◦ =−nFE◦ cell =−RT lnKc, so E◦ cell = 0.059 n logKc. Ecell depends on the actual (non-equilibrium) concentrations and becomes zero at equilibrium. (b)(i) Λm = κ× 1000 M = 3.905× 10−5× 1000 0.001 = 39.05 S cm2 mol−1; Λ◦ m = 40.9 + 349.6 = 390.5; α = 39.05/390.5 = 0.1. (ii)(I) Overall reaction Zn(Hg) + HgO−−→ZnO(s) + Hg(l) involves no ion whose concentration changes during its life, so the voltage stays constant (≈ 1.35 V). (II) The salt bridge completes the inner circuit and maintains electrical neutrality of the two half-cell solutions (also minimises liquid junction potential).
