CBSE 202656/2/15 marks
Question 24 · Class 12 Chemistry · Electrochemistry
Question as text
(A) (a) The conductivity of 0.1 mol L−1 solution of NaCl is 1 .06× 10−2 S cm−1. Calculate its molar conductivity and degree of dissociation. λ◦ Na+ = 50.1 S cm2 mol−1, λ◦ Cl− = 76.5 S cm2 mol−1 (b) (i) Following cell reaction occurs in a galvanic cell: 2 Ag+(aq) + Zn(s)−−→2 Ag(s) + Zn2+(aq) E◦ (cell) = +1.56 V Predict the direction of flow of current. (ii) Differentiate between a primary battery and a secondary battery. OR (B) (a) Resistance of a conductivity cell filled with 0.1 M KCl solution is 100 Ω. If the resistance of the same cell when filled with 0.01 mol L −1 KCl solution is 300 Ω, calculate the conductivity and molar conductivity of 0.01 mol L−1 KCl solution. The conductivity of 0.1 M KCl solution is 1 .29× 10−2 S cm−1. (b) (i) Write any two advantages of H 2–O2 fuel cell. (ii) Why does the cell potential of mercury cell remain constant throughout the life?
Answer
Answer as text
(A)(a) Λm = κ× 1000 C = 1.06× 10−2× 1000 0.1 = 106 S cm2 mol−1; Λ◦ m = 50.1 + 76.5 = 126.6; α = 106/126.6≈ 0.837. (b)(i) Zn is anode, Ag cathode: electrons flow Zn → Ag in the external circuit, so current flows from Ag to Zn (cathode to anode). (ii) Primary battery: reaction occurs only once, cannot be recharged (dry cell, mercury cell); secondary battery: can be recharged by passing current in the opposite direction and reused (lead storage, Ni–Cd). (B)(a) Cell constant G∗ =κR = 1.29× 10−2× 100 = 1.29 cm−1; κ(0.01 M) = 1.29/300 = 4.3× 10−3 S cm−1; Λm = 4.3× 10−3× 1000 0.01 = 430 S cm2 mol−1. (b)(i) Any two: high efficiency (∼70%), pollution-free (only water formed), continuous energy supply as long as reactants are fed; water produced is usable (spacecraft). (ii) Overall reaction Zn(Hg) + HgO(s)−−→ZnO(s) + Hg(l) involves no ion whose concentration changes during use, so the cell potential stays≈ 1.35 V.
