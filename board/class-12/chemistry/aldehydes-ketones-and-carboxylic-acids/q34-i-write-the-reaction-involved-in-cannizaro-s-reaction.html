CBSE 20235 marks
Question 34 · Class 12 Chemistry · Aldehydes, Ketones and Carboxylic Acids
Question as text
(a) (i) Write the reaction involved in Cannizaro’s reaction. (ii) Why are the boiling point of aldehydes and ketones lower than that of corresponding carboxylic acids? (iii) An organic compound ‘A’ with molecular formula C5H8O2 is reduced to n-pentane with hydrazone followed by heating with NaOH and Glycol. ‘A’ forms a dioxime with hydroxylamine and gives a positive Iodoform and Tollen’s test. Identify ‘A’ and give its reaction for Iodoform and Tollen’s test. OR (b) (i) Give a chemical test to distinguish between ethanol acid and ethanoic acid. (ii) Why is the α-hydrogens of aldehydes and ketones are acidic in nature? (iii) An organic compound ‘A’ with molecular formula C 4H8O2 undergoes acid hydrolysis to form two compounds ‘B’ and ‘C’. Oxidation of ‘C’ with acidified potassium permanganate also produces ‘B’. Sodium salt of ‘B’ on heating with soda lime gives methane. (1) Identify ‘A’, ‘B’ and ‘C’. (2) Out of ‘B’ and ‘C’, which will have higher boiling point? Give reason.
Answer
Answer as text
(a) (i) 2 HCHO + conc· KOH−−→CH3OH + HCOOK (aldehydes without α-H). (ii) Carboxylic acids are associated by extensive intermolecular H-bonding (dimers); aldehydes/ketones have only dipole–dipole forces. (iii) A = CH 3COCH2CH2CHO (4-oxopentanal): Wolff–Kishner gives n-pentane, two C=O give dioxime, CH 3CO– gives iodoform, –CHO gives Tollens’ test. Iodoform: CH3COCH2CH2CHO NaOI−−−→CHI3 + NaOOCCH2CH2CHO; Tollens’: CH3COCH2CH2CHO + 2 [Ag(NH3)2]+ + 3 OH–−−→CH3COCH2CH2COO– + 2 Ag↓ + 4 NH3 + 2 H2O. (b) (i) NaHCO 3: ethanoic acid gives brisk effervescence of CO 2; ethanol (or ethanal, the likely intended compound) does not; ethanal also gives a silver mirror with Tollens’ reagent. (ii) The C=O group is electron-withdrawing and the conjugate base (enolate) is resonance-stabilised. (iii) A = CH 3COOC2H5 (ethyl ethanoate), B = CH3COOH, C = C 2H5OH; B has the higher b.p. (more extensive H-bonding, forms dimers).
