Question 25 · Class 12 Chemistry · Coordination Compounds
Question as text
(a) Answer the following questions about the complexes [NiCl 4]2– and [Ni(CN)4]2– : (i) Write the hybridization involved in each case. (ii) Which of them is the inner orbital complex and which one is the outer orbital complex? (iii) Compare their magnetic behaviour. [Atomic number: Ni = 28] [3 ×1=3] OR (b) (i) Name two coordination compounds which are important in biological systems. (ii) What is meant by chelate effect? Give an example. (iii) Why are low spin tetrahedral complexes rarely formed? [3 ×1=3]
Answer
Answer as text
(a)(i) [NiCl4]2– : sp3 (Cl– weak field, no pairing); [Ni(CN) 4]2– : dsp2 (CN– strong field, pairing of 3d electrons). (ii) [Ni(CN) 4]2– is the inner orbital complex (uses inner 3d); [NiCl4]2– is outer orbital (only 4s, 4p used). (iii) [NiCl 4]2– paramagnetic (2 unpaired electrons, µ≈ 2.83 BM); [Ni(CN)4]2– diamagnetic. (b)(i) Chlorophyll (Mg), haemoglobin (Fe), vitamin B 12 (cyanocobalamin, Co) – any two. (ii) Chelate effect: complexes of polydentate (chelating) ligands are more stable than those of comparable unidentate ligands, e.g. [Ni(en) 3]2+ is more stable than [Ni(NH3)6]2+. (iii) ∆t = 4 9∆o is small and almost always less than the pairing energy, so electrons do not pair: high-spin.
