Answer the following: (a) Low spin tetrahedral complexes are not known.

CBSE 202556/1/15 marks
Question 30 · Class 12 Chemistry · Coordination Compounds
Question as text
(A) Answer the following: (a) Low spin tetrahedral complexes are not known. (b) Co2+ is easily oxidised to Co 3+ in the presence of a strong ligand [At. No. of Co = 27] (c) What type of isomerism is shown by the complex [Co(NH 3)6][Cr(CN)6]? (d) Why a solution of [Ni(H 2O)6]2+ is green while a solutions of [Ni(CN) 4]2– is colourless. (At. No. of Ni = 28) (e) Write the IUPAC name of the following complex: [Co(NH 3)5(CO3)]Cl OR (B) (a) What is meant by ‘Chelate effect’? Give an example. (b) Write the hybridisation and magnetic behaviour of [Fe(CN) 6]4– . (Atomic number: Fe = 26) (c) If PtCl 2· 2 NH3 does not react with AgNO 3, what will be its formula? [2+2+1=5]
Answer
Answer as text
(A)(a) ∆t = 4 9∆o is small, always less than the pairing energy, so electrons never pair: only high spin. (b) Co 2+ (3d7): strong ligand forces pairing for d2sp3, pushing one electron to a higher (4d/ eg) level from where it is easily lost; Co 3+ (t6 2g) has large CFSE. (c) Coordination isomerism. (d) Ni 2+ (3d8) with weak-field H 2O: 2 unpaired electrons, d–d transition absorbs red light, transmits green; CN – (strong field) gives square planar dsp2, all electrons paired, large splitting, no absorption in the visible (as the question states; the real ion is pale yellow). (e) Pentaamminecarbonatocobalt(III) chloride. (B)(a) Chelating (polydentate) ligands form more stable complexes than similar monodentate ligands, e.g. [Ni(en) 3]2+ is more stable than [Ni(NH3)6]2+ (also EDTA complexes). (b) Fe 2+ 3d6, CN – strong field pairs electrons: d2sp3, inner orbital octahedral, diamagnetic. (c) [Pt(NH3)2Cl2] (no ionisable chloride).
